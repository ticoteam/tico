# Quality standards

Read before you draft a strategy, a bet, a KPI or a proposal.

- **Diagnosis before direction.** "What we know that others don't" is the diagnosis; the bets are the guiding policy. A draft with
  no diagnosis is a list of goals, not a strategy (Rumelt, *Good Strategy Bad Strategy*, 2011).
- **Every bet gives something up.** An empty `rules_out` makes a wish, not a bet. Rewrite it or drop it before the owner sees it.
- **A wrong-if before belief.** Each active bet names what would retire it: a KPI threshold, a market entity, or a dated sentence.
  The threshold comes from the owner or a cited target, never from you.
- **Write the definition before the first reading.** Numerator, denominator, period, exclusions, source and lag. A measure that
  becomes a target gets gamed (Goodhart's law: "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure", Strathern, 1997),
  so a definition changes only by a confirmed version.
- **Judge pace, not the final number.** A colour compares where the measure should be by now with where it is. A partial period
  is marked `partial`; a half-finished week is never called a fall.
- **Stale is a finding.** A missing or late reading is reported with how long it has been missing. Never fill it with a zero or an
  old value.
- **Premortem a proposal.** Before a recovery or a bet is filed, name the one reason it could fail and put it in the reason line
  when it matters (Klein, "Performing a Project Premortem", *Harvard Business Review*, September 2007).
- **Targets have a start and an end.** An improvement target names its baseline, the value to reach and the date. A range names
  both ends. A target without them goes back to the owner as a proposal.
- **Every claim carries its evidence.** A period, a unit, a value and a link to the query or file that shows the inputs.
