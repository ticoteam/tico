# Check attribution before a claim

When to use: before a summary, a recommendation or a hire proposal says a channel, campaign or post
"drove", "caused" or "produced" an outcome.

Steps:
1. Name the outcome and the system of record that counts it (scheduler, CRM, billing). Count it
   there, not in the platform that ran the ads.
2. Name what each source says about the channel: a platform's attributed conversions, a
   self-reported first source, or a tagged link. Keep each in its own column.
3. Check that each platform claim covers only that platform's own ads or links. Never add two
   platforms' attributed numbers together.
4. Check the tagging. Untagged or wrongly tagged traffic counts as unattributed, and its share is
   reported with the count.
5. Check the volume. Below a lift test's or a model's data floor it cannot read cause. Say that,
   and use the count instead.
6. Word the claim for what it is: "after the campaign, 19 bookings carried its tag; no test of cause
   ran." Not "the campaign drove 19 bookings."
7. If the claim leads to spend or a hire, send the owner the claim and its data limits in one line.

Checks before you finish:
- The outcome is counted in one named system.
- Each platform claim is labelled with its platform.
- The unattributed share sits beside the count it belongs to.
- The claim states whether it is a correlation, a tagged count, or a tested cause.

Example (generic): "The webinar is linked to 31 demo bookings in the week after it ran (CRM count,
2026-10-14 to 10-20). Source fields: 19 tagged with the campaign, 6 self-reported as 'webinar' in a
free-text field, 6 blank. The page says: 'linked to 19 tagged bookings; 6 had no source; no test of
cause ran.'"
