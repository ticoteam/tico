# Marketing practice

Standing practice for running a marketing team's week: what the weekly page may say, how to
measure, how to plan, and when to propose a hire. It is general. Check the team's own numbers
against their source before using any of it.

## The weekly page

- Answer first: one sentence on how the week went, then the workstreams that moved.
- Lead with movement against last week's page. Leading signals (work shipped, reviews done, sends
  on time, meetings booked) show the coming weeks. Lagging results (closed revenue) show what
  already happened, and they belong to the team that owns the close.
- Grade a line only on what was read. A workstream with no report is "no report", never green.
- Keep the page short enough to forward unedited: one line per workstream, one line per blocker,
  six weeks of calendar.

## Planning and the calendar

- One owner per workstream, named by bot slug or person, with the place its status shows.
- Plan six weeks ahead. Mark collisions (two sends or two launches on one day), launches inside
  two weeks with no brief, and gaps.
- At most three priorities for next week, each with its workstream, reason and owner. Say what
  was dropped and why. A priority list that never drops anything is a wish list.
- Re-plan on the calendar's own rhythm, not on every single report.

## Measurement and attribution

- Count funnel stages in one system of record: the scheduler or CRM for bookings, held meetings
  and outcomes. Use a platform's own report only for what that platform sees.
- Keep three questions apart: did the outcome happen (system of record), what did the person say
  first heard about us (a self-reported field stored separately), and did the spend cause it
  (incrementality, which needs enough volume to read).
- A platform's attributed conversions are its own claim. Do not add two platforms' numbers
  together, and do not use one platform to check another.
- The share of results with no source is a finding. Report it next to the count it belongs to.
- Lift tests and media-mix models have data floors. Below them they cannot read. Say so and use the
  system-of-record count instead.
- Platform attribution defaults and eligibility rules change. Google's help pages recommend at
  least 200 conversions and 2,000 ad interactions in 30 days for data-driven attribution, and a
  separate page describes stricter eligibility for some conversion types. Check the account's own
  attribution settings before quoting either.
- UTM discipline: one shared naming document with a named reviewer; lowercase values; source and
  medium on every tagged link, campaign at minimum; dates as YYYY-MM-DD so they sort; tag external
  links only; review the document monthly and retire old campaign names.

## Experiments at low volume

- Set the smallest effect worth detecting and the sample it needs before the test starts. A test
  that cannot reach its sample is not run, and the page says so.
- Check the split is what was intended (sample ratio) before reading any result.
- A flat result is a result. Record its size and sample so nobody reruns it by accident.

## AI-assistant referrals

- GA4 added an AI Assistant channel to its default channel group in May 2026, as reported by trade
  press. The channel list was not checked against Google's own page. Verify the property's rules
  before quoting a number.
- Visits with no referrer are not captured by referrer rules and show as direct. A typed visit
  after an assistant answer is direct too.
- In custom channel groups, rule order matters. A generic referral rule that fires first hides the
  rules below it.
- Google's search features (AI Overviews, AI Mode) are reported as organic search, not as the
  assistant channel. Verify this on the property before relying on it.

## Hiring signals

- Propose a hire when the same kind of recurring work arrives three times in a month, or when a
  workstream sits at "no owner" for two summaries running.
- The proposal names the template, the work with its evidence (task ids, dates), its first routine,
  who it reports to, and what a weekly review would cost a person.
- One proposal at a time. A set-up task is created only after the owner says yes.
