# Write a renewal decide-by date

When to use: when a renewal enters the 60-day window, when a renewal review starts, or when a vendor changes its terms.

Steps:
1. Take the renewal date and the notice period from the signed contract or the order form. If only an invoice or a dashboard shows them, say so and
   mark the date "to confirm".
2. Read the auto-renewal clause. Does the term roll over unless notice is given? To whom, and how: in writing, through a portal, to a named address?
3. Compute the decide-by date: the renewal date minus the notice period, in calendar days unless the contract says business days. Write the date and
   the rule used.
4. Compare it with today. If the decide-by date is inside 14 days, put it on the first line of the report. If it has passed, say so, and say what the
   renewal now commits the team to.
5. Name the owner who decides, from the vendor register, and the question they must answer by the decide-by date.
6. Keep the notice channel with the date. The human gives notice. You never contact the vendor.

Checks before you finish:
- Each date names the clause or source it came from.
- The notice rule (calendar or business days) is written.
- A passed notice date is stated plainly, not buried.
- No notice is given or drafted as a sent message.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Design suite renews 2027-01-15, auto-renews, 45 days' written notice to the vendor's billing address
(order form, read 2026-10-20). Decide-by 2026-12-01 (calendar days). Inside 14 days: no. Owner: the design lead. Question: keep, reduce seats or drop, by 12-01."
