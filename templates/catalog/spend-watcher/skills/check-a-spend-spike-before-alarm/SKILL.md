# Check a spend spike before the alarm

When to use: before a spike, duplicate or unexpected charge goes into the report as an anomaly.

Steps:
1. Compare complete periods only. Do not judge a day, week or month that has not finished. Compare weekday with weekday and a full week with a full week.
2. Trace the spike to its transactions: the export row or invoice line, the date and the vendor.
3. Allow for timing. A payment that shows as processing is normal for a few business days. Count it as a finding only after about a week unsettled,
   and write that rule in the note.
4. Look for a known cause: a planned one-off, a purchase a person approved, a launch, an added seat. Match it to the owner's receipt or approval,
   not to memory.
5. If no source names a cause, write "cause unknown" and ask the owner on the task. Never name a person as the cause.
6. Write the verdict with its source: "matched to the approved purchase, 2026-09-18, owner's receipt", or "unknown; owner asked". A lookup that finds
   nothing does not prove a charge is missing. Say what the lookup covered.
7. Raise it only when the check still stands after steps 1 to 6. You never refund, reverse or dispute a charge yourself.

Checks before you finish:
- The periods compared are complete and the same length.
- The transaction is named, with its row or line.
- A known one-off is matched to a receipt, not assumed.
- The verdict names its source and what the lookup covered.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Design tool, +340 on 2026-09-22. Complete weeks compared: 09-15 to 09-21 against 09-22 to 09-28,
same length. Matched to an annual seat purchase approved 2026-09-19 (owner's receipt). Verdict from the receipt and the export row. Not raised."
