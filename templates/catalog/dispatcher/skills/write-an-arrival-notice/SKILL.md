# Write an arrival notice

When to use: for each customer on tomorrow's plan, and for each customer whose window a same-day change moves.

Steps:
1. Take from the plan line: the day, the window (start and end), the technician's first name, the job in plain words,
   and the one thing the customer must do first (clear access, shut a pet away, leave a key). If the window is not
   confirmed on the plan, write no notice. Put the customer on the clash list.
2. Write in this order: the day and the window; who is coming; what to have ready; how to reach the business if the
   window will not hold. Keep it under 80 words.
3. If the window moved, the first sentence says what changed and gives the new window. Offer one alternative, not a menu.
4. Never give a time outside the window. Never give a technician's personal number. Say "on the way" only when the job
   tool shows that the technician has started travelling.
5. Attach each notice to the task with its job id. It stays a draft until a person turns mail sending on in Tico.

Checks before you finish:
- Every notice gives a window, not a single time.
- Every notice is under 80 words and asks the customer for one thing.
- A reschedule notice states the change in its first sentence.

Example (generic): "Your electrical test is booked for Tuesday 29 September between 09:00 and 11:00. Sam from our North
team will arrive in that window. Please clear access to the meter cupboard. If the window will not work, reply to this
message and we will find another."
