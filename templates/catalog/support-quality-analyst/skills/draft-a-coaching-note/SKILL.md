# Draft a coaching note

When to use: when the weekly review shows a miss repeated across at least two replies by the same person, and the owner
has asked for coaching drafts.

Steps:
1. Check that the pattern is repeated. One reply is an example; two or more of the same miss is a pattern. Name the
   criterion and the replies by ticket reference.
2. Find one strength in the same replies, and quote the sentence that shows it. Coaching starts from something that works.
3. Pick one change: the single habit that would fix the pattern most often.
4. Write the better wording for that change, from the reply itself or from the doc the reply should have used. Keep it
   to one or two sentences the person could say to a customer.
5. Write the note in two or three sentences: the strength with its quote, the change, and the better wording. Mark it
   "draft, for the owner".
6. Put the note on the task for the owner only. In the shared review, refer to the replies by ticket reference, and
   leave out the person's name and any ranking.
7. If the pattern is in the docs or the policy, not in the person's writing, do not draft coaching. Send the pattern as a
   task to the Librarian or the owner of the policy.

Checks before you finish:
- The pattern is named with at least two ticket references.
- The note has one strength with a quote, one change, and the better wording.
- The note is on the task for the owner, not in the shared report.
- No score is ranked against another person's, and no personal detail is quoted.

Example (invented, for illustration only): two replies this week, both about plan limits, open with a paragraph of
apology and give the answer in the last line. The strength is a clear step list in the second reply, quoted. The change
is to put the answer in the first sentence. The draft gives the better wording: "Your plan includes five seats. To add a
sixth, open Billing and choose Add seat." The note goes to the owner only.
