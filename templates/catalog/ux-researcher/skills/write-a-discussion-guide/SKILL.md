# Write a discussion guide

When to use: before any interview or call the team has agreed to run. Read it again when the decision
changes. The guide is a draft for the person who runs the session. The bot contacts no one. Usability sessions
use the tasks and think-aloud script in `skills/plan-a-usability-round/SKILL.md` instead of this guide's questions.

Steps:
1. Write the decision the study serves, in one sentence, and the question whose answer would change it.
2. Choose four to six topics. Each one traces to that question. A topic that answers nothing is cut.
3. Under each topic write one opening question about a recent, specific event: "Tell me about the last time you..."
   In a discovery interview, do not ask what they would do, what they like, or whether they want a feature.
4. Under each opening question add two follow-ups: "What did you do first?" and "What happened after that?"
5. Give each block a time, so the session ends on time. Thirty to forty-five minutes is the usual range.
6. Write a short never list for the person running it: no demo before the end, no question that names the answer,
   no finishing the participant's sentence.
7. Open with consent, recording and anonymity in two sentences. Close with "Is there anything I should have asked?"
8. Name the segment the guide is for and the decision it serves.

Checks before you finish:
- In a discovery interview or call, every question is about the past or a specific recent event, not a prediction. Usability sessions follow `skills/plan-a-usability-round/SKILL.md`.
- No question asks "would you" or "do you like".
- Each topic traces to the stated question.
- The consent line and the closing question are present.
- The guide names its decision and its segment.

Example (generic, invented):
- Decision: whether to build a sync or an export for shared calendars. Question: how do teams move a booking between
  tools today, and what breaks?
- Topic 1, 10 minutes. "Tell me about the last time a booking had to move to another tool." Then "What did you do
  first?" and "What happened after that?"
- Topic 2, 10 minutes. "Tell me about the last time something went wrong after a move." Then the same two follow-ups.
- Never: do not show the sync idea before topic 4.
