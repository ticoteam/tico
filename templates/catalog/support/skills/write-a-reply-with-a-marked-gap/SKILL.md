# Write a reply with a marked gap

When to use: a ticket needs a refund, credit, discount, fix, date or policy detail that the Librarian's answer does
not state in full, or the answer says "Not in the docs".

Steps:
1. Write one line that names what the customer asked, in their words, so the requester can check the match.
2. Answer every part the cited doc covers. Where a policy is involved, use the doc's own terms and keep its date on
   the task, not in the reply.
3. For each part the docs do not cover, put a visible gap in the draft, in square brackets, with the decider's role:
   `[GAP: refund amount, decided by billing]`.
4. Do not fill a gap with a guess, a range or "usually". Do not promise the outcome of a gap.
5. Say what happens next. Give a time only when a person has committed to it. Otherwise say the question is with the
   person who decides, and that the customer will hear back.
6. Route each gap as one task to its decider, with the ticket reference and the exact question. A docs gap also goes
   to the Librarian as a task.
7. Put the draft on the task with a header: "Draft. Gaps: <count>. Decider: <role>." List each gap under it.
8. Read the draft once as the customer on a phone. Keep it under about 150 words, with no internal ticket numbers,
   task names or jargon the customer cannot see.

Checks before you finish:
- Every gap is marked in the text and none is filled by a guess.
- Every money, date or fix promise is either in a cited doc or a marked gap.
- No internal name, task id or personal detail appears in the customer-facing text.
- The decider and the question are on the task in one line each.

Example (invented numbers, for illustration only): a customer asks for a refund on a second monthly charge. The billing
doc covers refunds within 14 days of a charge. The charge was 9 days ago. The draft reads: "Thanks for asking about the
second charge. Refunds are available within 14 days of a charge, and yours is inside that window. [GAP: whether the
refund returns to the original card or as credit, decided by billing]." A task goes to billing with the ticket
reference and the one question.
