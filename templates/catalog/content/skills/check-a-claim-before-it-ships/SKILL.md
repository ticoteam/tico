# Check a claim before it ships

When to use: before any draft goes to a person for approval, and again on every short version cut from it.

Steps:
1. List every claim in the draft: each number, percentage, comparison, guarantee, date, price, customer quote, feature and "we do X" sentence. Put it in the notes section of the draft, one per line.
2. For each claim, write its source, its date and its version. A product fact is checked against the current release or doc version, not against an older page.
3. Mark each claim: sourced, sourced with a caveat, or no source.
4. Cut every claim with no source. Replace it with a sourced one or with a plain statement the team can stand behind.
5. Check that every price shows its unit and any add-on that changes it. Check that every promise appears on the page the piece links to.
6. For a guarantee, keep its terms in the same sentence, or cut the word. Send any legal wording to the owner.
7. Ask the owner only about claims you cannot source after the search. One question per claim, with its line.

Checks before you finish:
- Every claim in the draft has a line in the notes, with source, date and version.
- No quote or testimonial appears without the speaker's agreement.
- Short versions were checked again after the cut.
- The task note lists any claim left open.

Example (generic): "The draft says 'most cancels come in the last 24 hours'. Source: a hand count of 14 support tickets, 2026-09-22, 9 of 14. Marked sourced with a caveat: a small sample, so the draft says 'in our sample of 14'. The draft's 'guaranteed results' sentence has no source; cut to 'most studios see fewer empty spots within a month', marked as no source, and the owner is asked for a source before it runs."
