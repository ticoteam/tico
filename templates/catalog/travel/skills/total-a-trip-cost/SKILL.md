# Total a trip's cost

When to use: for each option in a trip plan, and when a traveller asks why one option costs more than another.

Steps:
1. For each option, write the fare with the bags and seat fees the trip needs. Do not use a headline fare that leaves them out.
2. Count hotel nights from the check-in date to the check-out date. A night before a fixed start is a hotel night and counts. Multiply by the nightly rate and check the rate against the policy limit per night.
3. Add ground transport at the arrival and departure airports: transfer, parking or a rideshare estimate. Name the basis of each estimate.
4. Note the fare's rules: refundable or not, the change fee, and what a change costs. If the fare shows no rule, write "none stated".
5. Sum the lines. Write the total with each line visible, so anyone can check it.
6. Mark the policy result for each total: within, or over by how much, with the reason.
7. Compare options on the total first, then on what the fare rules leave open. A cheaper fare that cannot be changed is named as a cost risk, not hidden.
8. Record the price and the time it was seen for each option. A fare is a quote for that moment only.

Checks before you finish:
- Every option has the fare, hotel, ground and total lines, and the total equals their sum.
- Hotel nights match the dates on the trip.
- Each policy result names the limit it was checked against.
- Fare rules are stated for every option.
- Each price carries the time it was seen.

Example (generic): "Option A: fare 312 (non-refundable), 3 nights at 139 = 417, ground 80, total 809, within the 240 nightly limit. Option B: fare 428 (changeable), 3 nights at 224 = 672, ground 60, total 1,160. The 351 difference buys a changeable fare. Prices seen 2026-09-28 10:40."
