# PR method

The standing practice behind the pitch, the release, the reporter list and the holding statement. Survey figures
come from vendors who sell PR tools; each one is dated and read as a direction, not a law.

## Pitching

- Relevance first. In Muck Rack's 2026 State of Journalism survey, reporters ranked a clear fit with their beat
  first (70%), then access to sources for interviews (58%), original data (40%) and images (37%) (cited through a
  2026 summary; check the original).
- One reporter, one reason. The reason is a piece the reporter wrote, dated, and what is new in the pitch.
- Email is still the channel reporters prefer. Muck Rack's 2026 survey found 62% want a one-to-one email (cited
  through a 2026 summary). Social and phone are for an existing relationship.
- One follow-up at most, two business days later, and it adds something new. Cision's 2025 survey found 62% of
  journalists consider one follow-up appropriate; 24% of US reporters never want one.
- Response rates are low. An analysis of 400,000 pitches by Propel, reported by Everything PR in 2026, found reporters
  replied to about 3.4%. Expect most pitches to get nothing, and send fewer.
- Cision's 2025 survey found 85% of reporters say a plain introduction email with no pitch is the best way to build a
  relationship. Use one before you need one.

## Releases

- Inverted pyramid: the news in the first sentence, the facts in order of importance, one quote per person, the
  boilerplate, a contact line. Under 500 words.
- A release supports a pitch; it does not replace one. Link it in the pitch, with the data and usable images.
- Quotes are recorded words or marked as drafts for the speaker to approve. Nothing is invented.

## Timing

- Press work starts four weeks before a tier 1 launch and two weeks before smaller news, so a reporter has time
  to plan (the team's default; change it in `knowledge/rules.md` if the owner agrees).
- Embargoes are offered only when the owner has agreed them on the task. An embargo breached by the team ends the
  relationship with that outlet.

## Holding statements

- One per known risk, written in advance and kept in `knowledge/rules.md`: what happened, what is known, what is
  not, what the team is doing, and who speaks.
- A holding statement names no person, no number and no cause that has not been confirmed. It is approved by the
  owner and by legal when the risk touches one.

## Corrections

- A factual error in an article is the first line of the review, with the correction the owner could ask for and
  the outlet's own correction route. The correction request is drafted, not sent.
