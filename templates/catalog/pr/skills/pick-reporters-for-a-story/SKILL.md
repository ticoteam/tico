# Pick reporters for a story

When to use: when an announcement or a story needs a list of reporters to pitch, and when a review proposes
someone for `knowledge/media-list.md`.

Steps:
1. Write the story's angle in one sentence and the audience it serves (buyers, sector press, a local outlet).
2. Search the media list for reporters whose beat matches. A reporter is listed only with two dated articles on
   the beat from the last six months, found on the public web.
3. Score each candidate on three things: a piece on the same topic in the last 90 days; a beat that reaches the
   audience; a past mention of the team or its sector. Score a reporter with no dated article as zero.
4. Rank them. Choose five to ten. Pitch the top ones first, and keep the rest as a second wave for a week later.
5. Drop anyone who has asked not to be pitched, who covers a competitor's launch this week, or whose outlet
   has a published rule against the type of story.
6. For each one chosen, write one line: the reporter, the outlet, the dated piece that gives the reason. Put the
   list on the announcement's task.
7. Note the date the list was checked. Lists older than 90 days are checked again before use.

Checks before you finish:
- Every chosen reporter has two dated articles in the media list.
- No one is on the list twice, under two outlets.
- The list has no personal contact details beyond a public work address.
- The second wave is named, with its start date.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Waitlist launch, angle: studios lose one in eight bookings to no-shows.
Chosen: Ruth Alvarez, Boutique Fitness Today (pieces 2026-09-10 and 2026-09-23 on studio margins, fits). Second wave,
2026-11-03: two reporters on fitness software. Not chosen: a general business writer whose last piece on the beat was
in 2025."
