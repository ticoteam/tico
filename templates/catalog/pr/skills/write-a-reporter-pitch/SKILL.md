# Write a reporter pitch

When to use: for each first pitch in an announcement pack, and when a reporter asks for something new.

Steps:
1. Read the reporter's two recent pieces in `knowledge/media-list.md`, with their dates. Note one sentence from
   the piece that the news connects to. If you cannot find a connection, do not pitch.
2. Write the subject line: under eight words, the news or the question, no exclamation mark, no "exclusive" unless
   the owner has agreed one.
3. Open with the reason for this reporter: their piece, its date, and why this news adds to it.
4. Give the news in one line, with its date and the single fact that makes it news.
5. Offer what the reporter can use: an interview with a named spokesperson, a figure the team can show with its own
   data (state its period), or a customer who has agreed to speak.
6. Close with one ask and a time. No attachments on a first pitch; link the release if one exists.
7. Count the words. The body is under 150 words. Cut the adjectives first.
8. Check it against `knowledge/rules.md`: nothing unapproved, no promise of an exclusive or an embargo unless agreed,
   and every number has a source.

Checks before you finish:
- The first sentence refers to the reporter's own work, with a date.
- The news is in one line and is dated.
- The offer is something the reporter can actually use.
- No claim is in the pitch that is not in a source or the release.
- The draft is listed on the task as a draft for the spokesperson or owner. Nothing is sent by this skill.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Subject: Studio no-show data, 1 in 8 bookings. Hi Ruth, your 23 Sept piece on
studio margins showed how much a missed booking costs a small studio. We have a number that fits: across 41 studios
in our beta, 1 in 8 bookings was a no-show in August. The data is ours to share. Our founder can talk about it on
Thursday or Friday. Could I send the one-page summary?"
