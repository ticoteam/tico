# Write a counsel brief

When to use: before any matter goes to a lawyer or an outside firm, whether the lawyer is named in `knowledge/escalation.md`
or the owner is still choosing one.

Steps:
1. Write the question first, as one sentence a lawyer can answer. If it holds two questions, split it into two briefs.
2. State the facts in date order. Each fact has its date and its source (a document, a message or a task id). Write what
   was said or done. Do not characterise it.
3. List the documents: name, date, where it is kept. Mark each one you were not given.
4. Name the parties and people by role and organisation. Leave out personal details the question does not need.
5. State the deadline and its source. A date you cannot source is "not confirmed", with what you checked.
6. Give the exposure in plain words: money, a person, data, a regulator, the business at risk. Use only the figures the record holds.
7. List what is unknown and what would change the answer.
8. Add what the team has already done or promised, and by whom.
9. Close with the request: what the owner wants (advice, a view on options, a letter reviewed, a filing check), and the
   decision the owner must make, with the date it is needed by.

Checks before you finish:
- The question is one sentence and can be answered.
- Every fact has a date and a source.
- Every deadline is sourced, or marked "not confirmed".
- The brief holds no personal data beyond what the question needs.
- It says it is a prepared question for counsel, not a conclusion.
- It ends with "Summary for a human, not legal advice. Have counsel review anything that matters."

Example (invented, for illustration): "Question: Does the 60-day notice in the signed supply agreement (s.9.2) need a written reply
before the 2027-03-31 renewal? Facts: 2026-10-14, the supplier emailed a price increase (task 812). Documents: the signed
agreement (PDF, not yet reviewed by counsel); the supplier email. Deadline: 2027-01-29 (the 60th day, 2027-01-30, is a Saturday), from s.9.2 (see the deadline check).
Unknown: whether the supplier's email counts as notice under s.14. Team action so far: none. Decision needed by 2027-01-15:
whether to reply in writing."
