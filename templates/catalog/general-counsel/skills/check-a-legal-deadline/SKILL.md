# Check a legal deadline

When to use: before you put any date, window or "due by" into a summary, a task, a calendar line or a brief.

Steps:
1. Find the source: the signed document, the court or agency notice, or the message that set the date. Record its id or
   name and the date you read it.
2. Find the rule that makes the date run: the clause, statute, order or notice. Quote its words in short.
3. Note the trigger (receipt, service, signature, end of term, or a fixed calendar date) and whether the count is in
   calendar or business days.
4. Do the arithmetic and write it out, for example "2027-03-31 minus 60 calendar days = 2027-01-30". If a weekend or a
   holiday falls on the date, say which rule you used.
5. Check the date against a second source if one exists (the calendar, the CRM, an agency page). If the two differ, record
   both with their sources. Do not pick one.
6. Mark the status: "confirmed" (source and rule read), "from a summary" (rule not read), or "not confirmed" (no source).
   Only confirmed dates go on the calendar as firm.
7. Set the action date: the day the team must act by, with the days left, not only the due date.

Checks before you finish:
- Every date has a source, a rule and its arithmetic.
- A date taken from a summary or from memory is labelled as such.
- Two conflicting dates are both shown, with their sources.
- The action date is before the deadline, and the days left are shown.
- Nothing is dropped because it could not be confirmed.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Renewal 2027-03-31 per the signed supply agreement, s.2.1. Notice: 60 calendar days before
the renewal date (s.9.2), so 2027-03-31 minus 60 days = 2027-01-30. Read 2026-11-01. Status: confirmed. The CRM shows 45 days;
both are shown to the owner, and neither is chosen here."
