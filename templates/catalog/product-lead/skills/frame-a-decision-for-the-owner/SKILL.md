# Frame a decision for the owner

When to use: before any decision goes to the owner in the weekly summary or on a task. Use it for build, park, hire, and routing choices alike.

Steps:
1. Write the question as a choice: "Build the waitlist this quarter, or research it first?" not "What about waitlists?"
2. List two or three options, each with its cost in one unit (weeks, accounts, risk). Include "do nothing" when it is a real option.
3. Attach the evidence for each option with its source and date. Mark every guess as a guess.
4. Say whether the choice is one-way (costly to undo) or two-way (cheap to reverse). A one-way choice gets more evidence before it is made; a two-way choice can be made quickly.
5. Give one recommendation and the reason for it. The owner may choose otherwise.
6. State the default: what happens if nobody decides by a named date, and who it affects.
7. Write what would reopen the decision: a number, an event or a date. When the owner decides, log it in `knowledge/decision-log.md` with the date, who decided, the evidence and the reopen trigger.

Checks before you finish:
- The question is one choice, not a topic.
- Every option has a cost in the same unit.
- Every piece of evidence has a source and date, and guesses are marked.
- Reversibility is stated.
- The default has a date and an effect.
- There is a reopen trigger.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Class waitlists: build this quarter, or research first? Build now: about six weeks of engineering (the owner's estimate), reach about 1,150 studios, 41 accounts asked for it. Research first: two weeks with `product-researcher`, which would move confidence from 50% to 80%. Reversible? No: the waitlist shapes the booking screens, so the choice is one-way and gets the research first. Recommendation: research first. Default if undecided by 2026-10-02: research starts, because it is cheap. Reopen if: the studios interviewed do not describe the problem, or a competitor ships it."
