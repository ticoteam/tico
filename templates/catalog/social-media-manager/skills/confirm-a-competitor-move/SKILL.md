# Confirm a competitor move

When to use: a page diff, sitemap date, headline, job post, forum thread or complaint suggests a competitor changed something.

Steps:
1. Name the signal and its source: the page, the date and the link.
2. Load the company's own page again, at least twice, and compare. A change seen once is not yet a change.
3. Rule out false diffs: an A/B test that flips between loads, prices shown by location or currency, a site-wide rewrite, a staged
   rollout, a seasonal banner, or a date bump with no content change.
4. Name the change in one line: a price (with its base and add-ons), a package, an ownership or product change, a shutdown, a
   lawsuit, a funding round, or a launch into a new market.
5. For a complaint, require a pattern: several independent sources on the same issue in the same window. One complaint is a
   watch note.
6. Classify it: a same-day move, a weekly watch note, or no move. Only a move goes into the digest's real-moves section, with its
   link and one line on why it matters.

Checks before you finish:
- The change was seen on two loads, or in two independent sources.
- The one-line description names what changed.
- Complaints are counted as a pattern only when the sources are independent.

Example (generic): "A rival's pricing page shows a new starter fee on one load and the old fee on the next two loads. Recorded as a
watch note, since the first load may be a test. A separate change: the rival's help page says the base plan now includes two extra
seats, seen on two loads and in its changelog. Classified as a move, one line: 'base plan now includes two extra seats', with the link."
