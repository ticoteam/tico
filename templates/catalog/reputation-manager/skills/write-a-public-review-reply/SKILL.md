# Write a public review reply

When to use: for each review that gets a reply, on any surface where the team may answer publicly.

Steps:
1. Read the whole review and note the one point that matters most: the thing that happened, the price, the wait, the staff member's behaviour (never named in the reply).
2. Write the opening in one line that names that point, in the reviewer's own terms where the review gives them.
3. Say what the team did or will do, in one sentence. If the team cannot say yet, say when it will know and who will
   contact the reviewer.
4. Give one route to resolve it: a support address or a contact form on the team's own site. Do not put an account
   number, an amount, a payment detail or a dispute detail in the reply.
5. Close without a promise you cannot keep: no refund, credit or feature in public, and no "we will fix it" without
   the owner's confirmation.
6. Check the length. Under 80 words for a routine review, under 120 for a serious one.
7. Check the platform's rule for replies on that surface (for example, whether a reply may name the reviewer). Check
   the text against `knowledge/review-rules.md`.
8. Put the draft on the batch with the review's id. A serious or legal review is not drafted here; it goes to the owner.

Checks before you finish:
- The reply answers the point the review made, not a generic one.
- No private detail, no argument, no blame on a named person.
- No promise of money, a feature or a date that the owner has not approved.
- Templated wording is not repeated across several replies in one batch: each names its own point.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Thank you for the note about the 40-minute wait on Tuesday. We have added a second
front-desk slot on Tuesdays from 2 November. If you would like to talk about your visit, write to support@ with the date
and we will reply the same week." The reply names no staff member and no payment.
