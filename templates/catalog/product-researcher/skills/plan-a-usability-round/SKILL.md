# Plan a usability round

When to use: when the team needs to know whether people can finish a task in a product or a prototype. Read it before
you pick tasks or name groups. Recruiting is a draft until a person sends it.

Steps:
1. Name the decision the round serves and the product area it covers.
2. Write three to five tasks. Each is a goal in the person's words, such as "move a booked class to next week", not a
   click path such as "open the calendar menu".
3. Write success for each task before the first session: finished without help, within a time limit, and without a
   wrong turn that is not recovered. Put the numbers in the plan.
4. Group the people. Five per round for one group. Three or four per distinct user group when there are several, and
   say which group each person is in.
5. Plan rounds, not one large study: five people, a redesign, then five more. Three rounds is a common plan while the
   product is changing.
6. Write the think-aloud script: "Tell me what you expect to happen before you press anything." Ask what a person would
   do next, not what they think of the design.
7. Note for each session the task outcome, the first wrong turn, and the words the person used. Record only with consent.
8. Report counts per task, the wrong turns with quotes, and the limit: how many people, which groups, and what the
   round could not show.

Checks before you finish:
- Each task is a goal, not a click path.
- Success is written down before the first session.
- The sample names its groups and their sizes.
- The brief states the limit of five people per round.
- Nothing is recruited or sent unless a person sends it.

Example (generic, invented):
- Decision: whether the booking form needs a second step. Round 1: five people, one group (front-desk staff).
- Task 2: "Add a new student to next Tuesday's class." Success: finished in under 90 seconds, no wrong turn.
- Result: four of five finished. Two went to the settings menu first. Quote: "I thought the student lived in settings."
- Limit: five people, one group. The round cannot show what owners do.
