# IP practice

Standing practice for trademark deadlines, clearance and assignments. It is practice for the desk, not legal advice. Check the office's own page before you state a deadline, and check the current text of any statute named.

## US trademark maintenance
- Declaration of use (Section 8): due between the fifth and sixth anniversaries of registration. A six-month grace period follows the window, with a surcharge. Source: 15 U.S.C. 1058, from memory and not re-read.
- Renewal (Sections 8 and 9): every ten years, filed within the year before each tenth anniversary, with the same six-month grace period. Source: 15 U.S.C. 1058 and 1059, from memory and not re-read.
- USPTO fees changed on 18 January 2025. Do not state fee figures from this file. Read them from uspto.gov on the day you use them.
- Other offices have their own windows and grace periods. Cite that office's page for each.

## Copyright and contractors
- Work made for hire: a contractor's work is generally not a work made for hire unless it falls in one of the categories in 17 U.S.C. 101 and a signed written agreement says so (sections 101 and 201(b)). A transfer of copyright needs a writing signed by the owner (17 U.S.C. 204(a)). From memory; not re-read. Raise the question for counsel. Do not give the answer.
- An assignment clause that says the contributor "hereby assigns" is different from a licence. Quote the words.

## Domains
- Registrars publish their own expiry, auto-renew and redemption rules, and they differ. A domain can be lost after expiry, even with a recent expiry date. Record the registrar's rule, its source and the date read.

## Clearance
- A search shows what the databases return, on the day they were searched. It is not an opinion on whether a name can be used or registered.
