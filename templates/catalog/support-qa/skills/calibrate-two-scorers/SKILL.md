# Calibrate two scorers

When to use: before the first weekly review, before each calibration session the owner schedules, and before you
change a scorecard rule or weight.

Steps:
1. Pick a shared set of three to five replies from the last review period, including one critical case and one with a
   refund or a reopen. Write the set's ticket references in `knowledge/calibration.md`.
2. Each scorer marks the set on their own, using `scorecard.md`, without seeing the other's marks. If one scorer does
   both passes, leave a gap of at least a day between them and do not look at the first pass.
3. Compare the marks criterion by criterion. Count the agreements and the disagreements for each criterion, not only
   the overall pass.
4. For each disagreement, write the two scores, the reason each scorer gave, and which one the reply supports. Write
   the rule that would have settled it in one sentence.
5. Propose a rule only when the disagreement shows the rule was unclear. Write it in `knowledge/calibration.md` with its date
   and the replies that prompted it. Add it to `scorecard.md` only when the task asks for a scorecard change and a granted
   Tool allows it; otherwise it stays a proposal for the owner. Do not change a weight on one disagreement.
6. Record the session in `knowledge/calibration.md`: the date, the set, the agreements, the disagreements, the rules
   proposed or added, and what the next session will check.
7. If the scorers disagree on a critical item, the owner decides before the review is published.

Checks before you finish:
- Every disagreement has both reasons and a settling rule, or says the owner must decide.
- Each rule proposed or added to the scorecard has its date and the reply that prompted it.
- The set and the date are in the calibration log.
- No individual's score is shared in a report: calibration is about the rules.

Example (invented, for illustration only): two scorers mark the same five replies. They agree on four of five on tone
and three of five on next step. The two disagreements on next step are the same case: a reply that says "we will update
you" with no date. The rule proposed for the scorecard is that a next step needs a named person or a date, and the reply
that prompted it is listed with its date. The owner or a granted scorecard change decides whether it goes in.
