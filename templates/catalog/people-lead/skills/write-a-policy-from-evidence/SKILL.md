# Write a policy from evidence

When to use: when a task asks for a new policy or a change to one, after the current pages have been read.

Steps:
1. List what the handbook says and how people work now, with a source for each. Where they differ, write both and say which
   one the owner must choose.
2. Settle the three to six decisions the policy makes. For each, give the current practice and one or two options. Keep this
   decisions list apart from the policy text.
3. For any point a law or regulation may set (leave, pay, working time, privacy, notice), write "check with counsel: <question>".
   Do not state the law from memory.
4. Draft the rules as short numbered statements that one person can apply the same way for everyone. Leave out any rule the
   team cannot enforce evenly.
5. Test the draft against three readers: a new hire, a manager applying it, and someone it could disadvantage (part-time,
   remote, on leave). Fix any rule that treats them differently without a reason.
6. Write the effective date and a review date (default: one year after the effective date). Record the change in
   `knowledge/policies.md` with its owner.

Checks before you finish:
- Every fact about current practice has a source. Legal points are marked for counsel.
- The decisions list and the policy text are separate documents within the task.
- The policy names an owner, an effective date and a review date.
- Nothing is published. The draft goes to the owner, and to the Librarian only when the owner asks.

Example (generic, invented): A remote-work policy. Decision: how many office days a week. Current practice: two days, per the
handbook page dated 2026-08-02. Option A: keep two days. Option B: one day, with a manager's exception. Counsel question: "Does
a change to a person's work location need written notice where we operate?"
