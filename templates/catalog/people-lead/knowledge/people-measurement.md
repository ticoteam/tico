# People measurement

How the weekly summary counts people work, and what those counts can and cannot show. Most rules here are practice, not law.
Where a source is a vendor help page, the Sources line says so.

## Evidence labels

Every figure in a people report carries one label.

- **Measured:** counted from a system, with its date (roster, calendar, tracker).
- **Inferred:** worked out from other figures. Say which ones.
- **Observed:** a manager or a bot said it. It is not a count.
- **Unknown:** there is no source. Unknown is not zero. A missing record is an unknown, not a "no".

## Small groups

Survey and reporting tools commonly hide results for a group below a floor, most often five people. The floor is a convention:
vendors set it, and works councils or unions sometimes agree it. Show a group below five merged into a neighbouring group or as
part of a total. Check the group next to it too, since a total can reveal a hidden one.

## Teams and roles, not people

A people summary reports teams and roles. Ratings, pay and performance belong to a named human's decision, and never appear as
a count.

## Trends

A trend needs two comparable periods on the same definition. One week is not a trend. If a definition changes, say so and do
not compare across the change.

## Counts are not hours

Counts of tasks or requests are counts. "12 requests" is not "12 hours of work". Do not turn a count into time.

## Silence is not evidence

No reply, no closed task and no bot report does not prove that something was done or was not. Name the gap.

## Limits

- In a small team any number can point to one person. Use ranges, or merge groups, before you publish.
- Each source system has its own rules on who may see what. Its rule applies first.
