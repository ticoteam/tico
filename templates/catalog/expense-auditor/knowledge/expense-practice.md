# Expense practice

Standing reference for the expense checks. This team's own policy lives in `policy-rules.md`; this file holds general
practice and the rules behind the defaults. Where the two disagree, the team's written policy wins. Each claim has its
source and the date it was read. Anything marked "verify" was not read from the primary text.

## Receipts

- The receipt threshold is a policy choice. The IRS rules for substantiating travel and entertainment (Publication 463)
  require documentary evidence for lodging of any amount and for other expenses of $75 or more. Several vendor summaries
  read in 2026 apply the $75 figure to every expense. Verify the scope against the publication before you cite it.
- A receipt shows the merchant, the date, the amount and the business purpose, or the purpose is noted on the claim. A card
  statement line alone is not a receipt for a meal.
- A missing receipt is a finding about evidence. Name the line and the rule. Do not treat it as proof the spend was personal.

## Accountable plans

- Reimbursements stay out of the employee's taxable pay when they follow an accountable plan (Treasury Regulation 1.62-2).
  The plan needs a business connection, substantiation, and the return of any excess advance.
- The regulation's safe harbor treats expenses as substantiated when they are submitted within 60 days of being paid or
  incurred. An advance is treated as reasonable when given no more than 30 days before the expense, and any excess is
  returned within 120 days. Verify these periods against the regulation text.
- A late claim is listed with its date. Whether it is taxable pay is the accountant's decision, not the audit's.

## Duplicates

- A duplicate candidate is the same spend recorded twice: the same receipt in two claims, or a card line and a claim for
  the same amount, merchant and date. Card posting can lag a purchase by a few days, so match on a window, not one date.
- Recurring charges (subscriptions, monthly fees) and split bills (two cards, two receipts, different totals) are not
  duplicates. Check before you flag.

## Limits and categories

- Meal, hotel and flight limits are the team's numbers. Write them in `policy-rules.md` with their policy section. Public
  per-diem tables are a reference only where the policy points to them.

## Sources

- Treasury Regulation 1.62-2 (accountable plans). Secondary summaries read 2026-11-01. Verify the safe-harbor periods against the text.
- IRS Publication 463, Travel, Gift, and Car Expenses (receipt threshold). Secondary summaries read 2026-11-01. The
  publication itself was not read for this file. Verify the threshold and its scope.
