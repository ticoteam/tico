# Write a win/loss interview guide

When to use: the team wants to talk to buyers after a decision and a person will hold the call. You write the guide. You do not hold the call or contact the buyer.

Steps:
1. Set the purpose in one sentence the buyer can read: learning how they decided, not winning them back.
2. Open with consent. Ask whether they agree to notes. Ask for a recording only with their agreement, given on the record. Say who will see the notes.
3. Plan for 20 to 30 minutes and five to seven open questions, in the order the buyer lived them: first need, options considered, what decided it, what nearly stopped it, what they would change.
4. Ask about wins and losses alike, and about the decision: who was involved and what was compared.
5. Do not ask leading questions ("was our price a problem?"). Ask what the buyer weighed, then follow their words.
6. Put no pitch and no rebuttal in the guide. If the buyer asks about the product, the interviewer answers only what is documented, or says they will follow up.
7. Write the stop rule: if the buyer asks to end the call, or asks not to be contacted again, the call ends and no further contact is made.
8. If the buyer does not reply, send one reminder and then stop.

Checks before you finish:
- The interviewer is not the seller who worked the deal.
- Consent and recording rules are at the top of the guide. Flag them for counsel if the team is unsure of the rule.
- No question is leading, and no answer is scripted for the buyer.
- The guide quotes no buyer identity beyond what the team already holds.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Purpose: learn how you decided between the options you looked at. Consent: notes only; a recording only if you agree on the record. Questions: 1) What made you start looking this year? 2) Which options did you compare? 3) What decided it? 4) What nearly stopped the decision? 5) What would have changed your answer?"
