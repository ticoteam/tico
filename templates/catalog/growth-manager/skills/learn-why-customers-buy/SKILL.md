# Learn why customers buy

When to use: an idea, a page or a positioning line rests on a guess about customers; the growth
interview reaches retention; the owner asks who to sell to next.

Steps:
1. Read what is already said: `tico brand summary`, `tico strategy show`, call transcripts and
   support tasks from the last 12 months. Older material
   describes a different product and buyer; weight it less.
2. Ask the people closest to customers, one question at a time, by chat
   (`skills/growth-interview/SKILL.md`, step 4): sales for why deals were won and lost, support or
   success for why customers leave, the founder for who the best customers are (biggest, fastest to
   buy, longest to stay). Ask for the customer's own words, not a summary: "What did they say,
   roughly?"
3. Keep asking why. The first answer is a feature ("they use the reports"). Ask why two or three
   more times until you reach the outcome they pay for ("the boss sees the saving at renewal").
4. Ask a question that could prove you wrong. If every question can only confirm what the team
   believes, rewrite one.
5. Sort each answer into: the job (what they are trying to get done), the trigger (what changed that
   made them look), the pain before, the alternative (including doing nothing or hiring someone), and
   their exact words.
6. Label each finding: **high** (3 or more independent people or sources, said unprompted), **medium**
   (2, or only when asked), **low** (one source). Never call a low finding true of all customers.
7. When the team does not know, propose asking customers. File a task to whoever owns customer
   research (`ux-researcher` or `customer-insights-analyst`, else a person): five to ten short
   conversations with the best customers, framed as a chat, not a study. End each with "Who else
   should we talk to?". For a survey, the one question that matters is "How would you feel if you
   could no longer use <product>?" (very / somewhat / not disappointed). 40% or more "very" means
   customers would be lost without it; read the "very" group's answers first.

Checks before you finish:
- Each finding has its label, its sources and at least one quote in the customer's words.
- The trigger and the alternative are written down, not only the pain.
- Findings go to `knowledge/customers.md`, dated; the ones that change wording go to the owner of
  positioning (`segment-marketing-owner`) on a task.

Example: "Why they leave (medium, 2 sources: support lead, founder): 'the person who set us up
left and nobody knew how to run the report.' Trigger: a hand-over. Proposed: an onboarding re-run
when an account's admin changes, to `success-owner`."
