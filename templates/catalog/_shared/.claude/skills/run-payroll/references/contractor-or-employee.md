# Contractor or employee

Do this before a new person starts. It takes 20 minutes and it is the cheapest protection the company has: a
contractor later found to be an employee can mean back payroll taxes, back overtime, penalties and state claims.

**This check is for arrangements that have not started.** If someone already working as a contractor looks like an
employee, do not "fix" it in the payroll system. That is a hard stop: the facts go to a lawyer or CPA, because how the
company corrects it changes what it owes.

## 1. There is more than one test

The same person can be a contractor under one law and an employee under another. Check each that applies, in the
state where the person physically does the work.

| Purpose | Who sets the test | Where to read it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Federal payroll taxes | IRS common-law test: behavioural control, financial control, type of relationship | IRS "Independent contractor (self-employed) or employee?" (updated 2026-05-19) |
| Federal minimum wage and overtime | US Department of Labor, an "economic reality" test | DOL Wage and Hour Division misclassification pages. The rule is in flux; see below |
| State wage and hour law | The state labour department; several states use a stricter three-part "ABC" test | The state's labour department page |
| State unemployment insurance | The state workforce agency | Its employer handbook |
| Workers' compensation | The state's workers' compensation agency | Its page; `manage-insurance` needs the answer |

Never state a test from memory. Read the page, and write the page and the date on the decision record.

Federal wage law, as read on 2026-11-18: the Department of Labor's 2024 rule (a six-factor economic reality test,
effective 2024-03-11) is still the published rule. The Department proposed to rescind it on 2026-02-26 (91 FR 9932),
comments closed 2026-04-28, and no final rule was found. Check the Department's rulemaking page before relying on
either version.

## 2. Collect the facts

Ask the teammate who wants the person, and read the draft contract. Write answers, not impressions.

**The work**
- What will they deliver, and is it a defined project or an open-ended role?
- Is this work the company's core business, done the way employees elsewhere do it?
- How long will it last? Is there an end date?

**Control**
- Who decides how, when and where the work is done?
- Will the company train them, set hours, require meetings or review the method (not only the result)?
- Can they send someone else to do the work?

**Economics**
- Do they have other clients now? May they take more?
- Do they use their own tools, software and workspace, and carry their own costs?
- Are they paid by the project or milestone, or by time like a wage?
- Can they make a profit or a loss on the job?
- Do they have a business entity, their own insurance, their own marketing?

**The arrangement**
- Is there a written contract that matches how the work will really run?
- Any benefits, paid time off, company email, title or place on the org chart?
- Can either side end it at will, like a job?

What the parties call it and what the person prefers do not decide the answer.

## 3. Decide

- **Every test that applies points to an independent business:** contractor. Write the record.
- **Any test that applies is failed or unclear:** not a contractor. Either hire the person as an employee through the
  provider, engage them through an agency or employer of record that employs them, or ask a lawyer. This is the
  default because the costs are lopsided: the wrong "employee" costs some payroll tax; the wrong "contractor" costs
  back taxes, back wages and penalties.
- **Signals that on their own make "contractor" unsafe:** full-time hours for months with no other clients; a former
  employee doing the same job; a manager who sets their schedule; an open-ended role in the company's core business.

For doubt on the federal tax side only, the IRS will rule on Form SS-8, but a determination can take six months or
more, so it does not help a start date.

## 4. The decision record

Save to the worker's task and note it in `knowledge/contractors.md`.

```
Worker (business name if any):
Role and start date:
State where the work is done:
Tests checked, each with page and date read:
Facts under each heading above:
Decision: contractor / employee / through an agency
Why, in three lines:
Decided by, and OK from (if any factor pointed to employee):
Review date: 6 months, or when the scope, hours or exclusivity change
```

## 5. Keep it true

Review each contractor every six months and whenever the work changes. The record describes the arrangement on day
one; what counts is how it actually runs. If it has drifted toward employment, that is the hard stop above.
