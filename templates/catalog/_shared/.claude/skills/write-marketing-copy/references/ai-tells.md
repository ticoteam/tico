# AI tells: the patterns to remove

Readers now spot machine-made copy in a sentence and stop trusting the page. These patterns are the usual giveaways.
Most were bad copy before models existed: they sound like meaning without carrying any.

**How to fix a hit.** Do not swap one word for a synonym. Ask "what do I actually mean, and what could the reader
see?" and write that. If nothing is left when the pattern is removed, the sentence had nothing in it: cut it.

**Where it matters.** Zero hits in the headline, the line under it, the button, an email subject and an ad. In body
copy, fix every hit you find. A pattern used once, on purpose, by a company whose Brand card says it writes that way,
is allowed: the Brand card wins.

## 1. The contrast reveal

Setting up a thing only to knock it down.

| Tell | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| "It's not just a repair service. It's peace of mind." | "We fix your bike while you're at work." |
| "This isn't about tools. It's about people." | Say the thing about people, with a fact. |
| "More than a dashboard" | Say what it does that a dashboard does not. |

## 2. The negation list

Selling by listing what is absent.

| Tell | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| "No contracts. No hidden fees. No hassle." | "Cancel any month from your account page. The price on this page is the whole price." |
| "No more waiting. No more guessing." | "You get a text when the mechanic starts and when the bike is on its way back." |

One plain "no" that answers a real objection is fine ("No card needed for the trial").

## 3. The pile-on

A tail added to sound complete: a list of three adjectives, or a clause starting with an "-ing" word.

| Tell | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| "Fast, simple and secure." | Pick the one that matters and prove it: "Back by 5 the same day." |
| "...so you can focus on what matters most." | Cut it, or name what matters: "...so you still make the 8:15." |
| "...helping teams move faster and work smarter." | Cut the tail. The sentence before it was the point. |
| "...and so much more." | List the next real thing, or stop. |

## 4. The self-answered question and the colon reveal

Building a drum roll in front of an ordinary statement.

| Tell | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| "The result? Happier commutes." | "Riders told us they stopped keeping a spare bike." (with the source) |
| "Here's the thing:" / "The best part:" / "The truth is," | Delete the lead-in. Start with the statement. |
| "Why does this matter? Because..." | State why. |
| "Ready to transform your commute?" | "Book a pickup" |

## 5. Stock openers and closers

Lines that could start or end any piece about anything.

- Openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-changing landscape of", "Whether you're a ... or a ...",
  "Let's face it", "We get it", "Imagine a world where", "Say goodbye to", "Meet your new".
- Closers: "At the end of the day", "The future of X is here", "Join thousands of happy customers" (without the count
  and its source), "Your journey starts here", "What are you waiting for?".

Rewrite: open with the reader's situation or the promise. Close with the action and what happens next.

## 6. Buzzwords

Words that claim a quality instead of showing it. Each one fails the picturable test.

unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, supercharge, streamline, revolutionize, reimagine, transform, leverage, harness,
seamless, effortless, robust, powerful, cutting-edge, next-level, next-generation, game-changing, world-class,
best-in-class, innovative, intuitive, holistic, tailored, curated, bespoke, solution, platform (when it is an app),
journey, landscape, ecosystem, tapestry, realm, delve, navigate (for anything but a route), testament, pivotal,
crucial, vibrant.

Rewrite: say what the thing does, in a verb the customer uses. "Seamless booking" becomes "Book in two taps".
"Powerful reporting" becomes "See every repair and what it cost on one page".

## 7. Padding and hedges

- Intensifiers: truly, really, very, incredibly, absolutely, simply, just, actually, literally.
- Hedges: "can help you to", "is designed to", "aims to", "may potentially", "one of the most".
- Announcements: "It's worth noting that", "It's important to remember", "As you can see", "Simply put".

Rewrite: delete the word. If the sentence turns false without the hedge, the claim was not proven: fix the claim.

## 8. Fake specifics

Numbers and crowds with nothing behind them.

| Tell | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| "10x faster" | The measured figure with its source and date, or no figure. |
| "Save hours every week" | "Riders on the plan skip about two shop visits a year" (only if counted). |
| "Trusted by thousands of teams" | The real count, dated, or named customers with permission. |
| "Industry-leading" | Who measured, what, and when, or cut it. |

An invented number is not a style fault. It fails the claims gate.

## 9. The vague subject

"Teams", "businesses", "users", "modern cyclists", "today's leaders". Name the reader the spine names: "If you ride
to work four days a week". Speak to "you".

## 10. Shape tells

What the page looks like before a word is read.

- Every section the same length, every list exactly three items, every bullet built the same way.
- A bold lead-in on every bullet ("**Fast.** We are fast.").
- Title Case On Every Heading (unless the Brand card asks for it).
- Dashes used as pauses in short copy, and long chains joined with semicolons. Use a full stop.
- Emoji as bullets or at the end of headings.
- A closing paragraph that repeats the piece ("In summary", "Overall").
- Headings that are labels ("Our Solution", "Key Benefits", "Why Choose Us") instead of statements.

Rewrite: let the content set the shape. Two proofs are two proofs. A section that needs one line gets one line.

## 11. Praise for the reader, and stage directions

- "Great question", "You deserve better", "We know how hard you work".
- "Let's dive in", "Let's break it down", "Read on to learn", "Without further ado".

Rewrite: cut. Start.

## A quick pass, in order

1. Read the headline, subhead and button against sections 1 to 6. Any hit: rewrite from the spine.
2. Search the body for the section 6 and 7 words. Rewrite each sentence they sit in.
3. Look at the page's shape against section 10.
4. Read aloud. Mark every sentence you would not say to a customer across a counter. Rewrite or cut.
5. Run the rival swap on what is left.

This list goes stale as habits change. When a new pattern starts showing up in the company's drafts, add it to
`knowledge/messaging.md` under "Our tells", with a rewrite and the date, and check that list too.
