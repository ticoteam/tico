# Admission control

Run every sentence of an outgoing reply, and every internal note about the matter, through this file. The aim is a
reply that is accurate, courteous and useful, and that hands the other side nothing to quote.

## The rule in one line

State what the record shows happened. Do not state why it happened, whose fault it was, what it means in law, or what
will happen next time.

## Words to avoid, and what to write instead

| Avoid | Why | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| "our mistake", "our error", "we failed", "we should have", "we dropped the ball" | Admits fault | What happened, as a fact: "The visit on 2 November began at 10:04 and ended at 12:11." |
| "negligent", "breach", "violation", "defective", "unsafe", "illegal", "fraud", "misled" | Legal conclusions, and their words | The neutral fact: "the kitchen counters were not wiped" |
| "liable", "responsible for", "at fault", "owe" | Admits the claim | "has agreed, as a courtesy, to" |
| "we apologize for our ..." / "sorry we ..." | Ties regret to the company's conduct | Nothing, or at most once, where the record shows something went wrong for them: "We are sorry to learn that the visit did not meet your expectations." Regret about their experience, not about an act. |
| "we apologize for any inconvenience", "sorry for the trouble" as an opener | Reflex sympathy reads as a form letter, and the reader learns nothing | Restate their complaint accurately instead (below) |
| "always", "never", "every time", "guarantee" | One exception makes the sentence false | The specific instance and its date |
| "probably", "it seems", "we assume", "it appears that our cleaner ..." | Speculation becomes an admission | "The record does not show ..." or leave it out |
| "this won't happen again", "we are retraining", "we have changed our process", "the employee has been disciplined" | Promise of future conduct; suggests the old way was wrong; private personnel matter | Leave out. If the team did make a change, it goes in an internal operations task. |
| "as you know", "obviously", "clearly", "frankly", "with respect" | Combative filler | Nothing |
| "you failed to", "you lied", "your claim is false", "frivolous", "bad faith" | Attacks the person; reads badly to a mediator | "The record shows otherwise: ..." |
| "refund" used for a goodwill payment larger than what was paid | Suggests the money was owed | "a courtesy payment of $X" |
| "settle", "settlement" in a first reply to a consumer | Suggests there is a claim to settle | "to resolve this" |
| "per our policy" with no document | Sounds like a brush-off | Quote the term, with its section and the date it was accepted |

## Sentences that are safe and strong

- "The Company does not accept that characterization. The record shows the following."
- "The Company has reviewed its records for [date range]. They show: (1) ... (2) ... (3) ..."
- "The Company has no record of [the call / the request / the promise] described."
- "Under Section [n] of the Terms you accepted on [date], '[exact words]'."
- "Without admitting any liability, and solely as a courtesy to a valued customer, the Company will [act] by [date]."
- "This offer remains open until [date] and is made without prejudice to the Company's position."
- "The Company declines the request for [item] for the reasons above."
- "Nothing in this letter waives any right, remedy or defense of the Company, all of which are expressly reserved."

## Acknowledging the person without admitting anything

The customer must feel heard. That needs three things, none of which is an admission:

1. Restate their complaint accurately, in a sentence or two, without softening or sharpening it.
2. Say that the company looked: what records it reviewed and for what dates.
3. Say what the company has done and will do, with dates.

Regret is used sparingly: at most one line, only where the record shows something really went wrong for them, and
never by reflex. It is aimed at their experience ("we are sorry to learn", "we understand this was frustrating"),
never at the company's conduct. A formal channel never carries an apology for what the company did, even when the
company got it wrong and even with an OK: the fix shows it instead. A few states have laws that keep certain expressions of sympathy out of evidence, mostly in
medical settings and mostly not covering statements of fault. Do not rely on them.

## Offers and settlement talk

- A plain remedy the customer is entitled to under the company's own terms (a redo, a refund within policy) is not
  a settlement. Give it, and say it is given "under the Company's [policy name]" or "as a courtesy".
- An offer that goes beyond that is a compromise offer. Put it in its own letter or its own clearly separated
  section, headed "Confidential settlement communication", and keep the factual answer free of it. Under Federal
  Rule of Evidence 408 and the state rules like it, compromise offers and statements made in compromise talks
  generally cannot be used to prove or disprove the claim. The heading alone does not create that protection, the
  rule has exceptions, and facts that exist anyway do not become protected by being mentioned. State rules differ:
  check the state when it matters.
- Never put a settlement heading on a letter that is only a denial. It suggests there is something to pay for.
- A reply to the BBB or a government office is not a private settlement talk. Assume it will be read by the agency
  and may be published. Make any offer there plainly and without a settlement heading.
- Money beyond a plain refund is paid only against a signed release. The release says: the amount, no admission,
  what claims are released, that each side keeps the terms private where the law allows. It never stops a person
  from complaining to a government body or from posting an honest review, and it never asks them to take one down
  in exchange for money. The first time the company uses a release template, a lawyer reads it.

## Internal notes are evidence too

- A bot's analysis is not a lawyer's. Notes, task comments, drafts and chat messages about a dispute can be demanded
  by the other side in a lawsuit. In February 2026 a federal court in New York held that documents a person created
  with an AI tool on his own, without a lawyer directing the work, were not privileged and were not work product
  (United States v. Heppner). That is one district court; assume it is how most courts will see a bot's notes.
- So internal writing follows the same rule as the reply: facts, sources, options, costs. Not "we are clearly liable",
  "this is bad for us", "the cleaner lied".
- Do not label bot-written material "privileged" or "attorney work product". The label is false and can mislead the
  team about what is protected.
- When a lawyer is engaged and asks for work, do it on the lawyer's written request, address it to the lawyer, and
  keep it on the private matter task. Whether it is protected is the lawyer's call.
- Keep the matter on its private task. Do not discuss it in open threads, and do not paste the complaint into
  unrelated tasks.

## What is never withheld

Admission control is about wording and scope. It never means stating something false, hiding a document that has
been asked for by a court or an agency, altering a record, or telling a government office something misleading. If
an accurate answer hurts the company, the answer is still accurate, and the matter goes to the approver and a lawyer
before it is sent.
