# Channels: who reads the reply, what the date means, what silence costs

Facts here were read on 2026-11-18. Every number can change, and many differ by state or by local office. The date
on the letter or in the portal always wins; these are the defaults to check it against.

## By channel

| Channel | Who reads the reply | Usual reply window | What silence costs | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A customer writes directly, threatening action | The customer, later perhaps a judge or mediator | Their date; aim to answer within 5 business days | Escalation to a lawyer, an agency, a card dispute or a review | Reply by the same channel, in writing |
| A lawyer's demand letter | The lawyer, then a judge as an exhibit | Their date (often 10 to 30 days). Not binding by itself. | A filed claim, and the letter is shown to the court unanswered | Reply to the lawyer, never the client. Ask for an extension in writing if needed and confirm it in writing. |
| A notice under a state consumer-protection law | The consumer's lawyer; later a court deciding damages | A statutory window, often 30 or 60 days from receipt | Loss of the chance to limit damages; in some states, multiplied damages and fees | See the next table. Always needs an OK, and a lawyer's eye is strongly advised. |
| Better Business Bureau complaint | A BBB dispute specialist, the customer, and usually the public | 14 calendar days from the BBB's notice; a second notice follows if there is no reply; most complaints close in about 30 days | The complaint is recorded as unanswered, which counts against the BBB rating and shows on the profile | Reply through the BBB portal. The reply is usually published: no personal data, no legal argument. |
| State attorney general or consumer-affairs complaint (mediation) | An agency mediator; the file can inform enforcement | The date in the agency's letter (commonly two to four weeks) | A note that the business did not respond; repeated silence invites an investigation | Voluntary mediation in most states, but treat the date as firm. Reply to the agency, in the form it asks for. |
| Licensing or professional board complaint | A board investigator | The date in the notice | Discipline can follow from not responding alone | The licence is at stake: OK always, and tell the licence holder the same day. |
| Consumer Financial Protection Bureau complaint (only for financial products and services) | The Bureau and the consumer | A response within 15 calendar days of the complaint reaching the company portal; if marked in progress, a final response within 60 | The complaint shows as untimely in a public database | Only applies if the company offers a consumer financial product. |
| A federal agency letter (for example the Federal Trade Commission) | Agency staff | The date in the letter | Serious | Federal agencies generally do not mediate single complaints. A letter from one is an inquiry: stop list. |
| Subpoena, civil investigative demand, notice of violation | Government lawyers | The date in the document; legally binding | Enforcement, penalties | Stop list. Counsel leads. |
| Marketplace or app-store dispute case | The platform's case team | The platform's date, often a few days | The case is decided for the buyer and counts against seller metrics | Use the platform's evidence form; same facts discipline. |
| Card chargeback, small-claims notice, arbitration demand, summons | | | | Not this skill. Go to `defend-a-consumer-claim`. |
| A public review | | | | Not this skill. Use the team's reputation skills. |

## Notices under consumer-protection laws

Most states have a law against unfair or deceptive practices that lets a consumer sue, often for more than their
loss and for their lawyer's fees. Several require the consumer to send a written notice first and give the business
a window to put things right. Used well, that window is the best protection the business gets. Three well-known
examples (secondary summaries read 2026-11-18; read the statute before relying on any of them):

| Law | Window | What a timely response can do | What to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| California Consumers Legal Remedies Act, Civil Code section 1782 | 30 days from receipt of the notice | If an appropriate correction, repair, replacement or other remedy is given, or agreed to be given within a reasonable time, within the 30 days, no action for damages under the Act may be maintained | The offer must actually correct the problem. A court has held that an offer tied to a broad release was not an "appropriate correction". Injunctive claims are not barred. |
| Massachusetts General Laws chapter 93A, section 9(3) | 30 days from mailing or delivery of the demand | A written tender of settlement that is reasonable in relation to the injury can limit recovery to the amount tendered and avoid multiplied damages | Failing to respond, or refusing relief in bad faith, risks double or triple damages and fees. The reply must be in writing. |
| Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act, Business and Commerce Code sections 17.505 and 17.5052 | The consumer must give 60 days' notice before suing; the business may tender a settlement offer during the 60 days after receiving it | An offer the court later finds to be the same as or substantially the same as the actual damages can cap damages and fees | The offer has required parts, including an amount for the consumer's reasonable lawyer's fees to date. Conditional offers have failed. |

How to handle one:

1. Day one: identify the statute cited, read the section at an official source, and put the last day of the window
   on the calendar with the arithmetic (`check-a-legal-deadline`).
2. Build the facts in the first week.
3. By the decision date, give the approver two drafts: a point-by-point reply, and a written offer sized to the
   customer's actual loss from the record. The approver, with a lawyer where possible, chooses.
4. Send in writing, with proof of delivery, inside the window.

If the letter cites a statute not listed here, the same four steps apply. Do not assume it has no window.

## Extensions

Ask early, in writing, with a reason and a new date. Confirm any extension granted in writing the same day ("This
confirms that you have agreed to extend the date for the Company's response to [date]"). A government office or the
BBB may grant one through its portal. An extension from a lawyer does not move a statutory window unless the lawyer
says in writing that it does, and even then check the statute.

## Recording the dates

On the matter page, show one line per date: what it is, where it comes from (document and section), the arithmetic,
the action date, days left, and "confirmed" or "from a summary". Two sources that disagree are both shown.
