# Reply templates

Square brackets mark what to fill from `facts.md`. A reply that still has a bracket in it is not ready. Keep what the
facts support and delete the rest. Run the result through `admission-control.md`.

## House style

- Formal, firm, exact and courteous. Third person for the company ("the Company"), after naming it once in full.
- The position comes first. Then the record. Then the answer to each point. Then what happens next.
- Numbered paragraphs. Their allegations answered in their order, each under a short heading that restates it
  neutrally.
- Dates in full. Quotes in quotation marks with their source. Exhibits numbered and attached.
- Short. A page or two for a customer or an agency; as long as the allegations need for a lawyer, and no longer.
- No law in replies to a customer, the BBB or an agency mediator. To a lawyer, the contract first; law only if read
  at its source in this run.
- No internal labels. No "privileged". A settlement heading only on a real compromise offer.

## 1. Substantive reply to a lawyer's demand

```
[Company letterhead]

[Date]

BY EMAIL ([address]) AND [TRACKED POST]

[Lawyer's name]
[Firm]
[Address]

Re: [Client's name] / [Company legal name] - Your letter dated [date]

Dear [Mr./Ms. Surname]:

1. [Company legal name] (the "Company") responds to your letter dated [date], received on [date], sent on
   behalf of [client] ("your client"). The Company has reviewed its records. For the reasons below, the Company
   does not accept the claims made in your letter and declines the demand for [what was demanded].

The record

2. The Company's records for the period [date] to [date] show the following.
   (a) On [date], your client [ordered / signed / accepted] [what], and accepted the Company's [Terms] dated
       [version date] by [method] (Exhibit 1).
   (b) On [date], [what was delivered or done] (Exhibit 2).
   (c) On [date], [message, call or payment] (Exhibit 3).
   [continue in date order; only what an exhibit shows]

Response to the matters raised

3. "[Allegation 1, restated neutrally]." The Company does not accept this. [The record shows ... (Exhibit n).]
4. "[Allegation 2]." [The Company has no record of ... / This is correct as far as it goes; however ...]
5. "[Allegation 3]." [Answer.]

The terms that govern

6. Section [n] of the Terms provides: "[exact words]". [One sentence applying it: the remedy it gives, the limit
   it sets, the notice it requires.]
7. Section [n] of the Terms provides that any dispute is to be resolved by [arbitration before ... / the courts
   of ...]. The Company reserves its right to require that any claim proceed there.

[Optional] Resolution

8. Without admitting any liability, and solely to avoid further cost to both sides, the Company is prepared to
   [act or amount], provided your client [signs a release in the enclosed form] by [date]. This paragraph is a
   confidential settlement communication.

Preservation

9. Please confirm that your client is preserving all documents and communications relating to this matter,
   including [messages, photos, account records].

Reservation of rights

10. This letter is not a complete statement of the Company's position or of the facts. Nothing in it waives any
    right, remedy or defense of the Company, all of which are expressly reserved.

Further correspondence on this matter should be sent to [signer's name] at [email].

[Sincerely,]

[Signer's name], [real title]
[Company legal name]

Enclosures: Exhibits 1 to [n]
```

Notes
- Paragraph 8 is better as a separate letter when the offer is more than nominal, so the factual reply can be shown
  to a court without it.
- If an allegation is right, do not deny it. Say what happened as a neutral fact and move to what was done about it.
- If the company has its own claim (an unpaid invoice, damage to equipment), state it in one paragraph and reserve
  it. Do not turn the reply into a counter-demand without the approver's decision.

## 2. Holding letter

For a lawyer, a customer or an agency, when the facts are not yet in or a decision is pending.

```
Re: [Matter] - Your [letter / complaint] dated [date]

Dear [Name]:

[Company legal name] (the "Company") acknowledges receipt on [date] of your [letter / complaint] dated [date].

The Company is reviewing the matters raised and its records. It will respond in substance by [date].

[If needed: To complete that review, the Company asks that you provide [the order number / the photos referred
to / a copy of the document quoted].]

Nothing in this letter is an admission of any matter, and the Company reserves all of its rights, remedies and
defenses.

[Sincerely,]
[Signer's name], [real title]
```

Notes
- Pick a date the team will meet. A missed promised date is worse than a later one.
- Do not send a holding letter in place of a statutory offer when the window would then run out.
- Say nothing on the merits. No "we take this seriously", no "we are sorry for the trouble".

## 3. Reply to a formal customer complaint (direct)

```
Re: Your complaint of [date] - [order or account reference]

Dear [Mr./Ms. Surname]:

1. Thank you for your [email / letter] of [date]. I have reviewed it and the Company's records for your
   [booking / order / account].

2. I understand your complaint to be that [their complaint, restated accurately in one or two sentences], and
   that you are asking for [what they asked for].

3. The Company's position is as follows: [one sentence: what the Company will and will not do].

4. The records show:
   (a) [dated fact] (copy enclosed);
   (b) [dated fact];
   (c) [dated fact].

5. [Only on a point the record shows they are right about; otherwise delete:] [Neutral fact.] We are sorry to learn
   that [their experience].

6. [On each point not accepted:] The Company does not accept that [point], because [record].

7. As a courtesy, and without any admission, the Company will [remedy] by [date]. [You do not need to do anything
   further. / To accept, reply to this letter by [date].]

8. The Company declines the request for [item], for the reasons in paragraphs [n].

9. Nothing in this letter waives any right or defense of the Company.

If you have further information you would like the Company to consider, please send it to me at [email].

[Sincerely,]
[Signer's name], [real title]
```

Notes
- Paragraph 2 is what makes the customer feel heard. Get it exactly right; use their own key words where they are
  neutral.
- One named person signs and stays the contact. Do not send them back to a general support queue.
- If the remedy fully gives what they asked, say so in paragraph 3 and cut the rest down to the record and the
  reservation.
- Paragraph 5 is the only place regret may appear, once. No apology for what the company did, and no "sorry for any
  inconvenience" anywhere.

## 4. Reply to a BBB complaint

Written for the BBB specialist and for the public page. Plain, calm, factual, no law, no personal data.

```
[Company name] has reviewed this complaint and its records for the customer's [order / booking] of [date].

Summary: [one sentence: the Company's position and what it has offered].

What the records show:
- [Date]: [fact].
- [Date]: [fact].
- [Date]: [fact].

Response to the complaint:
- "[Point 1]": [answer from the record].
- "[Point 2]": [answer from the record].

Resolution: As a courtesy to the customer, [Company name] has [done / offered] [remedy] [on date / by date]. The
customer can accept by [how]. [Company name] is not able to agree to [item declined] because [short factual reason].

[Company name] considers this a fair resolution and remains available at [contact] to complete it.
```

Notes
- Attach documents through the portal as private attachments where the portal allows; keep the public text free
  of addresses, phone numbers, card details and anyone else's name.
- If the customer rejects the reply, the BBB usually asks once more. Answer again, briefly, restating the offer.
  Do not escalate the tone.
- Never mention the customer's other complaints, reviews or payment history unless it is the subject of the
  complaint.
- No apology for the company's conduct and no stock sympathy. Showing the fix is what reads well on the public page.

## 5. Reply to a state attorney general, consumer agency or licensing board

Addressed to the agency, with the file number. Respectful, complete, on time.

```
[Date]

[Agency name, division]
[Mediator or investigator's name]
Re: Complaint No. [number] - [Consumer's name] / [Company legal name]

Dear [Mr./Ms. Surname]:

1. [Company legal name] (the "Company") responds to the above complaint, received on [date]. The Company
   appreciates the opportunity to respond.

2. Summary of the Company's response: [two sentences: position and resolution offered].

3. Background: [what the Company does, in one sentence; licence number if a board asked].

4. Chronology, from the Company's records (copies enclosed):
   (a) [date]: [fact] (Exhibit 1);
   (b) [date]: [fact] (Exhibit 2);
   [...]

5. Response to each point in the complaint:
   (a) "[Point 1]": [answer].
   (b) "[Point 2]": [answer].

6. Resolution: [what the Company has done or offers, by when], offered as a courtesy and without any admission.

7. The Company [has provided / is providing] a copy of this response to the consumer [as the agency requested].
   Please contact [signer's name] at [email, phone] if the [Division] needs anything further.

[Respectfully,]
[Signer's name], [real title]
[Company legal name]

Enclosures
```

Notes
- Answer what the agency asked, in the form it asked for. If it asked numbered questions, answer by number.
- Everything said to a government office must be accurate and complete as far as it goes. If an accurate answer
  is unhelpful, the matter goes to the approver and a lawyer before it is sent; it is not shaded.
- If the letter asks for documents, data or an interview, or mentions an investigation, it is not a mediation
  letter. Stop list.
- Leave out legal argument about the agency's authority. That is for a lawyer if it is ever needed.

## 6. Offer letter inside a statutory window

Use only with the approver's OK, and with a lawyer's review where one is available. Separate from the factual reply.

```
CONFIDENTIAL SETTLEMENT COMMUNICATION

Re: [Client] / [Company legal name] - Notice dated [date] under [statute as cited in their letter]

Dear [Mr./Ms. Surname]:

1. The Company refers to your notice dated [date], received on [date]. The Company does not accept that it has
   violated [the statute] and makes this offer without admitting any liability.

2. Within the time allowed by [the statute], the Company offers the following to your client:
   (a) [the correction, repair, replacement or refund, stated exactly];
   (b) [payment of $X, being [how it is calculated from the client's stated loss]];
   (c) [where the statute calls for it: $Y toward reasonable attorney's fees incurred to date].

3. The Company will carry out paragraph 2 within [n] days of your client's written acceptance.

4. This offer is open until [date].

5. All of the Company's rights and defenses are reserved.
```

Notes
- Read the statute for what a qualifying offer must contain and whether it may be made conditional on a release.
  Under at least one state's law, a correction offer that demanded a broad release did not count.
- Size the offer to the client's actual loss as the record shows it, and write the arithmetic on the matter page.

## 7. Reply to a cease-and-desist the company received

Same structure as template 1. In place of "The record":

- State the company's own right or basis: its first-use date, its registration, its licence, its independent
  creation, the truth of what it said, or the contract term that permits the conduct, each with an exhibit.
- Answer each demand: agreed (and done on what date), agreed in part, or declined.
- If the company will change something to end the matter, say exactly what and by when, "without admitting that
  the change is required".
- Do not promise never to do something in the future unless the approver has decided to give that undertaking.
- If the sender's claim is weak and it matters to the business, the approver may want a lawyer to consider whether
  the company should ask a court to rule first. Raise it; do not draft it.
