# Sources

Read or searched on 2026-11-18 unless a line says otherwise. Ideas were adapted; no text was copied.

This library's research notes did not cover partnerships, so this skill rests on a fresh web search. Nearly all
public writing on partner programs comes from companies that sell partner software or run partner communities.
Their advice is consistent and plausible, and their numbers are their own, unaudited, usually with no stated sample.
Every figure taken from them is dated and labelled where it appears, and none is used as a target.

## Practitioner and vendor sources

- Crossbeam, guide to scoping and qualifying technology partners.
  https://insider.crossbeam.com/resources/guide-scoping-and-qualifying-tech-partners
  Ideas used: start from customers' integration requests; check a candidate's readiness to partner and the
  openness of its API; check the team's own capacity; compare accounts and pick one overlapping account for an
  early joint win; agree a term sheet before a contract.
- PartnerStack, published guidance on partner attribution and program operations. https://partnerstack.com
  Ideas used: "sourced" means created or referred by the partner and "influenced" means the partner helped an
  existing deal; write the definitions down and audit them regularly.
- Pavilion, 2025 report on partnerships (no sample size stated in the summary read).
  Ideas used: common causes of failure are misaligned goals, attribution disputes, and weak partner selection and
  onboarding; do not fund partner fees out of the direct sellers' commission.
- Rewardful and similar affiliate-software vendors, 2026 commission summaries.
  Used only for the dated sanity-check range in `partner-types-and-terms.md`: referral and affiliate fees for
  subscription software commonly quoted around 20 to 30 percent of first-year revenue or a recurring share limited
  to about 12 months, with one vendor's own-program average of about 24 percent in 2026. Vendor data.
- Several vendor guides on partner onboarding. Ideas used: a 30-60-90 structure (access and alignment; enablement
  and first pipeline; momentum and review); a rule of thumb that 40 to 60 percent of new partners register a deal
  within 90 days, from one vendor with no stated sample.
- General writing on the "ideal partner profile": shared customers, complementary products, capacity and incentive.
  The six-question scorecard here is this library's own arrangement of those ideas.

No reliable public data on reseller margins was found. The skill says so and gives none.

## Law to check locally

Checked 2026-11-18. Not legal advice. These change; read the current text and use a lawyer where the skill says to.

- **Disclosure of paid recommendations (US).** FTC Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in
  Advertising, 16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023. A material connection between an endorser and a seller, including a
  referral fee or affiliate link, must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. The advertiser can be liable and is
  expected to instruct and monitor those it pays. Other countries have their own advertising rules.
- **Resale price maintenance (US).** Leegin Creative Leather Products v. PSKS (US Supreme Court, 2007) moved minimum
  resale price agreements to case-by-case review under federal law. State law differs: Maryland Commercial Law
  section 11-204 treats agreements setting a minimum resale price as unlawful, and enforcers in California and New
  York have treated them as unlawful in themselves. The summary here comes from secondary legal commentary.
- **Resale price maintenance (EU and UK).** Fixed or minimum resale prices are treated as a serious ("hardcore")
  restriction under the EU's vertical agreements rules and the UK's equivalent order. Stated from general
  knowledge of those rules, not re-read for this skill; confirm before relying on it.
- **Competitors.** Agreements between competitors on prices, customers, territories or bids are unlawful under US
  antitrust law (Sherman Act section 1) and competition law elsewhere. A partner that is also a competitor needs a
  lawyer's ground rules.
- **Payments.** The US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and laws like the UK Bribery Act cover payments made through
  third parties. Commercial bribery of a private company's employee is also unlawful in many states and countries.
- **Customer data.** Sharing customer lists or personal data with a partner needs a lawful basis and, often, notice
  or consent, under laws such as the GDPR, UK GDPR and US state privacy laws, and must be consistent with the
  team's own privacy notice and customer contracts.
- **Email outreach.** See the outreach notes in `run-a-sales-deal/references/sources.md`.

## Numbers in this skill

The limit of three active partners, the 30-day first win, the 90-day protection and lapse periods, the one-working-
day registration answer, the 12-month term with 30 days' notice, the fit-score cut-offs, and the 20-response and
20-customer floors for percentages are starting defaults chosen for this library, several of them inherited from
the Tico catalog's partner practice. None is a benchmark. The team's values in `knowledge/partnerships.md` and
`knowledge/rules-of-engagement.md` replace them.
