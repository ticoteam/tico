# Partner types, money and terms

Nothing here is legal advice. Every agreement is read with the review-a-contract skill, and a lawyer reads any with
exclusivity, resale, a term over 12 months, or a competitor on the other side.

## Pick the type for the job

| Type | The partner does | Paid how | Best when | Costs the team |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Referral | Introduces a prospect; the team sells and contracts | A fee on what the referred customer pays | The partner is trusted by the buyer and does not want to sell | Little: tracking, fast follow-up, prompt payment |
| Affiliate | Sends traffic through tracked links, at volume | A fee per sale | The product is bought online without a call | A tracking Tool, fraud checks, disclosure checks |
| Reseller or distributor | Sells the product under its own contract with the customer, often bundled | A margin: buys at a discount or keeps a share | The partner owns the customer relationship or a market the team cannot reach | A lot: enablement, price rules, support lines, less contact with the customer |
| Service partner (agency, consultant) | Sets up, customises or runs the product for customers | By the customer for its services; sometimes a referral fee too | Customers need help the team does not want to staff | Training, a point of contact, quality checks |
| Integration or technology partner | Makes the two products work together | Usually no money; each gains product value and exposure | Customers of both ask for it | Engineering time, documentation, upkeep for as long as it exists |
| Marketplace or app store | Lists the product where buyers already shop | The marketplace keeps a share set by its terms | The buyer purchases through that ecosystem | Listing work, the marketplace's fee, its rules |
| Co-marketing | Shares an audience for one campaign | Usually none; costs split | Audiences overlap and neither competes | One campaign's work; a lead-sharing rule |
| Strategic or OEM | Builds the product into its own | Negotiated | Rare; only when asked by a serious party | Legal and engineering time. Owner-led from the start |

Start with the type that needs the least from both sides and proves demand: usually referral, or one integration
that customers have asked for by name. Add heavier types when a lighter one has produced customers.

## Setting the fee from the team's own numbers

Do this before quoting anyone a percentage.

1. **Gross margin per customer** over the first 12 months: revenue less the direct cost of serving them.
   Source: `tico finance report pnl`, the billing Tool, or the measure-unit-economics skill.
2. **Cost to win a customer today** through the channels in use. Source: `tico growth numbers`, or spend divided by
   new customers for the same period, stated with the period.
3. **What a partner-won customer does differently.** Check, do not assume: do they stay longer or shorter, cost
   more or less in support, buy bigger or smaller plans? With fewer than 20 such customers, say "too few to tell".
4. **The ceiling:** the partner fee plus the team's own cost of supporting the partner route, per customer, should
   be no more than the cost to win a customer another way, and must leave a positive margin after it. Write the
   arithmetic down.
5. **Offer below the ceiling,** in a form simple enough to say in one sentence.

Public ranges, for a sanity check only (read 2026-11-18; all from vendors of partner software, none audited):

- Referral and affiliate fees for subscription software are commonly quoted at roughly 20 to 30 percent of
  first-year revenue, or a recurring share limited to about 12 months. One vendor's 2026 data set put the average
  across its own programs at about 24 percent.
- Percentages tend to fall as deal size rises.
- No reliable public data on reseller margins was found. Do not quote one.

If the team's ceiling is far below these ranges, a cash fee may not be the right offer; consider what else the
partner values (leads back, a better product for their customers, a listing, training).

## The term sheet

One page, plain words, agreed before lawyers draft anything. Headings:

1. **Parties and purpose.** One sentence on what the partnership is for.
2. **What each side does.** Three to five lines each. Named roles, not names.
3. **Fee or margin.** The percentage or amount; of what exactly (payments received, net of refunds and taxes); for
   how long; how often paid.
4. **Sourced and influenced, defined.** See below.
5. **Deal registration.** How to register, what makes a registration complete, how long protection lasts, renewal.
6. **Conflicts.** Who is paid when two partners, or a partner and the team's own sales, both claim a customer.
7. **Payment and clawback.** Paid after the customer pays; taken back if refunded or charged back within N days.
8. **Exclusivity.** Default: none.
9. **Term and ending.** Default: 12 months, renewing, either side may end on 30 days' notice; fees already earned on
   existing customers continue for the stated period.
10. **Names and logos.** Each may name the other as a partner in agreed words; anything more needs approval.
11. **Customer data and confidentiality.** What is shared, on what basis, and how it is protected.
12. **Disclosure.** The partner tells prospects it may be paid.
13. **Not a contract.** A line saying this page is a summary for discussion and binds neither side until an
    agreement is signed.

## Definitions to write down before the first dispute

- **Sourced:** the customer was not in the team's records as an open opportunity or active lead, and the partner
  registered them, or made a recorded introduction, before the team's first sales contact. Earns the full fee.
- **Influenced:** the team already had the opportunity and the partner helped it close (a recommendation, joint
  work, an integration that decided it). Default: no fee, or a smaller stated one. Count it and report it apart.
- **Active lead:** say what makes a lead "already ours": for example, a two-way exchange in the last 90 days.
- **Customer:** one paying account. Say how upgrades, extra seats and second products are treated.

Keep these in `knowledge/rules-of-engagement.md` and audit a sample of credited deals against them each month.
Disputes over who gets credit are among the most commonly reported reasons partner programs sour, so settle the
wording before the first deal.

## Registration and conflict rules (defaults from the Tico catalog's partner practice)

- A registration is valid only when complete: company, contact, what they need, the date of the partner's
  conversation.
- The first complete registration wins, unless the team already had a dated open opportunity.
- Answer every registration within one working day: accepted, or refused with the reason.
- Protection lasts 90 days and is renewed on evidence of progress. It ends on its date without anyone having to act.
- A partner with no deal in 90 days gets one dated next step or the relationship lapses.
- Do not fund partner fees by cutting what the team's own sellers earn on the same deal; that sets the two against
  each other. (A practitioner report read for this skill names this as a common failure.)

## Clauses that need a lawyer and an OK

| Clause | Why it matters | Default position |
|---|---|---|
| Exclusivity (by territory, segment or product) | Blocks every other partner, sometimes for years | None. If unavoidable: narrow, time-limited, with minimums the partner must hit and automatic loss of exclusivity if it misses |
| Most-favoured terms | Every later deal must match or better it | None |
| Non-compete or non-solicit | Can restrict the team's own selling or hiring | None, or narrow and mutual |
| Non-circumvention | Stops either side going round the other to a customer | Reasonable if limited to registered deals and a set time |
| Resale price terms | Setting or policing a reseller's price is a competition-law risk | Suggested prices only; see below |
| Minimum purchase or volume commitments | Creates a payment duty | Only with exclusivity, and only on the partner |
| Intellectual property and data rights | Who owns integration work, who may use customer data | Each keeps its own; data used only for the stated purpose |
| Liability, indemnity, insurance | Who pays when something goes wrong | Read by a lawyer every time |
| Auto-renewal and notice | A long tail the team may forget | 12 months, 30 days' notice, the date counted with `count-a-notice-deadline` |
| Governing law and disputes | Where and how a fight happens | The team's home jurisdiction where possible |

## Resale prices

Do not set the price a reseller must charge, agree a minimum resale price, or punish discounting, without a legal
read. US position as of 2026-11-18: under federal law minimum resale price agreements are judged case by case
(the Supreme Court's Leegin decision, 2007), but state law differs: Maryland's statute treats them as unlawful
outright, and enforcers in California and New York have taken a similar view. The EU and UK treat fixed or minimum
resale prices as a serious restriction. A recommended price the reseller is free to ignore is the safe default
everywhere. Check current law for each place the reseller sells.

## Reselling other people's products inside yours

If the partnership means the team resells, bundles or white-labels someone else's service, read every layer of terms
first (the partner's, and their suppliers') with `check-partner-terms-before-resale`. A permission the partner does
not hold cannot be passed on.

## Paying partners

- Pay companies, under a signed agreement, through Finance, with an invoice or statement on file.
- In the US, payments to a partner may need a tax form on file and year-end reporting; the Finance Owner decides.
- Never pay an individual who works for the customer or prospect. Never pay or gift anything to a government
  official or anyone acting for one; the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and similar laws elsewhere apply to payments
  made through partners too.
- A partner outside the team's country adds sanctions and export checks. Ask the owner before the first payment
  abroad.
