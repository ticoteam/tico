# Working with the CPA

The CPA's time is the most expensive input in the tax year. The bot's job is to make every hour of it count: clean
books, one packet, clear questions, no chasing.

## What the CPA does and what the bot does

| The bot | The CPA |
| --- | --- |
| Closes the books and ties every number | Turns book income into taxable income |
| Keeps the calendar and files routine returns | Prepares and signs income tax returns |
| Reports facts: sales, payroll and property by state | Decides where the company owes income tax and how it is split |
| Flags anything unusual the moment it happens | Advises on elections, credits, equity pay and structure |
| Answers the CPA's questions within two business days | Represents the company in an audit |

Ask the CPA for three things once, and write them in `knowledge/taxes.md`: the method for quarterly estimates, the
list of filings they take, and the events they want to hear about before they happen (a new state, a new entity, a
financing, a large asset purchase, a foreign customer or owner).

## The year-end packet

Build it after December is closed (`close-the-month`). Publish an index page and attach the files to one task shared
with the CPA. Each row: item, file, period, status (ready, open with owner, not applicable with the reason).

1. Trial balance and general ledger for the year, and the closing balance sheet and P&L.
2. Bank and card reconciliations for every account at year end.
3. Last year's returns as filed, federal and state, if this CPA did not prepare them.
4. Formation papers, the ownership table at year end, and any change in owners during the year.
5. Fixed assets bought or disposed of: date, cost, what it is, invoice.
6. Loans, notes and convertible instruments: agreement, balance, interest paid.
7. Payroll: the provider's annual summary, the four quarterly returns, the W-3, and state returns.
8. Contractors: the 1099 workpaper and the forms filed.
9. Sales tax: returns filed by state, and the thresholds watch at year end.
10. Sales, payroll and property by state for the year.
11. Estimated tax payments made: date, amount, authority, confirmation.
12. Owner transactions: pay, distributions, loans, expenses paid personally and reimbursed.
13. Equity: grants, exercises, repurchases, valuation reports.
14. Leases signed or changed (`knowledge/leases.md`) and insurance premiums by policy (`knowledge/insurance.md`).
15. Every tax notice received in the year and what was done.
16. Anything unusual: a settlement, a grant, a forgiven debt, a write-off, a customer refund programme.

Under the index, list **mismatches**: any place two records disagree (payroll provider wages against the book, 1099
totals against vendor spend, sales tax returns against revenue by state), with both figures and what you think the
cause is. A mismatch you report costs minutes. One the CPA finds costs hours.

## A request from the CPA

1. Log it on the packet task: what was asked, the date, the date they need it.
2. Add the date to the tax calendar as a line with the CPA as filer.
3. Answer from records. If the answer needs a person's memory, open a sub-task for that person with the one question.
4. Never send an identifier, a bank detail or a login by email. Share files through the task or the CPA's own portal.

## Reviewing the draft return

- Revenue, total payroll, officer pay and year-end cash on the return tie to the book, or the difference is listed.
- The entity name, address, identifier location and tax year are right.
- Estimated payments shown equal the payments made.
- Every state the company touches is either on a state return or has a written reason it is not.
- Ask the CPA for the list of book-to-tax differences, and save it: it explains next year's estimates.
- The officer signs. The signed authorisation form and the acceptance notice are saved to the task.

## The CPA brief (for a hard stop)

One page: the one question; the facts with dates and where each comes from; the documents attached; the deadline and
where it comes from; what is unknown and who would know; the decision the owner will have to make. No conclusions.
