# The letter: skeleton, model paragraphs and style

Square brackets mark what to fill from the matter folder. A letter that still has a bracket in it is not ready. Every
model paragraph is a starting point: keep what the facts support and delete the rest.

## House style

- Formal and exact. Third person for the company ("Lumora Goods LLC"), then a defined short name ("Lumora").
- Numbered paragraphs under plain headings. One point per paragraph.
- Dates in full (18 November 2026). Quotes in quotation marks with their source. Exhibits numbered and attached.
- Facts and demands, no adjectives about the recipient. Not "your brazen theft"; write "the listing at [URL], first
  seen on [date] (Exhibit 1)".
- "Must" for demands. No "we would appreciate", no "unfortunately", no exclamation marks.
- State the law in one or two plain sentences per claim, and only law read at the source in this run. Name the
  statute once. No string of citations.
- Nothing admitted: no mention of the company's doubts, delays, earlier tolerance or what it might accept.
- One to two pages. A long letter reads as weak.
- No internal labels on the letter. Do not mark it "without prejudice" or "settlement communication": a
  cease-and-desist is a demand, not an offer, and the company may want to show it to a court later.

## Skeleton

```
[Company letterhead: exact legal name, address]

[Date]

BY EMAIL ([address]) AND [TRACKED POST / COURIER]

[Recipient's exact legal name]
[Attention: name and title, or the lawyer if they have one]
[Address from the state business register or the contract's notice clause]

Re: [The right in a few words] - Demand to Cease and Desist

[Dear Mr./Ms. Surname: / Dear Counsel:]

1. Who is writing and why        (one paragraph)
2. The company's right           (the right, how it is owned, the register entry)
3. The conduct                   (what, where, since when, exhibits)
4. Why the conduct is unlawful   (the rule in plain words, applied to the facts)
5. Demands                       (numbered, each checkable)
6. Deadline and how to confirm   (a calendar date, to a named person, in writing)
7. Preservation                  (keep all related records)
8. Consequence                   (only what the company has decided)
9. Reservation of rights

[Sincerely,]

[Signer's name]
[Real title]
[Exact legal name of the company]

Enclosures: Exhibits 1 to [n]
```

## Model paragraphs

### Opening
> [Company legal name] ("[Short name]") writes regarding [the conduct in ten words or fewer]. This letter sets out
> [Short name]'s rights, the conduct at issue, and the steps [Recipient short name] must take by [date].

### The right

Trademark, registered:
> [Short name] owns the mark [MARK], registered with the United States Patent and Trademark Office under
> Registration No. [number] for [goods or services as listed] (Exhibit 1). [Short name] has used [MARK] in commerce
> continuously since at least [first-use date].

Trademark, unregistered:
> [Short name] has used the mark [MARK] for [goods or services] continuously since at least [date], in [where],
> and owns common-law rights in the mark in those markets (Exhibit 1).

Copyright:
> [Short name] is the owner of the copyright in [the work] (the "Work"), created in [year] [and registered with the
> United States Copyright Office under Registration No. [number], effective [date]] (Exhibit 1).

Contract:
> [Short name] and [Recipient] are parties to the [agreement title] dated [date] (the "Agreement"). Section [n] of
> the Agreement provides: "[exact quoted words]".

Non-solicit:
> Under Section [n] of the [agreement title] dated [date] (the "Agreement"), [Recipient] agreed that, for [period]
> after [trigger], [he/she/it] would not "[exact quoted words]". That period runs until [date].

Defamation (there is no "right" paragraph; go straight to the statements):
> On [date], [Recipient] published the following statements at [where] (Exhibit 1): "[exact words]".

### The conduct
> On [date], [Short name] became aware that [Recipient] is [act] at [location] (Exhibit 2). [The earliest record of
> this conduct known to [Short name] is dated [date].]

Add only what the exhibits show. For a contract: each failure with its date and the document that shows it. For a
non-solicit: each approach, with who, when and how.

### Why it is unlawful

One short paragraph. Pick the one that applies and adapt it to the facts.

- Trademark: "[Recipient]'s use of [their name] for [their goods] is likely to cause buyers to believe that those
  goods come from, or are approved by, [Short name]. That use infringes [Short name]'s rights in [MARK] under the
  Lanham Act, 15 U.S.C. sections 1114 and 1125(a)." Use section 1114 only for a registered mark.
- Copyright: "[Recipient] has reproduced and displayed [what] from the Work without [Short name]'s permission. That
  infringes [Short name]'s exclusive rights under 17 U.S.C. section 106."
- Contract: "[Recipient]'s [act or failure] is a breach of Section [n] of the Agreement."
- Non-solicit: "[Recipient]'s contact with [customer or person] on [date] is a breach of Section [n] of the
  Agreement."
- Defamation: "Each of these statements is a statement of fact, and each is false. [Statement 1] is false because
  [fact, with exhibit]. [Statement 2] is false because [fact, with exhibit]."

Read the cited section at its source before it goes in. If it was not read today, write the sentence without the
citation.

### Demands
> [Short name] demands that, no later than [date], [Recipient]:
>
> (a) cease [the specific act];
> (b) remove [the specific items, listed or by exhibit];
> (c) [for a contract: cure the breach by [specific act]] [for defamation: remove the statements identified above
>     and publish no further statement to the same effect];
> (d) confirm in writing to [signer's name] at [email] that each of the above has been done.

Optional, only when supported and approved: an accounting of units sold and revenue; delivery or destruction of
infringing stock; transfer of a domain or account name; payment of a stated sum with the arithmetic shown.

For a contract with a cure period:
> This letter is notice of breach under Section [n] of the Agreement. [Recipient] has [number] days from
> [receipt / the date of this letter, as the clause says] to cure, that is, until [date].

### Preservation
> [Recipient] must preserve all documents, communications and data relating to the matters in this letter,
> including [sales records, listings, drafts, messages], and must suspend any routine deletion that would affect
> them.

### Consequence

Pick the line that matches the owner's standing answer. Never stronger.

- Not decided (the default): "If [Recipient] does not comply by that date, [Short name] will consider all available
  remedies."
- Decided to act: "If [Recipient] does not comply by that date, [Short name] will take the steps necessary to
  protect its rights, which may include [a report to the platform / proceedings in a court of competent jurisdiction
  / arbitration under Section [n] of the Agreement], without further notice."

Do not write "we will sue" unless a lawyer is engaged and the approver has said so.

### Reservation of rights
> This letter is not a complete statement of [Short name]'s rights or of the facts. Nothing in it waives or limits
> any right, remedy, claim or defense available to [Short name], all of which are expressly reserved.

## Variations

- **To a lawyer:** address counsel, name their client in the Re line, and keep the same content.
- **The softer first contact** (innocent small user, a relationship worth keeping, or sue-first risk above low):
  same facts and the same request, as a short letter with no legal conclusion ("we believe this is likely to confuse
  buyers" instead of "this infringes"), an offer to talk, and the reservation of rights. It still needs the gate
  and the OK.
- **Retraction demand under a state statute:** follow the statute's own list of contents and its deadline exactly,
  and say on the matter page which statute and which version was read.
