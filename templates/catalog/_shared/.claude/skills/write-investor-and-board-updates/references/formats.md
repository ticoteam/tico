# Formats

Four formats. Pick by who reads it and what they must do with it.

| Format | Readers | When | Length | They do |
|--------|---------|------|--------|---------|
| Monthly investor email | All investors | Early company; raising inside 12 months; under about 18 months of runway | Under 350 words plus the table | Help with an ask |
| Quarterly letter | All investors | Steady company; or what the agreements require | One to two pages | Stay informed; help |
| Board pack | Directors and observers | Before each board meeting | 8 to 15 pages plus appendix | Decide |
| Same-day note | Board first, then investors | Material news between updates | Under 200 words | Know before they hear elsewhere |

Rules for picking:

1. What the investor agreements require is the floor. If they say quarterly financials within a number of days, that
   is delivered whatever else is chosen.
2. More often and shorter beats less often and longer while the company is young or the news is moving.
3. Once a cadence is chosen, keep it. Going quiet reads as trouble.
4. One update serves everyone at the same level. Do not write different numbers for different investors.

## Monthly investor email

```text
Subject: <Company> investor update, <Month Year>

<Month> in one line: <the most important true thing, good or bad>.

<Numbers table: same 6-8 rows every month; this month, last month, change>
<One line under the table for any "not supplied", restated or preliminary figure.>

Asks
1. <What, from whom, what a yes looks like>
<Thanks, by name, for help received since last time.>

Lowlights
- <What happened, with the number. Why. What is being done, by whom, by when.>

Highlights
- <Fact with a number or a named event and date.>

Last time we said
- <Promise>: done / not done, <why in a clause>
By next update
- <2-3 things that are already tasks with owners>

<Name>
```

Notes:

- The summary line carries the bad news if a lowlight moved a number in the table.
- Asks sit directly under the numbers so a reader who stops there has seen them.
- Plain text or very simple HTML. No images, no attachments, no tracking links. Sent from a real person's mailbox so a
  reply reaches them.
- Send within ten business days of the books closing, on the same day of the month each time.

## Quarterly letter

The monthly email's sections, plus:

- **Numbers against plan.** The quarter's actuals beside the plan and beside the same quarter last year, from
  `tico finance report budget_vs_actual`. The three largest differences explained.
- **Trends.** Small charts of cash, revenue and the North Star over the last 12 months.
- **What we learned.** One short paragraph on what the quarter changed in the team's thinking, tied to a strategy bet
  (`tico strategy show`).
- **Next quarter.** The three to five goals, each with its key result.
- **Runway and funding.** Months of runway on current burn and on plan, and whether and when the company expects to
  raise. No terms.

Send within 30 days of the quarter's close unless an agreement says sooner. If the agreements call for financial
statements, attach the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow as exported from the books and say whether they
are unaudited.

## Board pack

A board meeting is for decisions and for the directors' judgment on hard questions. The pack exists so that the
meeting is not spent presenting.

Send it as a pre-read page at least three working days ahead (the settings may change this). Structure:

1. **Decisions needed.** Each item the board must vote on or approve, first, as a one-line resolution with a link to
   its detail in the appendix: minutes of the last meeting, option grants, a budget, a financing, a key hire or
   departure. The Corporate Secretary bot or counsel drafts resolutions and minutes; this skill lists them.
2. **Summary from the chief executive.** One page: what went well, what did not, what keeps them up at night, and the
   one or two things they want the board's help thinking through. Drafted from the owner's own notes and asked for in
   their words; do not invent the owner's worries.
3. **Numbers against plan.** The fixed table extended to plan and prior year, the income statement, cash and runway,
   and the three to five operating KPIs. Differences over the Finance Owner's limit explained.
4. **Lowlights and risks.** What is off, why, what is being done. The top risks with what would trigger each.
5. **Discussion topics.** One or two real questions where the board's experience helps, each as a one-page decision
   frame: the question, the options, the evidence, the recommendation.
6. **Asks of the board.** Specific introductions, advice, approvals.
7. **Appendix.** Detailed financials, the customer pipeline, team changes, the cap table summary if the board has
   asked for it, and the last meeting's action items scored done or not done.

Rules:

- The pack states what has changed since the last one. Do not make directors hunt for it.
- Every page answers "so what?". Cut pages that only show activity.
- The same KPIs in the same order every meeting.
- Legal advice and anything about a dispute are sent separately by counsel.
- After the meeting: action items become tasks with owners; draft minutes are the Corporate Secretary's skill
  `draft-minutes-from-a-meeting`.
- Suggested time in the meeting: decisions and consent items briefly, most of the time on the discussion topics, and
  a closed session for directors at the end. The chair sets the agenda.

## Same-day note

For material news between updates. Board first, then all investors, the same day where possible.

```text
Subject: <Company>: <the event in plain words>

What happened: <facts only, with date and numbers>.
What it means: <the effect on customers, cash or plan, as far as known>. What we do not know yet: <...>.
What we are doing: <actions, owners, dates>.
What we need from you: <a specific ask, or "nothing now">.
Next word from us: <date>.
```

No speculation about cause or blame. No legal conclusions. If it concerns a claim, a security incident, a regulator or
anyone's employment, a lawyer reads it before the OK.

## Lender updates

A loan agreement usually fixes what is delivered and when: financial statements, a compliance certificate, covenant
calculations. Deliver exactly that, computed exactly as the agreement defines each term, and have the Finance Owner
confirm each covenant figure. A covenant close to its limit is a same-day matter for the owner, before the lender
hears.
