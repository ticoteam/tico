# Sources

All text in this skill is original. Ideas were drawn from the sources below. Read 2026-11-18 unless dated otherwise.
The project's research notes held nothing on pricing, so this was researched for the skill.

## Adapted ideas (MIT)

- coreyhaines31, `marketingskills`, `pricing` skill (MIT). https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills
  Taken: three separate decisions (packaging, metric, price point); the price sits between the next best choice and
  the value perceived; test on new customers first; keeping old prices as a bridge with an end, not forever; raising
  existing customers in waves; prices in the page's text so AI assistants can read them.

## Books and articles (ideas and facts; no wording beyond short quoted terms)

- Madhavan Ramanujam and Georg Tacke, "Monetizing Innovation" (Wiley, 2016). Read through summaries and reviews in
  search results; the book was not opened for this skill. Taken: have the willingness-to-pay conversation before
  building; group customers by what they would pay; sort features into must-have, nice-to-have and unwanted per group;
  how you charge matters more than how much; one stated aim (maximise, win share, premium); the four ways pricing
  fails; hold the price and control discounts.
- Patrick Campbell (ProfitWell), "Pricing your SaaS product", Lenny's Newsletter, 2020-10-27.
  https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/saas-pricing-strategy
  Taken: choose the value metric first; test several candidates and keep one or two; larger customers should have more
  of the metric; per seat only where each user gets their own value; keep discounts at or under about 20%; annual
  offers as fixed free months; local currency; revisit pricing often; a free plan is an acquisition cost, not a tier.
  The figures in that article are the author's company's own data and were not independently checked.
- Kyle Poyar, Growth Unhinged, "2025 SaaS Benchmarks Report" (2025-11-12)
  https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/2025-saas-benchmarks-report and "State of B2B Monetization" (2026), the second read
  through summaries only. Taken: the share of companies with mixed and outcome-based pricing, dated; uneven AI cost
  across users; the four conditions for outcome pricing.
- Rafi Mohammed, "The Good-Better-Best Approach to Pricing", Harvard Business Review, September-October 2018. Read
  through summaries. Taken: fences between plans; the gap rule of thumb, cited only as one of several that conflict.
- Simon-Kucher, published guidance on price increases, read through search results. Taken: differ the rise by
  customer; close legacy gaps over time; lead with value; prepare sales and support; estimate losses first; watch
  daily.
- Peter Van Westendorp (1976) and the Gabor-Granger method, through survey-method guides in search results. Taken:
  what each survey method answers and does not.
- James Murphy and others, "A Meta-Analysis of Hypothetical Bias in Stated Preference Valuation" (2005), as cited in
  Tico's `pricing-analyst/knowledge/pricing-practice.md`. Taken: stated willingness to pay runs above real payment,
  median ratio about 1.35.
