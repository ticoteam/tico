# Lenses for step 4 (Diverge)

Use these when the option list stalls. Each lens is one question. Write the option it gives you in one line and move on. Do not work through all of them: three lenses that produce new options are enough.

## Take away

- **Delete the step.** Which step causes the problem, and what breaks if it is gone?
- **Delete the choice.** Which setting or option forces the customer to decide something we could decide for them?
- **Change the default.** Would the problem vanish if the default were the other way round?
- **Merge two things.** Which two screens, steps or roles are really one?

## Change who does the work

- **A bot does it.** What if the customer never did this task at all, and only checked the result?
- **The other side does it.** Could the person on the receiving end (the customer's customer, the admin, the payer) do the step instead?
- **Do it earlier.** Could this be settled at setup, once, instead of every time?
- **Do it later.** Could this wait until the moment it matters, and be skipped when it never does?

## Change the size

- **One screen.** What is the version that fits on one screen with one button?
- **One customer.** What would you build if it only had to work for the one account that complained loudest? What does that teach?
- **Ten times.** What would have to be true for this to be ten times better, not ten percent? Is any part of that cheap?
- **One day.** What could ship tomorrow that solves a third of it?

## Change the angle

- **The opposite.** State the obvious solution, then its reverse. Is the reverse absurd, or just unusual?
- **Make it worse.** List five ways to make the problem worse, then reverse each.
- **Another trade.** Who solved this shape of problem in a different industry, and how?
- **Each kind of user.** How would a first-day customer solve it? A five-year customer? An admin? Someone about to cancel?
- **Is it knowing, doing or wanting?** Do they not know they can, find it too hard, or not care enough? Each has a different fix.

## Reframe the problem

- **How might we.** Rewrite the frame as "How might we <result> for <who> without <cost>?" Too wide ("improve invoicing") gives nothing; too narrow ("add a button") is already a solution. Write three versions and brainstorm against the one that surprised you.
- **What did they hire instead?** What do customers use today in place of the missing thing: a spreadsheet, a notebook, a person? The option may be to make that workaround unnecessary, or to make it official.
- **What would have to be true** for this problem not to exist?

## Traps to name when you see them

- **Solution first.** The task says "build X". Find the problem; keep X as option A.
- **They have it, so we need it.** A competitor's feature is a clue about a need, not a need.
- **One idea in disguise.** Seven options that are the same idea at seven sizes. Add options that differ in kind.
- **Constraints too early.** "We can't because..." belongs in step 5, not step 4.
- **Circling.** The same question keeps coming back because nobody knows a fact. Stop, name the fact, and go and get it (`learn-from-customers`, or a query).
