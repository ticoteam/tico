## Crisis practice

- US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, Crisis and Emergency Risk Communication (CERC) manual. Six
  principles: be first, be right, be credible, express empathy, promote action, show respect. The basis for the
  statement order (facts, concern, action, what to do, next update).
- W. Timothy Coombs, Situational Crisis Communication Theory (2007 and later). The response should match how much
  responsibility the public assigns to the organisation. Used for the severity levels. Idea only.
- The 60-minute default for a first statement is common practice in crisis guides, not a published standard.

## Law referred to (flag to counsel; check the current text)

- EU General Data Protection Regulation, Article 33: notify the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where
  feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach. The UK GDPR has the same rule.
- US Securities and Exchange Commission, Form 8-K Item 1.05 (rules adopted 2023): a public company reports a material
  cybersecurity incident within four business days of deciding it is material.
- US state data-breach notification laws: every state has one; deadlines and content differ. Not checked state by
  state.
- Attorney-client privilege and public relations firms: US courts have split on whether communications with a PR
  firm are protected (for example, In re Grand Jury Subpoenas dated March 24, 2003, S.D.N.Y., protected some;
  Calvin Klein Trademark Trust v. Wachner, S.D.N.Y. 2000, did not). Read from secondary summaries: counsel decides.
- US state "apology laws": many states make expressions of sympathy inadmissible as evidence of liability, fewer
  protect admissions of fault, and many apply only to medical or accident cases. Not verified state by state.
- Duty to preserve records once litigation is reasonably anticipated (US federal case law and Federal Rule of Civil
  Procedure 37(e)). Summary only.

## Press practice

- Muck Rack, State of Journalism 2026, and Cision, State of the Media 2025, as recorded in Tico's `pr-method.md` and
  secondary coverage: relevance to the reporter's beat is the main reason pitches are rejected; one-to-one email is
  the preferred channel; one follow-up is the accepted limit.
- Propel, PR pitching benchmark reports: reply rates in the low single digits per hundred pitches. Read second-hand.
