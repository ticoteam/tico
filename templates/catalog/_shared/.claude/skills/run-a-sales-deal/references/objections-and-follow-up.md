# First replies, follow-up, objections and negotiation

All sample wording here is invented. Write fresh words for the buyer in front of you.

## The first reply

Speed matters most here: a buyer who wrote in is usually writing to others too. Reply inside the team's reply time
(default one hour, around the clock).

Shape:

1. **Answer what they asked,** in the first sentence. If they asked the price and it is on the list, give it.
2. **One sentence that shows you read their message,** tied to their situation, not a compliment.
3. **One question or one ask.** Either the single thing you most need to know, or two real times to talk.
4. **Sign-off** as the team's AI teammate.

Limits: 120 words, one ask, one link at most, no attachments on a first reply, no feature list, no "I'd love to".

If they can buy without talking to anyone, say how in the same reply. Many buyers prefer it, and a forced call loses
them.

If the answer to their question is no (the product does not do it, they are outside the fit rule), say so in the
first sentence and, where you can, point them somewhere useful. That reply takes one minute and is remembered.

Offered times: at least two, on working days, inside working hours in their time zone, with the zone named, read
from `tico calendar free`. Create the invite with `tico calendar schedule` when they pick. Never write "confirmed"
before the invite exists.

## The recap

After any call or substantial exchange, within the hour, under 120 words:

- what you heard, in their words (the problem, what it costs, the date);
- what was agreed;
- who does what by when;
- anything you promised to find out, and when you will have it.

Ask them to correct anything wrong. The recap is the record both sides rely on later.

## Follow-up

**The rule: every touch gives them something new.** Before writing, name what this message adds. If the answer is
nothing, do not send it; go and get something first.

Things worth sending:

- the answer to a question left open;
- a number for their business case;
- a short account of how a similar customer handled the same step, if the team may tell it;
- a fix or workaround for a concern they raised;
- the next row of the mutual action plan, with what you have already done toward it;
- a relevant change: a release, a price-list date, a slot opening up.

Never: "just checking in", "bumping this", "circling back", "any update?", a guilt trip, a false deadline, or a
"last chance" that is not one.

**Default cadence when the buyer has gone quiet** (starting defaults for this library; the team's replace them):

| Touch | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 working days after your last message | Something new, one ask |
| 2 | 5 working days later | Something new, a different angle, one ask |
| 3 | 7 working days later | Try the other contact, or a different channel they have used with you |
| 4 | 10 working days later | The closing note |

The closing note is honest and short: you have not heard back, you assume the timing is wrong, you will stop
writing, and here is how to pick it up again. Then stop. Mark the deal Nurture or Lost with the reason "went quiet
after <stage>" and set one reminder tied to their critical event if they had one.

An existing thread with a real next step keeps the mutual action plan's dates instead of this table.

Stop at once when they say no, ask you to stop, or unsubscribe. Record it the same day.

Use `run-a-follow-up-cadence` to keep the dates in `knowledge/follow-ups.md`.

## Objections

An objection is information about what the buyer still needs. Handle it in four moves:

1. **Say it back** in your own words, to check you understood.
2. **Ask what is behind it.** "Too expensive" may mean compared with a competitor, compared with the budget,
   compared with the value they have seen so far, or "I need something to tell my boss".
3. **Sort it:**

| Kind | It sounds like | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| Not understood | "I don't see how this handles X" | Show it, briefly, on their case |
| Not believed | "Everyone says that" | Evidence: their own pilot, a reference who agreed, a document |
| Not worth it | "It's a lot for what it does" | Return to Impact. If the cost of the problem is smaller than the price, they are right |
| Not now | "Maybe next quarter" | Return to the Critical Event. If there is none, nurture |
| Not their call | "I'd have to run it by..." | Return to Decision. Help them make the case; meet the decider |
| Risk | "What if it doesn't work for us?" | Lower the risk: a pilot with a pass mark, a shorter first term if the list allows |
| A real gap | "We need it to do Y" and it does not | Say so. Check feasibility before saying anything about the future |

4. **Answer with evidence, then check:** "Does that deal with it, or is there more to it?"

Do not argue, do not answer an objection they have not raised, and do not treat a real gap as a misunderstanding.
An objection you cannot answer truthfully is a reason to disqualify or to ask a teammate, not to improvise.

Record each objection and what resolved it in the deal file. Patterns go to the sales-enablement skills
(`check-a-talk-track-against-calls`).

## Negotiation

- **Know your limits before the conversation:** the list price, the standard terms, and what you may offer without
  an OK (by default, nothing beyond the list).
- **Find out what they need changed and why** before responding to any of it. Get the whole list first; negotiating
  item by item gives away more.
- **Never open with a discount,** and never offer one to speed things up. A discount given for nothing teaches the
  buyer that the price was not real.
- **Trade, do not give.** If something moves, something moves back: a longer term, payment up front, a faster
  signature, a case study, a smaller scope. Write down both halves.
- **Reduce scope before price.** A smaller package at list price keeps the price honest.
- **Anything off the list needs an OK** for exactly what will be offered, before it is offered. Ask with the deal
  facts, what they asked for, what you recommend trading, and what happens if the answer is no.
- **Their paper:** if the buyer sends their own contract or asks for changed clauses, run the review-a-contract
  skill and ask for an OK. Do not accept, reject or redline legal terms on your own.
- **Procurement:** answer their forms fully and fast, from sourced answers
  (`check-a-security-answer-against-its-source`). Ask what their process is and add each step to the mutual action
  plan.
- **Be willing to stop.** A deal that only closes at a price or on terms the team would not repeat is not a win.

## Rules for outreach that is not a reply

Replying to someone who wrote to you is one thing. Writing first is regulated. US defaults, with what to check
elsewhere, are in `sources.md`. In short: identify the sender truthfully, give a working way to opt out and honour
it promptly, do not call or text numbers without the consent the law requires, and check the rules of the
recipient's country before any cold email to people in the EU or UK. Prospecting itself is the outbound-sales-owner
skills' job.
