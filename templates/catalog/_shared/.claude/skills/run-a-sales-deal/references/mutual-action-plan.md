# The mutual action plan

A mutual action plan is one short list, agreed with the buyer, of every step from today to the day they are getting
value, with a date and an owner on each. It exists because most deals that die do not die from a "no"; they stall on
a step nobody was tracking, such as a security form or a signer on holiday.

It is the buyer's plan as much as yours. If they will not look at it, that tells you how real the deal is.

## When to make one

As soon as the buyer has said they want to go ahead in principle, and always before sending an order. For a small,
simple purchase that a buyer can complete alone at a checkout, skip it: three lines in an email are enough.

## Build it backward

1. **Start at the end.** The last line is not "contract signed". It is the result the buyer wants, on the date of
   their critical event: "Scheduling runs on the new system before the spring rush, 1 February."
2. **Work back to first value:** what must be true the week before that, and before that. Use the onboarding
   milestones from get-customers-to-their-outcome if the team has them.
3. **Then the purchase:** signature, and everything their organisation needs before a signature. Ask the champion to
   list their steps; they know them and you do not:
   - who must review (security, IT, legal, finance, procurement) and how long each usually takes;
   - whether a supplier form, a tax form or an insurance certificate is needed;
   - who signs, and whether they are away;
   - whether budget is approved or needs a request.
4. **Then what is left of evaluation:** a demo for the decision-maker, a pilot, a reference call.
5. **Check the arithmetic.** If the steps do not fit before the critical event, say so now and agree what gives:
   the date, the scope, or a step.

## Layout

Keep it to one table, in an email body or a short shared document.

| # | Step | Owner | By | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | <evaluation step> | Us / them: role | <date> | Done / open / at risk |
| 2 | <their review step> | Them: role | | |
| 3 | Order signed | Them: role | | |
| 4 | <setup step> | Us | | |
| 5 | <first value, as the customer would see it> | Both | | |
| 6 | <the result, on the critical event date> | Both | <critical event date> | |

Above the table, two lines: the goal in their words, and the date it all hangs on with the reason for that date.

Rules:

- **Six to ten rows.** More than that and nobody reads it.
- **Every row has one owner,** named by role, on one side.
- **Every row has a date.** "TBD" is a flag that the step is not understood yet.
- **Their steps are in it.** A plan with only your steps is a to-do list.
- **It goes past the signature** to value. That is what makes it theirs.
- **It is honest about risk.** Mark a row "at risk" the day you think so, and say why.

## Agreeing it

Send it as a draft and ask the champion to correct it: "Here is what I think needs to happen for you to be running
by <date>. What have I got wrong, and what is missing on your side?" Their edits are the point. A plan they changed
is a plan they own.

Share it in a form they can keep and forward: in the email body, or as an attached document. Tico pages are for
teammates and a buyer cannot open them, so keep the working copy in the deal record and send the buyer the table.

## Running it

- Update the status after every exchange and whenever a date passes.
- When a row slips, tell the buyer the same day what it does to the end date, and propose the fix. Do not quietly
  re-date.
- Use each open row as the reason for your next follow-up. "Row 2: has security had a chance to look? I can send the
  answers in the format they prefer."
- If two rows on their side slip with no reply, ask directly whether the date still matters. Sometimes the critical
  event moved; sometimes the deal is over and nobody said.
- When the order is signed, the remaining rows become the outcome plan's milestones. Hand the table over as it is.

## Reading it as a forecast

A deal's real stage is the last row done, not how the buyer sounded on the last call.

- **Commit** means: every row before "order signed" is done or has a date inside the period, the signer is known and
  has seen the proposal, and the buyer has edited or confirmed the plan.
- A deal with no agreed plan is not in Commit, whatever its size.
- Put the critical event date and the next open row on the weekly pipeline page for every deal past Discovery.
