# Moments that matter

Customers do not judge every step equally. A handful of moments decide whether they stay and what they tell other
people: the first time it works, the first time money moves, the first time something goes wrong, and the end. Pick
those, promise something specific at each, measure it, and start recovery from the measure rather than from a
complaint.

## 1. Find the moments from evidence

Do not brainstorm the list. Read:

- **The recovery log and support categories:** which steps produce the most complaints and the costliest fixes.
- **Cancellation reasons** (coded with `code-a-cancellation-reason`): which step do leavers name?
- **Survey comments and reviews:** which step do the happiest and the unhappiest customers each talk about?
- **The journey itself:** walk it as a customer in the Tools, from first visit to renewal or reorder to leaving.

Mark a step as a moment that matters when at least one is true:

- it is a first (first use, first delivery, first bill, first support contact);
- money moves (a charge, a renewal, a refund, a price change);
- the customer has a deadline of their own riding on it;
- the customer is exposed in front of others (their own customers, their boss, a guest);
- a failure here is hard to undo;
- it is the last thing they experience (the end of a project, a cancellation). People remember endings.

Keep five to ten. With more than ten nobody watches any of them.

## 2. Write each one down

Save this table in `knowledge/recovery.md`:

| Moment | Promise, in the customer's words | Measure that shows it was kept | Source of the measure | Trigger that starts recovery | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules for the columns:

- **Promise:** something a customer would say. "It arrives the day you said", not "on-time delivery rate".
- **Measure:** observable per customer, from a Tool, without asking them. If it can only be known by asking, the
  measure is a one-question survey at that moment.
- **Trigger:** the condition that makes you act before they write. "Shipment has no scan for 48 hours." "No login in
  the 7 days after sign-up." "Second ticket on the same issue."
- **Owner:** the teammate who can change the cause, not the one who apologises.

If a moment has no measure you can read today, write "no measure yet", file a task to get one, and do not report it
as kept.

## 3. Starting list to adapt

Examples of moments by kind of business. Use them to check your evidence-based list for gaps, not as the list.

- **Subscription software:** sign-up to first useful result; the first invoice; the first support contact; a
  release that changes something they use; renewal notice; cancellation.
- **Online shop:** order confirmation; the promised delivery day; opening the parcel; a return; the refund landing;
  a reorder.
- **Services and bookings:** the first reply to an enquiry; the booking confirmation; arrival on the day; the work
  itself; the invoice; the follow-up afterwards.
- **Marketplaces and platforms:** onboarding each side; the first match or sale; the first payout; a dispute.

## 4. Watch them

- Daily, or as often as the Tool allows: check each trigger. Each hit starts recovery at step 1 of SKILL.md, before
  the customer writes.
- Weekly: for each moment, count kept and broken. Show them on the recovery page as a row of cells, one per moment.
  Counts first; a percentage only when the base is 20 or more.
- If a trigger could not be checked (Tool down, no access), the cell says "not read". Never show it as kept.

## 5. Change the list

Every quarter, or when the product changes:

- A moment with no failures for two quarters and no mentions in comments can leave the list.
- A step that shows up three times in the recovery log and is not on the list joins it.
- A promise the team keeps missing is either fixed or changed to one it can keep. Do not keep promising what the
  team cannot deliver.

## 6. Make the good moments on purpose

Recovery is the defensive half. For one or two moments, plan something that makes the moment better than expected at
little cost: the order arrives a day early with a line about how to care for it; the first invoice comes with a plain
summary of what they got; on the day a customer reaches the result they came for, they hear about it from you. Keep
these inside the gesture menu and budget, and test whether customers notice before making them routine.
