# Sources

All text in this skill is original. The ideas below were adapted; no wording was copied. Read 2026-11-18.

## Adapted (Apache-2.0)

- **Anthropic, `knowledge-work-plugins`, product-management, `synthesize-research`** (Apache-2.0). https://github.com/anthropics/knowledge-work-plugins/blob/main/product-management/skills/synthesize-research/SKILL.md
  Taken: a quote is evidence and not yet a finding; each finding carries its evidence, how many sources support it and a confidence level; separate what people did from what they said; check one kind of source against another. Not taken: personas and opportunity sizing.

## Ideas only (not adaptable text)

- **Rob Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test** (book). https://www.momtestbook.com/
  Ideas: ask about past behaviour, not hypotheticals; compliments and wish lists are not data; never pitch in a discovery conversation.
- **Teresa Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits** (book, 2021), and https://www.producttalk.org/
  Ideas: interview as a habit, not a project; collect one specific story per interview; write a one-page snapshot straight after. Known through secondary summaries; the book was not read for this skill.
- **RefoundAI, `lenny-skills`, `customer-interviews` and `analyzing-user-feedback`**. https://github.com/refoundai/lenny-skills
  Ideas only, as summarised in `research/product.md`: choose the problem space before recruiting; compare a theme's share of the noise with its segment's share of customers before ranking it. No wording used.
- **Dean Peters, `discovery-interview-prep`** (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). https://github.com/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills/blob/main/skills/discovery-interview-prep/SKILL.md
  Idea only: keep a list of interviewer habits to avoid (leading, yes/no questions, pitching) on the guide itself. No wording used.
- **Bob Moesta and others, switch interviews** (the buying story from first thought to decision). Named in `pick-the-method.md` as a method; no text or question list used.

## Facts that can change

- **Interview sample size.** Jakob Nielsen, "Why You Only Need to Test with 5 Users" (Nielsen Norman Group, 2000), is about usability tests. Guest, Bunce and Johnson, "How Many Interviews Are Enough?" (Field Methods, 2006), is cited here from secondary summaries; the paper was not opened. This skill's stopping rule does not rest on either.
- **Recording consent, United States** (web-checked 2026-11-18): federal one-party consent under 18 U.S.C. § 2511; a minority of states require all parties' consent, and published lists differ on a few states. The sources found were law-firm and vendor summaries, not the statutes. The skill therefore always asks everyone, on the record. Check current state law, and the law where a non-US participant is, before relying on anything narrower.
- **The Sean Ellis test** (40% "very disappointed"): a practitioner rule of thumb, not a validated threshold.

## Defaults that are this skill's own

"Five per segment", "stop after two that add nothing", "twelve without an answer means the questions are wrong", "one research request per customer in 90 days", "a new theme needs three lines from different accounts", "over half from three accounts is the loud few", "a finding is stale after 180 days" and "fewer than about 30 answers: report counts" are working defaults, not published standards. The pattern threshold (three distinct accounts, two kinds of source) matches the Feature Owner template's ranking rule. A team changes them in `knowledge/product.md`.
