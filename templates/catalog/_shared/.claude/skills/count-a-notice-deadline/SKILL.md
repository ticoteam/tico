# Count a notice deadline

When to use: whenever you state a notice deadline, a renewal window, a decide-by date or the last day to give notice, in
a summary, a calendar line, a register row or a task.

Steps:
1. Read the dates from the signed contract or order form: the end or renewal date, the notice period, and whether it
   renews automatically and for how long. Note the page and clause for each. Where the order form and the contract
   disagree, record both with their pages and ask the Contracts Manager (`contracts-manager`) which governs; never pick
   one silently. A date seen only on an invoice or a dashboard is marked "to confirm".
2. Name the anchor the clause uses: "before the end of the term", "before the renewal date", "after receipt of notice", or
   "from the date of the notice". Quote the words.
3. Name the period and its unit: calendar days, business days or months. Note whether the clause says "at least", "not less
   than" or "no later than". For a period in months, say which day of the month is used.
4. Name the delivery rule: when notice counts as given (sent, received or deemed received), to whom, and in what form
   (email, letter, portal). If it must be received, the deadline is the day it must arrive.
5. Count from the anchor in the direction the clause gives: back for "before" anchors, forward for "after receipt" or "from
   the date of the notice". Write the arithmetic, for example "2027-03-31 minus 60 calendar days = 2027-01-30" or
   "2026-11-01 plus 30 calendar days = 2026-12-01".
6. If the last day is a weekend or holiday, apply the contract's own rule. If it has none, use the earlier date and say so.
7. Compare with today. If the deadline has passed, the contract has usually renewed: say so plainly, with the new end
   date if the clause gives it, or "not on file". Do not count a deadline for a term that has already started.
8. Show two dates: the last day to give notice, and an action date (for planning) with the days left. In a register,
   mark each contract **open now** (inside the window, default 90 days), **urgent** (inside 30 days) or **passed**, and
   sort by deadline, not end date.
9. Name the owner who decides and the question they must answer by the date (keep, reduce or drop).
10. If the clause is silent on the anchor or the unit, flag it for counsel and show the deadline both ways.

Checks before you finish:
- The anchor, the unit and the dates are quoted from the clause, with the page.
- The arithmetic is written out.
- The delivery rule is stated, with whether notice must be received or only sent.
- The weekend rule is stated, or its absence is said.
- Both the last day and the action date are shown, with days left.
- A passed deadline says so and shows the new term, or "not on file".
- Any ambiguity is flagged, not resolved by guessing.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Clause 9.2: notice 'no later than 60 days before the renewal date', calendar days, in writing
to the supplier's notices address. Renewal date 2027-03-31. 2027-03-31 minus 60 days = 2027-01-30, which is a Saturday. The
contract has no weekend rule, so the last day used is the earlier one, Friday 2027-01-29, and this is flagged. Action date
2027-01-15, 14 days before the last day used. Owner: the design lead. Question: keep, reduce seats or drop, by 2027-01-15."
