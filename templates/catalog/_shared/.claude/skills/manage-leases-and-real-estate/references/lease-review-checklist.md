# Lease review checklist

For each item: find the clause, quote it, say what it costs or risks, and say what to ask for. An item the draft
does not address is listed as "silent", which is a finding. Benchmarks marked "commonly" are negotiating
observations from law-firm and broker articles read on 2026-11-18, not rules; the lawyer and broker know the local
market.

## 1. Parties, premises and area

- The landlord's legal name matches the owner on the public property record. The tenant is the company, not a
  person.
- The premises are identified by a plan, not only an address.
- **Area.** Rent is charged on rentable area, which includes a share of common space. Ask for rentable area, usable
  area, and the measurement standard used (the lease should name it, usually a BOMA standard). The ratio of rentable
  to usable is the load factor. Compare buildings on usable area. Ask for the right to remeasure, with rent and
  every area-based charge adjusted if the figure is wrong.

## 2. Term and dates

- Commencement date, rent commencement date and expiry date, and what each depends on (delivery of the space,
  completion of landlord's work, a permit). A date that floats needs a signed commencement letter fixing it.
- What happens if the landlord delivers late: free rent, and a right to walk away after a stated delay.
- Shortest term that works, with options to extend, beats a long term. Ask for an early termination right, and
  price it.

## 3. Rent and increases

- Base rent by year. Escalations: fixed percentage, fixed steps, or an index. For an index, ask for a ceiling.
- Free rent and other concessions, and whether they are clawed back on default.
- Late charges, interest and grace periods.

## 4. Operating expenses and other additional rent

- What kind of lease it is: gross (landlord bears building costs), net or triple net (tenant pays its share of
  taxes, insurance and maintenance), or a base year (tenant pays increases over a named year).
- The tenant's share: its rentable area over the building's, and how the building figure is fixed.
- **Exclusions.** Ask that capital improvements, structural repairs, the landlord's financing, leasing and legal
  costs, costs reimbursed by insurance or other tenants, and management fees above a stated percentage are out.
- **A cap** on yearly increases in controllable expenses. Commonly negotiated at 3 to 5 percent a year.
- **Gross-up.** If expenses are grossed up to a full building, the base year must be grossed up the same way, or the
  tenant pays for other tenants' vacancies.
- **Audit right.** The right to inspect the landlord's books within a stated period after each yearly statement,
  with a refund of overcharges and the audit cost if the error passes a stated size. This right generally exists
  only if the lease grants it.
- For a base-year lease, the base year itself is the number to examine.

**Reviewing a yearly statement:** compare each line with last year's and with the estimate; check the tenant's
share against the abstract; check excluded categories are absent; check the cap and gross-up were applied; ask for
backup on any line that rose more than the cap or more than 10 percent. Note the last day to object.

## 5. Money at risk beyond rent

- **Security deposit**: amount, whether it reduces over time, when it is returned, interest.
- **Personal guarantee.** Ask for none. If one is unavoidable: capped at a number of months' rent, falling away
  after a period of on-time payment, and ending when the company hands the space back.
- **Tenant improvements**: who builds, who pays, the allowance, who owns the work, and whether it must be removed at
  the end.
- **Restoration and surrender**: the condition the space must be returned in. Ask that ordinary wear is accepted
  and that the landlord says at approval whether any alteration must be removed.
- **Holdover**: the rent charged if the company stays past expiry. Commonly 150 percent of the last rent, sometimes
  more, plus damages. Ask for a lower rate and no damages for a short overstay.

## 6. Use and running the space

- **Permitted use** covers everything the company will do, including storage, light assembly, visitors, and any
  hazardous materials. Check zoning and the certificate of occupancy.
- Hours of access and of heating and cooling, and the charge for after-hours service.
- Who repairs what. Structural elements, roof and building systems should be the landlord's.
- Compliance with laws: who pays for changes the law requires to the building. Accessibility duties to the public
  fall on both landlord and tenant under federal law; the lease only divides the cost.
- Signage, parking, internet providers, rules and regulations (attached, and changes must be reasonable).

## 7. Flexibility

- **Assignment and sublease.** Consent not to be unreasonably withheld or delayed. No landlord right to take the
  space back (recapture) or to keep all sublease profit. A change in ownership of the company, a financing or a
  merger should not count as an assignment.
- **Renewal option.** The window, the method of notice, how renewal rent is set (a fixed figure or a market process
  with an independent appraiser), and conditions. Options often require that the tenant is not in default when
  notice is given and when the new term starts; ask that only an uncured default after notice counts.
- Expansion rights, and a right to terminate early for a fee.
- Relocation: the landlord's right to move the tenant. Ask to delete it, or for comparable space at the landlord's
  cost.

## 8. Risk

- **Insurance** the tenant must carry, limits, additional insureds, waiver of subrogation. Send the clause to
  `manage-insurance` before signature.
- Indemnities: mutual, and limited to each side's own negligence.
- **Default**: written notice and a cure period for money and non-money defaults before any remedy.
- Casualty and condemnation: rent stops while the space is unusable; a right to terminate if not restored within a
  stated time.
- **Subordination**: the lease ranks behind the landlord's lender. Ask for a non-disturbance agreement so the lease
  survives a foreclosure.
- **Estoppel certificates**: the deadline to return one, and what happens on silence.
- **Notices**: addresses, methods, and when a notice counts as given. This clause decides whether an option
  exercise is valid.
- Governing law and dispute resolution.

## 9. Before the lawyer sees it

Send the draft, the issues table, the whole-term cost, the letter of intent, and three questions at most. Ask the
lawyer for state-specific points: anything the state implies into commercial leases, taxes on the lease or on
rent, and local rules on deposits and guarantees.
