# Subleases, assignments and leaving

## Subletting the company's space to someone else

Do this when the company has more space than it uses and the lease has time left.

1. **Read the assignment and sublease clause.** Is the landlord's consent needed, and on what standard? Can the
   landlord take the space back instead (recapture)? Does the landlord share in any profit? What must be sent with
   the request, and how long does the landlord have to answer?
2. **Know what stays with the company.** Unless the landlord signs a release, the company remains fully liable for
   the rent and for everything the subtenant does. A sublease moves the use of the space, not the risk.
3. **Price it honestly.** Sublease rent is often below what the company pays. Add the broker's fee, any work to
   divide the space, and the months it will sit empty. Compare with the cost of doing nothing and with a negotiated
   surrender (paying the landlord to end the lease early).
4. **Check the subtenant** like a customer on credit: legal entity, what they will do there, financial standing, a
   deposit.
5. **Paper it** with the lawyer. The sublease ends at least a day before the lease does, binds the subtenant to
   the lease's rules, requires their insurance with the company and the landlord as additional insureds, and gives
   them nothing the company does not have.
6. **Get the landlord's consent in writing** before the subtenant moves in.
7. **Afterwards**: abstract the sublease as its own site; bill the subtenant through the book; collect their
   certificate of insurance; calendar its end.

An **assignment** hands the whole lease to another party. The company usually stays liable unless released. A sale
of the company, a merger or a change of owners can count as an assignment under some leases; check the clause before
any such deal and tell the owner early.

## Taking a sublease from another tenant

1. Read the lease above it (the master lease) in full, as well as the sublease. The company will be bound by both.
2. Confirm the landlord's written consent is a condition of the sublease.
3. Check the sublandlord is not in default and ask what happens if they stop paying: the company's right to stay
   depends on theirs. Ask the landlord to agree to recognise the sublease if the master lease ends; many will not,
   which is a risk to price.
4. Services, repairs and consents come through the sublandlord. Make sure the sublease obliges them to enforce the
   master lease for the company.
5. Furniture and equipment left in the space: list it, and say who owns it and who removes it.
6. Abstract it like any lease. A sublease longer than 12 months, or with a deposit, goes to the lawyer.

## Getting out early

Options, in the order to try them:

1. A termination right already in the lease: follow its notice and fee exactly.
2. A negotiated surrender: ask the landlord what payment would end the lease. Landlords agree more readily when
   they have a replacement tenant or rents have risen.
3. An assignment or sublease as above.
4. Never: just stop paying and leave. The rent stays owed, the guarantee is exposed, and what the landlord must do
   to reduce the loss depends on the state.

Each is a legal commitment and goes through the lawyer and the owner's OK.

## Moving out

Open the task 90 days before the company must be out.

1. **Read the surrender and alterations clauses.** List what must be removed (cabling, signage, fixtures, anything
   the company installed) and the condition required.
2. **Ask the landlord in writing** what they will require removed and whether they will walk the space in advance.
3. **Get quotes** for removal and repairs, and decide what is cheaper: doing the work, or agreeing a payment in
   place of it.
4. **Give any notice the lease requires** before leaving, and book the building's move-out slot and lifts.
5. **End or move the services**: utilities, internet, cleaning, security, deliveries, mail forwarding.
6. **Walk the space with the landlord on the last day.** Photograph every room with dates. Hand back keys and
   access cards against a signed receipt. Write down anything the landlord objects to.
7. **Ask for the deposit** in writing, citing the clause and the due date; calendar the date.
8. **Change the address everywhere**: the state of formation and every state registration, the IRS (it has a
   change-of-address form for businesses; confirm the current one on irs.gov), state tax and payroll accounts,
   banks, insurers, licences, customers' vendor records, the website and invoices. Tell `stay-on-top-of-taxes`,
   `run-payroll` and `manage-insurance`.
9. **Close the record**: final operating expense reconciliation (it can arrive a year later; keep the right to
   audit it), deposit received, abstract marked ended, policies updated, books told the lease has ended.

## Taking possession of new space

The mirror image, before the first day:

- Insurance in force and the certificate with the landlord.
- A dated photo record of the condition, sent to the landlord.
- The commencement letter signed, fixing the dates.
- Permits, a certificate of occupancy for the company's use, and any local business licence.
- Access, keys and after-hours contacts recorded in `knowledge/office.md`.
- Critical dates on the calendar the same day.
