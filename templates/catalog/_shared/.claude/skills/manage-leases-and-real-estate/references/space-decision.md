# Does the company need space at all?

Bots need none. People need less than they ask for. Goods need somewhere, but not necessarily the company's own
somewhere. Start at the top of each ladder and move down only when a named need cannot be met on the rung above.

## The need, written down

For each proposed use, answer from records:

- Who would use it, how many, how often? (Roster, calendar history, booking or badge data.)
- What happens today without it, and what breaks?
- Is it steady, seasonal or a one-off?
- Does it need a fixed address (a licence, a regulator, a customer contract, returns)?
- For goods: units and pallets on hand by month, inbound and outbound volume, special handling (temperature,
  batteries, hazardous goods), value.
- For a lab or workshop: power, ventilation, loading, noise, test equipment, materials stored.

A need that cannot be stated in numbers is not ready for a lease.

## Ladder for people

| Rung | What it is | Commitment | Fits when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. No space | Remote, with a legal address elsewhere | None | Nobody needs to be together on a schedule |
| 2. Address service | A registered agent for legal papers, and a mail service that scans | Monthly or yearly | The company needs a legal and mailing address only |
| 3. Rooms by the day | Meeting rooms or day passes booked when needed | Per booking | People meet a few times a month |
| 4. Flexible membership | Desks or a private room in a coworking space, under a licence or membership agreement | Usually 1 to 12 months | A few people work together most weeks |
| 5. Sublease | Another tenant's surplus space, often furnished | The rest of their term, or less | A steady team wants its own door at below-market cost and a short term |
| 6. Direct lease | The company's own lease with a landlord | Usually years | A steady need of several years, or space no one else offers (lab, retail) |

Notes that change the answer:

- A corporation or LLC must keep a registered agent in its state of formation and each state where it is
  registered to do business. That is a legal requirement separate from having an office; check the state's rule.
- Using an owner's or employee's home as the company address puts a home address on public records and can
  conflict with home zoning, a residential lease or home insurance. Avoid it by default.
- A membership agreement is usually a licence, not a lease: fewer rights, but easy to leave. Read the notice period,
  automatic renewal and price-increase terms; `check-a-renewal-deadline` covers them.
- A sublease is only as safe as the lease above it. See `sublease-and-exit.md`.

## Ladder for goods

| Rung | What it is | Commitment | Fits when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Ship from the maker | The manufacturer or supplier ships to customers | Per order | Low volume, simple products |
| 2. 3PL | A third-party warehouse stores, picks and ships for a fee per pallet and per order | Monthly, sometimes with minimums | Most small product companies |
| 3. Shared or flexible warehouse | Space by the pallet position or small unit, the company's own people work it | Months | The work needs the company's hands (kitting, testing, repairs) |
| 4. Leased warehouse | The company's own industrial lease | Years | Steady high volume, or special handling a 3PL will not do |

Before goods go anywhere:

- Stock in a state usually gives the company a sales tax connection with that state. Tell `stay-on-top-of-taxes`
  before the first pallet arrives.
- The warehouse's liability for the goods is limited by its contract, often to far less than their value. Tell
  `manage-insurance`, and get the warehouse's certificate of insurance.
- Batteries, chemicals and other regulated goods limit which buildings and operators can be used. Ask in writing.

## Comparing options

Put every option on one table for the same period (default 36 months, or the longest term on offer).

| Line | How to get it |
| --- | --- |
| Rent or fee | The quote or draft, with every stated increase applied |
| Operating expenses, taxes, insurance charged by the landlord | The landlord's current estimate, grown at a stated assumption |
| Utilities, internet, cleaning, security | Quotes, or the last tenant's figures if the landlord will give them |
| Fit-out and furniture, less any allowance | Quotes |
| Deposit | Cash tied up; show it separately |
| Moving in and restoring on the way out | Quotes; the restoration clause decides the second |
| Insurance the lease makes the company buy | From `manage-insurance` |
| **Total for the period** | Sum |
| **Cost per regular user per month** | Total divided by users and months |
| **Cost to leave at month 12 and month 24** | What the document says: remaining rent, a break fee, or nothing |

Use `price-the-whole-term` for the arithmetic. State every assumption beside the figure. Compare on usable area: ask
each landlord for both rentable and usable figures and the measurement standard, because the share of common space
loaded onto rentable area differs from building to building, so the same rate per rentable foot is not the same
price.

Then write the recommendation as: the need; the lowest rung that meets it; what it costs; what it costs to be
wrong (the exit cost); and the date the choice should be looked at again.
