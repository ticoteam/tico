# Check an affiliate for fraud

When to use: when an affiliate's referrals change sharply, when referred customers share a detail with the affiliate, when declared traffic does not match its referrals, or when a coupon appears somewhere it was not approved.

Steps:
1. Name the rule you are checking, from `knowledge/fraud-signals.md`: self-referral, coupon abuse, brand-term bidding, cookie stuffing, or repeated details across sign-ups. A check with no rule is not a flag.
2. Gather the evidence from the export and from public sources read on the date you check: the shared detail, the referral timing against the affiliate's own traffic, the coupon's page, the ad copy.
3. Write one innocent explanation for the pattern. Examples: a family member placed the order, a reviewer posted a code the team once allowed, a launch week brought a spike.
4. Name the check that separates the explanation from the fraud: a question to the affiliate through a person, a look at the account owner, a comparison with the affiliate's own analytics if it shares them.
5. Record the flag as open with the rule, the evidence, the explanation and the check. Do not remove the referral, withhold the commission or name the affiliate outside the team.
6. If the flag is strong, say so in one line for the owner, with the amount it puts at risk. The owner decides any hold.

Checks before you finish:
- The flag names one rule and its evidence with dates.
- An innocent explanation is written beside it.
- No affiliate was named outside the team, and no commission was held or removed by this run.
- Anything read from a public page is dated.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Rule: self-referral. Two referred customers in September share a delivery address with the affiliate's account. Innocent explanation: a family member. Check: the owner confirms whether the account holder is related. Open. Amount at risk: 40.00."
