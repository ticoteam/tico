# Program method

The standing rules behind the weekly affiliate review. `program-terms.md` holds the team's published terms. `fraud-signals.md`
holds the rules a flag must name. This file says why each rule is there, so each run does not re-derive it. Every figure
carries its source and the year it was read. Check it at its source before you quote it.

## Counting a referral

- A referral is a visit or a code use that the tracking tool attributes to one affiliate inside the cookie or code window
  in the published terms. A referral is not a sale.
- A conversion is a referral that became a paid order or a paid plan start. Count one event type per line, and say which.
- One customer is counted once for the affiliate that first brought them inside the window. A customer who arrives through
  two affiliates is a dispute for a person, not two commissions.
- Keep the window in every figure. A sale 40 days after a 30-day window does not belong to that affiliate.

## Computing a commission

- Work out each commission from the rate and base in the published terms: the first order only, or every order, net of
  tax and shipping only if the terms say so. Round the same way the terms say.
- Reversals come out. A refund, a chargeback or a cancelled plan inside the reversal window reverses the commission it earned.
  A reversal after the window is a term question for a person.
- Holds are not losses. A commission still inside a hold period is owed later, and the review lists it as held, not unpaid.
- A payout list is a draft. It shows the affiliate, the period, the recomputed amount, the export amount and the difference.
  A person pays. The bot never sends money or a payment file.

## Fraud patterns to check

- Self-referral: the affiliate's own account, email domain, payment card or delivery address matches the referred customer.
- Coupon abuse: a code posted on a site the program never approved, or a code used far outside its stated audience.
- Brand-term bidding: an affiliate's paid search ads on the team's brand name, where the terms forbid it. Ad platforms also
  set their own trademark rules; quote the team's own terms and the platform's policy page, not memory.
- Cookie stuffing: referrals that arrive with no visit to the affiliate's declared site, or in bursts with no matching traffic.
- Many sign-ups from one device, address or payment method.
Each pattern needs its evidence and one innocent explanation before it becomes a flag. A flag goes to a person.

## Disclosure

- In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission's Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in
  Advertising (16 CFR Part 255) say that a material connection between an endorser and a seller must be disclosed when
  it is not obvious to the audience. Check the current text before you quote it; it was not read for this file.
- A missing disclosure on an affiliate's declared site is a finding for a person, with the page and the date it was checked.
