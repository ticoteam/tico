# Build a mutual action plan

When to use: once a deal is past discovery and the buyer has a decision or go-live date, or when a review needs the next milestone of a plan.

Steps:
1. Fix the anchor: the go-live date or decision date the buyer gave, in their words. If they gave neither, the first line of the plan is "date to agree". Keep any step date already fixed by the buyer or a calendar, with its source; every other step is "date to agree".
2. List the steps the buyer must take (their security review, legal, procurement, budget approval, signature, kickoff) and the steps we take (the proposal, the answers, the price approval by a human, the contract draft, the onboarding plan).
3. Order them and give each one a date, working backwards from the anchor. With no anchor, a step gets a date only from the fixed dates kept in step 1. Leave one day of slack before any step that the buyer's calendar could move.
4. Name one owner on each side for each step. Use a role only where no person is known, and say so.
5. Mark each step the buyer has agreed (with the date and source) and each one they have not. Do not show an unagreed step as agreed.
6. Find the critical path: the step that, if it slips, moves go-live. Put it at the top.
7. Share the plan with the buyer only when the task asks, mail sending is on in Tico, and a granted Tool allows it. Otherwise it stays a draft on the task.
8. At each review, show a step that has slipped twice above everything else.

Checks before you finish:
- Every step has an owner on each side, or a role that is marked as such.
- Each date comes after the one before it.
- Any step that needs a price or term approval names the human who gives it.
- Steps the buyer has not agreed are marked as not agreed.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Go-live 2026-12-01 (buyer's words, call 2026-09-22). 2026-10-09 buyer IT security review: Priya (buyer) and Omar (seller). 2026-10-16 price approval: [price: pricing owner]. Critical path: the security review, because the signer waits on it."
