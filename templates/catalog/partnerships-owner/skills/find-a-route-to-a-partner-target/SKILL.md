# Find a route to a partner target

When to use: before a partner outreach, a meeting request to a partner, or a one-page brief on a named firm or person.

Steps:
1. Pull what is already known from the record: notes, past contacts and any existing link to the target. Read the source note itself, not only its one-line summary.
2. Establish what is true now from public sources, each with its date. If the record and the public source disagree, state both and say which is newer.
3. Look for a direct route first: a person who knows the target, a shared partner, a customer who uses both.
4. If there is none, look for a second hop: a partner or customer who knows someone who knows the target. Record each route with an honest strength: direct, second hop or cold.
5. Write the one-page brief: who they are; why them and why now, with one dated fact; the route; and the next step, put as a question for a human. The brief takes no action itself.
6. If there is no route, write "no route found" and name the two people most likely to have one. That is a real answer.

Checks before you finish:
- Every claim has its source and date.
- No contact or message was made; the brief ends with what a human would do next.
- The route's strength is stated, not implied.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Target: a regional reseller. Route: second hop through a current referral partner who sent them two leads in 2025 (record, dated). Strength: second hop. Why now: a new-region announcement on their public page, read 2026-10-30. Next step for a human: ask the referral partner for an introduction."
