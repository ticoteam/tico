# Check a reply before a human posts

When to use: a thread is worth a human's reply, and a draft or suggested angle is about to go to that person.

Steps:
1. Check whether the reply would be a plain answer. If it names the company, links it, compares it or recommends it, it is an
   endorsement.
2. If the speaker works for the company, is paid by it or gets a free service, write the connection in plain words in the same
   message: "I work at [company]." A label only on a profile is not enough.
3. Check the reply makes no claim about results, prices or licensing that the team cannot source. A typical result is stated as
   typical, or not stated.
4. Check it does not ask for or reward a review. A paid review is not allowed, even with a disclosure.
5. Check the platform's rules on promotion and staff accounts. If unsure, flag it.
6. Flag the draft to the person with the rule and the link. The bot does not post. A draft with a missing or buried disclosure is
   flagged for the person to rewrite, not fixed in place.

Checks before you finish:
- The disclosure, where needed, sits in the same message as the endorsement.
- No results, price or licensing claim is unsourced.
- The draft is flagged to a person, and nothing was posted.

Example (generic): "A thread asks which tool handles waitlists. The reply would name the team's product. The draft says 'I work at the
company that makes this', then the feature. The results line 'two studios saw fewer no-shows' is cut, since it has no source. Flagged
to the community owner with the thread link and the disclosure rule."
