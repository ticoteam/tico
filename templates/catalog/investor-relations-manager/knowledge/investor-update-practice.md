# Investor update practice

The standing reference behind `playbooks/monthly-investor-update.md` and the skills in `skills/`. It holds what the format and the measures are for, the
dated sources, and what is a team choice rather than a rule. Benchmarks describe other companies and are context only.

## Cadence

- Monthly updates are common for early-stage companies. Growth-stage and later firms usually receive quarterly reports (Carta guidance, 2025, read through
  a secondary summary, 2026-11-01).
- The cadence in the signed investor agreement, side letter or board calendar wins over any guide. Check it before the first update.
- Pick a day and keep it. Consistency is the main thing a reader notices (Visible, investor update guidance, 2026, a vendor blog read 2026-11-01).

## Measures

- **Monthly recurring revenue (MRR)**: the monthly value of subscriptions in force on the last day of the month. Define exclusions in the metric line.
- **Net revenue retention (NRR)**: revenue this month from customers who paid a year ago, divided by what they paid a year ago. Includes expansion,
  contraction and churn from those customers. Excludes new customers.
- **Gross revenue retention (GRR)**: the same, without expansion. It cannot exceed 100%.
- **Runway (months)**: cash at the month end divided by average net burn over the last three months. State the burn window. If burn is zero or negative,
  write "not applicable" rather than a large number.
- **Burn multiple**: net burn divided by net new ARR over the same period. It is commonly attributed to David Sacks. The post that defines it and the
  thresholds quoted for it do not agree across secondary sources, and the primary post was not read. Quote the formula; do not quote thresholds without
  reading the original.

## Format

- Headline, metrics table, asks, highlights, lowlights, two-line recap, in that order, every month. One page.
- Investors read the metrics first. The table shows this month, last month and plan, with the same definitions.
- A short, honest lowlight does more for trust than a long list of highlights. Bad months open with the bad news.
