The first post, as it will look:

> ## What should a class reminder text say?
>
> *Acme blog · Nov 24, 2026 · 3 min read*
>
> A client books your Tuesday 6 p.m. class on Sunday. By Tuesday at 5:30 they have forgotten. The spot sits empty,
> and the person on your waitlist never hears it was free.
>
> A reminder text fixes that only if it says the right things. Studio owners ask us about this on almost every call.
> Here is what works.
>
> **Send it the day before, not the hour before.** An hour before is too late to give the spot to someone else. The
> day before gives your waitlist time.
>
> **Put the class, the time and the place first.** "Yoga Flow, Tue 6 p.m., Main St." Most people read the first line
> and stop.
>
> **Make cancelling easy.** Add one link to cancel. It feels backwards. But a client who cancels the day before frees
> a spot you can fill. A client who just doesn't come does not.
>
> **Keep it to one text.** Under 160 characters.
>
> Here is one that does all four:
>
> > Yoga Flow, Tue 6 p.m., Main St. See you there! Can't make it? Free your spot for someone else: acme.example/c/8F2
>
> Across 212 studios on Acme in September and October, reminders with a cancel link filled 1 in 3 late-cancelled
> spots from the waitlist. Reminders without one filled 1 in 8.
>
> **Start a free trial**, and Acme sends a reminder like this before every class.

The LinkedIn version (48 words): "Studio owners ask us this on almost every call: what should a reminder text say? Four
things. Send it the day before. Put the class and time first. Add a cancel link. Keep it to one text. The cancel link
is the one that fills spots. acme.example/blog/reminder-text"
