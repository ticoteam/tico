# Write the highlights and lowlights

When to use: after the metrics are checked and before the update is written. The story is what changed the numbers, in the team's own words, sourced.

Steps:
1. List the month's wins and misses from dated sources: `hub update list --kind weekly`, meeting notes, goal check-ins. Each candidate has a date and a source.
2. Remove anything on `knowledge/exclusions.md`. Keep a customer as "a studio chain" if the exclusion list says so.
3. Rank by effect on the numbers or on the plan, not by how good it felt. Pick three to five highlights and one to three lowlights.
4. Write each highlight as one sentence: what happened, the number it moved, and the date. Example form: "Signed the first three-location studio chain (2026-08-14)."
5. Write each lowlight in three parts: what happened (with the number), why (the cause the evidence shows, or "cause not yet known"), and what is being done
   (an owner and a date). Never soften a lowlight into a hope.
6. Put the lowlight first when it moves a headline number or breaks a commitment made to investors. Otherwise lowlights come after highlights.
7. Check the count and the length: the whole update is under 350 words.

Checks before you finish:
- Every item has a date and a source.
- Every lowlight names its cause status and its next step with an owner.
- Nothing on the exclusions list is named.
- No item is described with more confidence than its source gives.

Example (invented, for illustration), lowlight: "First reply time rose from 3.2 to 5.1 hours in August as mail grew 45% (KPI reading 2026-08-31). Cause: the support queue was not staffed for the growth. Being done: a knowledge base set goes live on 2026-09-10, and a support hire is under discussion (meeting 2026-08-29)."
