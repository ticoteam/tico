# Community practice

The standing method behind `playbooks/weekly-community-digest.md` and `playbooks/answer-a-thread.md`. `community.md`
holds the team's own rules, target and routing. This file holds what is true about running a community in general.
Every source carries the year it was read.

## Measures

- Time to first response is the gap between a question that needs an answer and the first reply. CHAOSS, a Linux
  Foundation project, defines it as a community health metric and reads a long gap as a sign of low engagement or of
  a support gap (CHAOSS metric page, read 2026-11-01 through search excerpts).
- Report the median and the count still waiting past the target. An average hides the long tail of unanswered questions.
- Track who answers: members answering members, and members who return after a first post. A community that only
  the team answers stays small. A post count alone does not say whether anyone was helped.
- Set the target for your community. Vendor benchmarks for first response are mostly about customer-support tickets. A
  2025 Freshworks report on about 1.2 billion tickets puts median first response near an hour (support tickets, not
  community posts; read 2026-11-01 through search excerpts). Use it as a context signal, not as the target.
- The Community Roundtable publishes an annual State of Community Management survey (the 2024 edition is the 15th).
  Compare with its survey averages only with the year stated. Feverbee publishes community benchmark data; its
  figures were not read for this file.

## Replies

- The answer comes first, then the steps, then the source. A reply that stands alone is the norm in help communities
  (practice; no single source).
- Quote the current policy document and its version. Two conflicting statements are both recorded with dates. Nobody
  chooses between them without the owner.

## Recognition

- Name the specific answer when you thank a member. Thanks tied to a named, checked contribution read as sincere.
  Generic thanks read as filler (practice).
- Keep the champions list small and dated. Remove anyone who asks to be left off.

## Moderation

- The team sets the rules; a person applies them. The bot reports the post, the rule and the link. It hides, edits,
  warns, mutes or bans only when the task asks for it and a granted Tool allows it (see AGENT.md, Sending). The rules
  it applies are in `knowledge/community.md`.
