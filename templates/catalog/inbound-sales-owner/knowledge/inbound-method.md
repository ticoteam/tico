# Inbound method

The standing rules behind every sweep, reply and read-out. The figures marked "default" are replaced with the
team's own at setup, and the rules around them stay as they are.

## Speed to lead

- The clock starts at arrival: the form's submit time, or the time the email reached the sales inbox. It never starts
  when a sweep reads the lead.
- It stops at the first reply ready for review, meaning a draft on the task. Once sending is on and a person has
  approved a reply, a second clock runs to the sent reply.
- Business hours are counted in the team's own time zone, Monday to Friday, within the working hours set at setup
  (default 09:00 to 17:00).
- Default target: a draft within two business hours; a sent reply within one business day of approval. A lead that
  arrives outside hours counts from the next opening.
- Report the median and the share inside target. Late leads stay in both figures.

## Qualifying criteria

The default five, replaced by the team's own at setup. Each one is met, not met or unknown.

1. Problem: they describe a problem the product solves, in their own words.
2. Fit: size, sector, location and role, against the ideal customer profile.
3. Timing: a decision in the next 90 days, or a named trigger such as a renewal, a launch or a hire.
4. Buyer: the person can join a call, or names who can.
5. Budget: a range given, or not asked yet. Budget is never asked for in the first reply.

Exclusions are checked first: customers (to the Account Manager), competitors, a current opportunity with another seller,
the do-not-contact list, students and job seekers, and vendors selling to the team.

Sort outcomes: meeting, question for a person, not a fit (naming the criterion), or stop. A spam message or a vendor pitch
is logged and nothing more.

## Reply rules

- The first sentence answers the question.
- Under 120 words, plain text, one ask, one link at most.
- When a call is wanted, two calendar times with the time zone written out.
- Prices, discounts, terms and dates come only from the recorded price list. Anything else is a marked gap.
- No "I hope you are well", no flattery, and no urgency the lead did not show.
- Signed by the named person who approves it.

## Lost reasons

Each closed-lost lead gets one primary reason from this list:

- No decision: the buyer stopped without choosing anyone.
- Chose another vendor: named, or not.
- Price or budget: the buyer said cost was the blocker.
- Timing: the decision moved out, or a contract is locked in.
- Fit: the product does not do the thing they need.
- Wrong buyer: the person could not decide, and nobody else took the call.
- Internal project stopped: the buyer's own plan changed.
- No reason given: the last contact date is recorded.

A secondary reason is allowed only with its own evidence.

## Metrics and windows

- Inbound leads: arrivals in the window, by source.
- Speed to lead: median business minutes to a draft, and the share inside target.
- Lead-to-meeting: calls booked by leads that arrived in the window, within 14 days of arrival. An open lead is open.
- Lead-to-close: closed-won over leads that arrived in the window, counted only once the cohort is older than the sales
  cycle (default 60 days). The open count is reported beside it.
- Lost reasons: the count of each primary reason in the cohort.

## Rules that do not change

- A stop is recorded in the run that reads it.
- No rate, speed or count is written without a dated source and a window.
- Replies stay drafts until a person approves, and a send needs a task that asks for it and a granted Tool.
