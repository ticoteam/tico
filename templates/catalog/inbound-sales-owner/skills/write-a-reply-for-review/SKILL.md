# Write a reply for review

When to use: for every inbound lead sorted as a meeting or a question, before anyone is asked to send anything.

Steps:
1. Restate the lead's question in your own words, then find the answer in the recorded sources: the product pages, the
   team's FAQ, the price list in the task. An answer you cannot find is a gap, not a guess.
2. Open with the answer, in the first sentence. Do not open with thanks, a compliment or "I hope you are well".
3. Use their words for their problem, once. Repeat nothing they already know.
4. If a call is wanted, offer two times from the calendar: read the bookings in `tico calendar list`, set them against the
   seller's working hours, and write the time zone out. Never offer a time the calendar shows as taken.
5. Where the lead asks for a price, term, date, or a security or legal answer that is not on record, write
   `[for the seller: ...]` in that place. Do not fill it.
6. End with one ask. Keep the reply under 120 words, in plain text, with one link at most.
7. Put the draft on the task with the recipient, the subject, the body, the source of each fact, and the time it was
   drafted. It stays a draft until a person approves it. It is sent only when a task asks for the send and a granted
   Tool allows it.

Checks before you finish:
- The first sentence answers the question.
- Every fact in the body has a source on the task, or it is removed.
- No price, discount, term or date appears unless it came from the recorded list.
- The reply is under 120 words and has one ask.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Subject: Re: your question on class packs. Body: Packs renew on the first of each
month, and you can pause one for up to a month. [for the seller: confirm the pause rule for annual plans]. Would Tuesday at
10:00 or Wednesday at 14:00 Pacific work for a 20-minute call? Draft, not sent."
