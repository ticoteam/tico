# Size a price change

When to use: when a task asks you to write an impact note, or to put a number on a proposed price, plan or limit change.

Steps:
1. State the change exactly: the plans, the new price or limit, the effective date, and whether it applies to new customers only or to everyone. If any part is open, lay out the variants side by side.
2. Count the customers affected per plan and segment from a billing or CRM export (read only). For each, the current bill, the new bill and the renewal date. Customers on annual terms are counted at renewal, not at the effective date.
3. Compute the static effect: the sum of (new bill minus current bill) per period across the affected customers, assuming none leaves. Show it per segment.
4. Compute break-even churn. For a percentage rise p on the affected base, the break-even share of revenue at the new prices that can leave is p divided by (1 + p), on revenue only and before costs. A 15% rise breaks even at about 13% of revenue lost. Bills differ, so one large account can pass that line while the customer count stays below it. Give a customer count only with a stated billing assumption, such as one bill for every customer. Say plainly that churn response is unknown unless a study measured it.
5. Cost the options. Grandfathering existing customers for a period costs the static effect on them over that period. A notice period defers the increase for its window: its cost is the monthly static effect times the months in the window. Customers who leave during the window are a separate churn assumption, shown on their own line. An exception (large accounts, non-profits) costs its share of the static effect.
6. List the accounts with the largest absolute increase, by name, for the owner. Flag any contract term that may limit the change as a question for `legal-review`. Do not interpret contracts yourself.
7. Write the note: a one-line summary of the facts with no recommendation unless the task asks for one; the segment table; the break-even; the options with their costs; the unknowns and what would reduce each.

Checks before you finish:
- Every number traces to a named export or a stated assumption.
- Break-even churn is stated as revenue lost, with its formula and its assumption (revenue only, before costs).
- No churn figure appears that a study did not measure; "unknown" is an allowed answer.
- The summary line states facts only.
- Contract wording is a question for `legal-review`, not a conclusion.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Pro +15% for new and existing customers from 2026-12-01. 400 Pro customers at $100 a month. If none leaves, +$6,000 a month (+15%). Break-even revenue churn is 0.15 / 1.15, about 13% of revenue at the new prices ($46,000 a month), which is about 52 customers at the new $115 bill each. No churn study on file. Grandfathering existing customers for 12 months defers about $72,000 of the increase. Unknowns: how Pro customers respond at the one-location limit; whether any annual contract fixes the price until renewal."
