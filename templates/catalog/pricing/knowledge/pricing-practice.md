# Pricing practice

Standing reference for how price research and price changes are done. Facts about this team's own prices live in `our-prices.md`; this file holds general practice and dated benchmarks, each with its source and year. Where a benchmark and this team's own data disagree, the team's data wins for this team, and the disagreement is noted.

## Willingness to pay is stated, not observed

- Survey answers overstate what people pay. A meta-analysis of stated-preference studies found hypothetical willingness to pay about 35% above what people paid when real money was involved (median ratio about 1.35 across 28 studies). Use a survey to bound a range, not to set a price.
- The Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter asks four questions (too cheap, a bargain, getting expensive, too expensive) and finds where the answers cross. It gives a range of acceptable prices, not a point. Introduced in 1976.
- A real test beats an asked one: show a price to a random share of new buyers, compare conversion and revenue per visitor, and size the test before it starts.

## Price rises and profit

- Marn and Rosiello (1992) estimated that a 1% price improvement, with volume held steady, raised operating profit by about 11% across 2,463 companies. Volume rarely holds, so treat this as the upper bound for a price rise, not a forecast.
- A rise pays only if the customers lost cost less than the margin gained. For a percentage rise p on the affected base, break-even revenue churn is p / (1 + p) of revenue at the new prices, on revenue only and before costs (derived here, not from a source). A 10% rise breaks even at about 9% of new-price revenue, a 15% rise at about 13%, a 25% rise at 20%.

## Discounts

- Report discounts as a distribution: the share discounted, the median among discounted deals, the spread, and the share over the written cap. A mean is pulled by one large deal; a median is not.
- A discount the written rule does not allow is a finding about the rule, the approvals or the price list. Name it that way, not as a failure of a person.
- Compare complete periods on the same definition. A few points of change on a few dozen deals is usually noise.

## Competitor pages

- A public page shows the price its owner wants seen. The price the buyer pays is what the checkout or quote shows, with required add-ons and the minimum commitment.
- Prices vary by region, currency and promotion. Record the region each price was read from.

## Sources
- 1976: Van Westendorp, P., Price Sensitivity Meter (PSM), ESOMAR Congress.
- 2005: Murphy et al., meta-analysis of hypothetical bias in stated-preference valuation, Environmental and Resource Economics, DOI 10.1007/s10640-004-3332-z (28 studies; median hypothetical-to-actual ratio about 1.35).
- 1992: Marn, M. V. and Rosiello, R. L., "Managing Price, Gaining Profit", Harvard Business Review, September-October 1992 (11% operating-profit effect of a 1% price improvement, 2,463 companies; check the article before quoting it).
