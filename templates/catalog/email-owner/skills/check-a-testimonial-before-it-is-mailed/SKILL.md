# Check a testimonial before it is mailed

When to use: an email quotes a customer, shows a review or star rating, states a result, or offers a guarantee.

Steps:
1. For each quote or review, name its source: the person, the date, and where it was written. The file that holds it is
   named in the notes. A quote with no file does not go in.
2. Confirm the person agreed to be quoted, and with what name and detail. Keep the name as they gave it.
3. Confirm no reward was tied to the review being positive. If one was, cut the review.
4. If the writer works for the business or is paid by it, the email says so next to the quote.
5. For a result, record its basis (the sample, the period, the date) and what most customers get. Mail only a result that most
   customers get, with its basis beside it. A best case is cut, or replaced with the typical result. A "results not typical"
   line on its own does not qualify a best case under US FTC guidance; check the Endorsement Guides at source, since other
   countries set their own rules.
6. For a guarantee, keep its terms in the same paragraph, or cut the word.
7. Never write a review, a persona or a customer quote. If one is missing, cut it or mark a gap for the owner.
8. Anything with a legal question goes to the owner before it is mailed.

Checks before you finish:
- Every quote and result has its file, date and permission in the notes.
- No review or quote was made up or reworded beyond what the customer approved.
- Every guarantee shows its terms in the same paragraph.

Example (generic): "The draft quotes a studio owner: 'It cut my no-shows.' Source: a call note dated 2026-10-02, and the
owner's approval to use a first name and studio name, both in the file. The result 'fewer empty spots' is kept only as the
typical outcome, with its basis beside it: 'two-month sample, six studios, most saw fewer empty spots'. The 'money back'
line is replaced by its terms: 'refund within 14 days of the first charge, see the policy'."
