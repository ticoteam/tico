# Hiring practice

Durable reference for the recruiting role. Every figure carries its source and the year it was read. Check the source
before you quote a number in a summary or a post. Laws change: the dates below are secondary reads, not statute texts.

## Interview evidence

- Structured interviews (the same questions for every candidate, a scoring guide, scores written before discussion) are
  the best-supported interview format. Sackett, Zhang, Berry and Lievens (2022, Journal of Applied Psychology 107(11))
  revised the estimate for structured interviews to about 0.42, down from about 0.51 in Schmidt and Hunter (1998). The
  lower 80% credibility value is about 0.18, so one interview predicts a job outcome only loosely.
- Work samples: the same paper revised the estimate from about 0.54 (Schmidt and Hunter 1998) to about 0.33.
- Practical reading: use several measures (a structured interview, a short work task, the criteria in the application).
  No single one should decide a hire, and no score in a summary is a rank.
- Source note: read from secondary write-ups (vendor and HR blogs), 2026-11-01. The paper's own table was not read. Check
  Table 3 of the paper before you quote the exact decimals.

## Pay transparency

- A growing number of US states and cities require a pay range in a job posting. Dates below are from secondary summaries
  read 2026-11-01:
  - Colorado: 2021, all employers.
  - California: 2023, employers with 15 or more employees.
  - Illinois: January 1, 2025.
  - New Jersey: June 1, 2025.
  - Vermont: July 1, 2025.
  - Virginia: July 1, 2026, according to one source. Check it.
  - Maine and Delaware: dates conflict between sources. Check the state's labour department page before relying on either.
- Sources disagree on the total: between 13 and 15 jurisdictions as of mid-2026. Use the state's own list, not a vendor's.
- Many state laws apply when a job could be filled from that state, not only when the employer is based there. Check the
  statute's own wording for the role's location.
- There is no federal pay transparency law as of this reading.

## AI in hiring

- New York City, Local Law 144: in force since July 5, 2023. It covers an automated employment decision tool: a computational
  process that issues a score, classification or recommendation and substantially assists or replaces discretionary
  decisions, used with an NYC nexus. Such a tool needs an independent bias audit before use, a public summary of the audit,
  and notice to NYC candidates at least 10 business days before use. A tool that does not substantially assist or replace a
  decision may fall outside the law. Check the DCWP guidance for remote roles and for candidates who live in NYC.
- Illinois, HB 3773 (amends the Illinois Human Rights Act, 775 ILCS 5/2-102(L)): in effect from January 1, 2026, according to
  one vendor summary. It requires notice when an employer uses AI for the employment purposes the amendment lists, which
  include recruitment and hiring. It has no "significant decision" threshold. Check the statute text for the list and the
  notice rule. The 2019 Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act still applies to video interviews analysed by AI.
- California: Civil Rights Council regulations under the Fair Employment and Housing Act on automated-decision systems,
  in effect from October 1, 2025, according to secondary summaries.
- Colorado: the Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205) was due to take effect June 30, 2026. In May 2026 Colorado signed SB 26-189,
  which repeals and re-enacts the Act. A lawsuit filed in April 2026 sought an injunction. Its current operative date and
  status were not confirmed in this read. Check before you rely on any Colorado rule.

## Job post wording

- Gendered wording in job ads affects who feels they belong in the role. Gaucher, Friesen and Kay (2011, Journal of
  Personality and Social Psychology) found that masculine-coded adverts made women less likely to see themselves in the
  job. Cited from the published paper, not re-read for this file.
- Each requirement you add lowers the number of people who apply. Keep the list to what the work needs in the first six
  months. A number of years is a proxy unless the work truly needs that exact experience.
- Practice, not measured here: show the pay range near the title, and the commission plan beside it, not in a footnote.

## Candidate replies

- Default in this template: acknowledge within two working days, and name the next step with a date. This is a team
  choice, not a published benchmark. Change it in `knowledge/wording.md` when the team decides otherwise.
- A decline is written only after the hiring manager has recorded it. Its wording comes from `knowledge/wording.md`.

## What a summary may use

| May use | Must not use |
| --- | --- |
| The criteria as the manager wrote them | Proxies: name, photo, graduation year, employment gap, postcode |
| What the application shows, quoted | Searching the candidate online |
| Stated right-to-work or visa status, only where the law lets the employer ask | Health, pregnancy, family, religion, age, origin, disability |
| The date and stage a human set | Pay history, or the current salary |
