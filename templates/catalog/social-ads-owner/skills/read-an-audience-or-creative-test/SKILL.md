# Read an audience or creative test

When to use: when you build a new audience or creative test, before it goes live, and when you measure it.

Steps:
1. Write the question in one sentence that names the measure: "Does the morning-routine hook give a lower cost per trial than the price-proof hook?" One idea per test.
2. Name the variants: two or three, each differing only in the idea under test. Hold everything else the same (audience, placement, budget, landing page), or the test is not readable.
3. Name the primary conversion and the target from `knowledge/targets.md`. A test read on clicks, views or engagement has no result.
4. Size it. The count per variant is the default in `knowledge/targets.md` (30 primary conversions). Estimate the weeks it takes from this campaign's last four weeks of conversions per variant, split evenly. If that is more than eight weeks, the test is not run as a test. Make one before-and-after change instead, read it at four weeks, and label the evidence as weaker.
5. Set the dates in the test's record: the earliest read is seven days after launch or the day the count is reached, whichever is later. Do not stop early because one variant looks good. Stop a variant early only when it is broken (no delivery, or a platform rejection), and say why.
6. At the read, show each variant's count and cost per primary conversion side by side. Read the gap against its spread (see the Creative testing section of `knowledge/paid-social-method.md`): with about 30 conversions in each variant, a gap under about 52 per cent is no winner yet.
7. Record the test in `knowledge/tests.md`: the question, the variants, the primary conversion, the count needed, the launch date, the earliest read, the reading and the decision (keep, replace, or no difference yet).
8. A winner becomes the control for the next test. Start the next test from it.

Checks before you finish:
- The question was written before launch, and it names one idea.
- The count and the earliest read date are stated, and no call is made before either.
- Each variant's count is shown beside its cost, and the gap is read against its spread.
- The result is recorded in `knowledge/tests.md`, including "no difference yet".

Example (invented, for illustration): "Test: morning-routine hook against price-proof hook, home-brewer prospecting, primary conversion subscription trial. Needs 30 trials per variant; the last four weeks show about 12 per variant per week, so about 2.5 weeks. Launched 2026-11-09, so the earliest read is the day both variants reach 30, expected about 2026-11-27. Interim look on 2026-11-23, not a read: morning routine 22 trials at a cost of 56.10 each, price proof 27 at 48.80 each. The gap of 15 per cent is under one standard error for counts this size (about 29 per cent), so it is no difference yet. Keep both running and read on 2026-11-27, or on the day both reach 30."
