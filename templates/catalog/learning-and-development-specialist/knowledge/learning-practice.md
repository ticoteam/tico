# Learning practice

What research and practice say about how adults learn at work, and how this bot applies it. Written for the HR owner and for
managers who plan training. It shapes plans and the tracker. It does not judge a person.

## Training works on average, and design decides how much

Arthur, Bennett, Edens and Bell (2003) meta-analysed training studies in organisations. The mean effect was positive (d about .62
for behaviour and results criteria, as the abstract reports). Design features, including practice and feedback, were linked to
larger effects.

Applied here: a course in a plan has to name the practice and the feedback it gives. A course that only presents content is
listed as awareness, not as building the skill.

Source status: abstract read; full text not read.

## Spaced practice beats one block

Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted and Rohrer (2006) synthesised distributed-practice studies of verbal recall: 839 assessments from 317
experiments. Practice spread over time gave better retention than the same practice in one block.

Applied here: skill courses and mandatory refreshers are set as short sessions with a review date, not one long session. The
review covered verbal recall tasks. Carrying the result to other skills is a reasoned step, not a measured one.

Source status: abstract and summaries read.

## Transfer depends on the manager

Blume, Ford, Baldwin and Huang (2010) reviewed transfer of training to the job. Supervisor support and chances to use the new
skill at work were among the strongest links to transfer.

Applied here: every role plan names the manager's part: the next piece of work to use the skill on and the check at the next
review. The new-manager curriculum has a manager check at day 30 and day 60.

Source status: secondary summary read; full text not read.

## The 70:20:10 split is a rule of thumb

The split (70 percent on the job, 20 percent from others, 10 percent formal training) is widely quoted. It is commonly traced to
a 1996 survey of managers by Lombardo and Eichinger, used in development planning. Clardy (2018) reviewed the claim and found
little empirical support for the exact proportions. It has not been shown as a measured ratio across organisations.

Applied here: role plans use the split as a prompt to include work and people. It is not a target, and the bot does not report
a share of time by type, because no team's data supports one.

Source status: Clardy's review read through a secondary summary. The 1996 survey was not read.

## House rules (not research)

- A completion counts only with a record: a provider export, a certificate on the task, or the person's own word on the task.
- Training records never feed a performance decision.
- Course lists are dated. A course no longer required is removed from `knowledge/mandatory.md`, with the date.

## Open questions

- Which required courses have a provider export at all. Unknown until the first export arrives.
- What spend per person is reasonable is not stated here. The budget comes from the requester.
