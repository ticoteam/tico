# Cancellation practice

Standing reference for how cancellation, downgrade and save requests are handled. It holds the rules that apply and the measures
that matter. The team's reason codes and offers live in `save-policy.md`. Where the two disagree, the team's policy wins unless a
rule under "Rules that apply" requires otherwise, and the disagreement is written on the reasons log line. This is not legal
advice. Check the rules for each market with counsel.

## Cancelling stays as easy as signing up
- Cancelling uses the same medium as sign-up, or an easier one, and finishes in one step once the customer has asked.
- One save offer per request, shown once. A second offer after a no, a maze of screens and a guilt line all count against the
  customer and, in some markets, against the team.

## Rules that apply
- United States, federal: the FTC click-to-cancel rule was set aside by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025 (most summaries give
  8 July) and never took effect. State automatic-renewal laws were not affected.
- California: AB 2863 amended the Automatic Renewal Law, effective 1 July 2025. As summarised in law-firm alerts, it requires
  cancelling in the same medium used to enter the subscription, adds a click-to-quit option, requires annual reminders and
  express consent, and allows retention offers during cancellation subject to conditions. The statute text (Bus. & Prof. Code
  sections 17601 and 17602) was not read for this file.
- European Union: Directive (EU) 2023/2673 (Article 11a) requires a withdrawal button for distance contracts concluded through
  an online interface, where the right of withdrawal applies, from 19 June 2026. National rules vary. Check the directive text
  on EUR-Lex and the customer's country before relying on it.

## Save offers
- Match one offer to one reason (see `skills/match-one-save-offer/SKILL.md`). A discount for a few months does not fix a customer
  who does not use the product; for that reason a pause or a help call may fit better.
- Measure saves at 30 days and at 90 days. A save is a customer still paying. A reply that was sent is not a save.

## Failed payments are a separate queue
Failed payments cause a share of cancellations that customers never asked for. Vendor write-ups put that share in a wide range,
roughly a fifth to a third in most figures; these were not verified at source and are not used here as a target. Treat failed
payments as a billing-recovery queue owned by the billing owner, and report them on their own line, not as a reason code.

## Small numbers are counts
With few requests a week, one customer moves a percentage a lot. Below about 5 requests a week, report counts without percentages.
A change in a reason counts as a finding only when it holds for two weeks running.
