# Survey practice

Standing reference for running and reading pulse surveys. The team's own questions, threshold and schedule live in
`survey.md`; this file holds general practice and dated context, each with its source. Where the two disagree, the team's own
written policy wins.

## Context on engagement (dated)

- Gallup's State of the Global Workplace 2025 report (2024 data) puts 21 percent of employees worldwide as engaged, 62 percent as
  not engaged and 17 percent as actively disengaged. The report compares its manager group with individual contributors; neither figure was checked for this file, so read them at source before quoting either. A 2026 Gallup
  release gives 20 percent for 2025. Read the latest release before you quote a number, and quote only the one you read.
- Use external engagement figures as context for a readout. They are not targets for this team, and they do not replace the
  team's own trend over the last three quarters.

## Anonymity

- Five people is the most common minimum group size in employee survey tools (vendor help pages, read 2026-11-01). Some tools
  let an admin lower it to three. Vendors warn that a lower number raises the risk of identifying someone.
- Comments carry more risk than scores. A theme drawn from very few comments in a small group can point at a person. Set a
  stricter rule for comments than for scores in `survey.md` and state it.
- Filters are the usual leak. Each filter that splits a group can push it below the threshold. Check the threshold after every
  filter, not only at the start.

## Questions and trends

- A trend needs identical wording and the same scale. Change one word and the series starts again.
- Keep the question count short enough to finish: a pulse is a few minutes, not a review.
- Say how far a change must move before it counts as real, and set that rule before the readout, not after seeing it. A few
  points on a small group is usually noise.

## Participation

- Report participation against the number invited, overall and for each group above the threshold. A score without its base
  is not a finding.
- Low participation in one group is itself a finding. Report it, without guessing why.
- No benchmark for response rate was checked for this file. Use the team's own last three quarters as the baseline.

## Acting on results

- Two or three actions per readout, each with one owner, a date and a line on what employees will be told.
- An action done but not yet told to employees is not finished. Telling people is what makes the next survey worth answering.
- Keep the action list in `actions.md` so the next readout can say what happened to each one.

- Survey reporting thresholds: help pages from vendors (Reflektive, 15Five, CultureMonkey and Microsoft Viva Glint materials),
