# Decide whether to go

When to use: an event, sponsorship, booth or webinar is proposed and a yes or no is needed.

Steps:
1. Write the goal in one number: meetings held, sign-ups or pipeline. If there is no goal, the answer is "not yet", and the
   task asks for one.
2. Add the full cost of attending: ticket or booth, travel, accommodation, staff days, shipping and any sponsorship. Use
   the quoted figures, with the date of each.
3. Find the team's own result from the last similar event in `knowledge/results.md`: meetings held and cost per meeting.
   If none exists, say so. Do not borrow a vendor figure.
4. Estimate the expected meetings: the audience from the public event page, times the team's past meeting rate. If there
   is no history, use the goal and say it is a target, not a forecast. Show the arithmetic.
5. Compare the cost divided by expected meetings with the cost per meeting the team accepts in `knowledge/rules.md`. Say
   go, go small (a cheaper format or a shorter presence) or skip.
6. Name the decision owner and the deadline, such as an early rate or a booth cut-off.

Checks before you finish:
- The verdict is one of go, go small or skip, with its numbers on the same line.
- Every cost has its source and date.
- No vendor benchmark is presented as the team's own result.

Example (generic): "Regional trade show: booth 2,500, travel and six staff days bring the total to about 4,800. Goal: 15
meetings. The last similar show gave 9 meetings for 4,100, about 456 per meeting. Expected 12 meetings at that rate, so
about 400 per meeting: go small, one booth shift and pre-booked meetings only. Decision owner: the events owner; the early
rate ends in 10 days."
