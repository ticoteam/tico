# Close practice

How this bot measures a close and reviews the books. Values marked **house default** are starting points for this team, not standards. The Head of Finance can
change them in `knowledge/close-checklist.md`. Benchmarks are directional: the samples are small, the sources are mostly vendor surveys, and most figures below came
from secondary summaries.

## Measures
- **Close time.** Business days from the period's last day to the day the Head of Finance signs off. Count business days and say so. Vendor surveys mostly use
  business days; APQC uses calendar days, so convert before comparing.
- **Reconciled accounts.** Share of the accounts in `knowledge/reconciliations.md` that tie on the close day. Each one either ties or has a named open item.
- **Post-close changes.** Entries posted after sign-off. Each one gets a `late-adjustment` task.

## Benchmarks (directional)
- Ledge, 2025: of 100 finance professionals at firms from 51 to over 10,000 staff, 18% closed in one to three business days, 32% in four or five, 23% in six or
  seven, and 27% in more than seven. Half take longer than five business days.
- Consero, 2025 Finance Leaders Survey: of 103 CFOs and VPs of finance at firms with revenue of $10 million to $200 million, 7% close in under three days. The share
  closing within nine days rose from 8% in 2024 to 62% in 2025.
- Venasolutions survey (year not stated on the page): average close of 6.2 business days. 78% of teams had restated or corrected numbers in the past year after
  marking the close complete.
- APQC (calendar days, date not confirmed): median close roughly 6.0 to 6.4 days; top performers 4.8 days or fewer.

**House target (proposal, not a benchmark):** five business days for the first close, down to three once the checklist has held for three months. Five sits near
the middle of the Ledge split.

## Reconciliations
- Tie to the statement or subledger at the same date. Tolerance is zero unless the checklist sets one. A rounding difference under 1.00 may be named as rounding,
  never hidden in a plug.
- **Aged reconciling items (house default).** Up to 30 days: normal. 31 to 60 days: a named next step. 61 to 90 days: the owner is told in writing. Over 90 days:
  the Head of Finance approves in writing before it is carried forward.

## Journal entries
- **Test the population first.** The count and total of the export must match the system's own report for the period. If they do not, stop and list the gap. The
  PCAOB's Audit Focus on journal entries lists the completeness of the entry population as a basis for the auditor's testing; the same discipline applies here.
- **Include automated entries.** Override risk is not limited to manual entries (PCAOB Audit Focus).
- **Vary the tests from month to month.** One vendor describes an element of unpredictability in audit selection. That is a secondary claim, not checked against
  AS 2401, so treat it as good practice, not a rule.
- **House defaults for flags.** Round amounts (multiples of 1,000) above the review limit. Duplicates within 60 days. Every entry posted after sign-off.

## Variance review
- **House default:** explain a movement when it is over 10% and over 5,000 against the prior month. Both must hold. Change the amount in the checklist to fit the
  business.
