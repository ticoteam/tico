# Scheduling practice

Standing reference for building and chasing interview loops. Each figure carries its source and the year it was read.
Vendor figures are weak evidence: quote one only with its source, and say that it is a vendor figure.

## How a loop is built

- Offer three options, not one and not ten. Three gives the candidate a choice without a long search, and gives you
  a fallback if one option is lost.
- Keep the loop short. Fewer rounds, with the same panel members where possible, reduces the number of calendars that can
  break. Two days at most for a loop of several rounds.
- Put back-to-back rounds on one day with a short break, so the candidate is not waiting between them.
- Hold the buffer that the interviewer rules set before and after each interview. Rules without a buffer produce late
  starts.
- Offer times in the candidate's time zone and the team's, and name the time zone once.

## How candidates experience the process

- Vendor data disagrees on how slow a first reply can be before candidates drop out. One 2026 vendor benchmark (Pin, 2026,
  from its own sample of outreach threads) puts the median time from first contact to a first interview at about 5.8 days.
  Treat that as a vendor figure, not a standard.
- Vendor reports also disagree on where candidates drop out. A 2025 frontline hiring report (iCIMS) is cited for a
  share of drop-off at the interview stage, but secondary sources quote it differently. Do not quote a drop-off share
  without the original report open.
- The practical rule in this template: reply within the agreed wait (default two working days), and give the next step
  with a date even when the next step is "we are still reviewing".

## Feedback

- Scores are written before the panel talks. Kahneman, Sibony and Sunstein (2021, "Noise") treat collecting independent
  judgments before a group discussion as a decision-hygiene step against the influence of an early view in the room. Cited
  from the book, not re-read for this file.
- Recruiting software often locks submissions until every reviewer has submitted. Where the team's tool does not, the
  coordinator keeps the order by hand: the pack lists scores in submission order.
- A debrief without every score is a discussion, not a decision. The pack names who is missing.

## Limits and gaps

- No peer-reviewed study was found in this reading that measures how much a pre-debrief scoring rule changes hiring
  decisions. The practice rests on the argument above and on common use, not on a measured effect.
- Time-to-interview and drop-off figures in this file are vendor or secondary figures, read 2026-11-01. Check the original
  report before you use one in a summary.
