# Write a one-round candidate message

When to use: each time a candidate needs an interview invitation, a time offer, a confirmation, a reminder, or a change.

Steps:
1. Name the purpose in one line: offer times, confirm a booking, remind, or change a time.
2. Include, in this order: the role; the rounds and who the candidate will meet (first name and role only); the length and the
   format (video or on site); the options or the confirmed time, in the candidate's time zone with the team's named once;
   the join link or the room; what to prepare, if anything; and one line on how to ask for a different time or an
   accommodation.
3. Use the candidate's first name, and the contact the thread already uses. Do not add a phone number or another address.
4. Say nothing about how the interview went, what the panel thinks, or what happens next beyond the next scheduled step.
5. Keep it under 120 words. Read it once as a candidate who has not seen the role before, and check that every question they
   will have is answered in the message.
6. Put the draft on the task. It is sent only when a person has turned mail sending on in Tico and the task asks for it.

Checks before you finish:
- Time, time zone, length, format, join link and the change route are all present.
- There is no hint of a decision, a score, or a comparison with other candidates.
- Accommodation or personal details are not in the message. Requests of that kind go to the hiring manager untouched.
- The message is under 120 words and has one ask at most.

Example (generic, invented): "Hi Sam, thank you for applying for the Support Specialist role. The next step is two
conversations of about 45 minutes each, by video. You will meet a member of the support team and the hiring manager. Please
choose one of these times (your time zone, Pacific): Tuesday 10:00, Wednesday 14:00 or Thursday 09:30. The join link comes
with your confirmation. If none of these work, reply and tell me two other times that suit you."
