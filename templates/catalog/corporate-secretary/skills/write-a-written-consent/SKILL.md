# Write a written consent

When to use: when a decision is needed between meetings and the board or stockholders can act by written consent.

Steps:
1. Identify the entity, its state of incorporation and the kind of consent. Board consent needs every director to sign. Stockholder consent is allowed unless the certificate of incorporation provides otherwise (in Delaware, DGCL §228(a)); check the same default in the state of incorporation. The consent must be signed by holders of at least the votes a meeting would need to take the action, and prompt notice must go to stockholders who did not consent (DGCL §228(e)).
2. Write each resolution in plain wording: what is approved, for whom, how much, and on which earlier approval it rests (cite the minute or consent).
3. Name the signers the bylaws require, and the order if it matters.
4. Add a date line left blank for each signer.
5. Name where the signed copy will be filed in the minute book.
6. Head the draft "Draft for counsel". Do not send it for signature. A person circulates it once counsel has settled it.

Checks before you finish:
- Each resolution stands alone and can be read without the others.
- The signers come from the bylaws, not from memory.
- A grant appears only with its board terms, or a flag for counsel.
- A stockholder consent shows the votes it needs and notes the prompt notice to non-consenting holders.
- The draft is headed for counsel and is not marked final.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Resolved, that the board approves the grant of options over 10,000 shares to Lena, under the plan adopted on 1 March 2026, at the exercise price set by the board on the grant date. Signers required by bylaws 4.3: all five directors."
