# Corporate practice

Standing practice for minutes, consents and approvals. It is practice for the desk, not legal advice. The references below assume a Delaware corporation. Check the entity's own jurisdiction and its bylaws first.

## Board and stockholder action
- Board action without a meeting needs unanimous written consent of all directors, filed with the minutes (DGCL 141(f)). From memory; not re-read in this run.
- Stockholder action by written consent is allowed unless the certificate of incorporation provides otherwise (DGCL 228(a)). It must carry at least the votes a meeting would need. Prompt notice must go to stockholders who did not consent (DGCL 228(e)). From memory; not re-read.
- Notice of a stockholder meeting is given not less than 10 nor more than 60 days before the meeting (DGCL 222(b)). From memory; not re-read. The bylaws may set a longer period, so check them first.

## Equity grants
- The board approves the terms of options and other rights, or delegates that within the limits the statute allows (DGCL 157). From memory; not re-read. A grant with no board approval is a flag for counsel.
- The board authorises an issuance of stock and sets the consideration for it (DGCL 152 and 153). From memory; not re-read.

## Minutes
- Minutes record outcomes: who attended, quorum, conflicts declared, each resolution with its vote, and actions. They do not record discussion.
- A minute is a draft until the approving body approves it and the signed copy is on file.

## Sources
