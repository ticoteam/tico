# Read a contract for its renewal terms

When to use: before you state a renewal date, a notice deadline, an uplift, a price basis or a term in a calendar line, a
pack, a review or a note to a person.

Steps:
1. Find the signed version, not the draft or the CRM summary. Record its name, the date signed and where you read it. If only
   a summary exists, say so and treat every date from it as unconfirmed.
2. Read the term: start date, end date, and whether the end date is fixed or set by a renewal.
3. Read the renewal clause. Record: auto-renew or signed; the notice period in days; who the notice goes to; the form it must
   take (in writing, by email, by post); and whether the notice runs from the end date or from the renewal date.
4. Read the price clause. Record the basis (per seat, per unit, percentage of a base, flat), the currency, and any minimum or
   floor. Keep the basis with the price every time you write it.
5. Read the uplift clause. Record whether an increase is allowed, its cap or formula, and the notice it needs.
6. Count the notice deadline. Take the anchor the clause names in step 3 (the term end date or the renewal date), subtract the notice days as the clause counts them (calendar days if it does not say). If the result is not a working day, move it back to the working day before, never forward. Write the deadline and the rule used. If the deadline is within 30 days and no
   decision exists, flag it at the top of the review.
7. Compare with the CRM. Where a date or value differs, record both with their sources and dates. Do not average them and do not
   pick the one you like. Flag the contradiction to the requester.
8. Write each fact with its clause number and the date you read it.

Checks before you finish:
- The renewal date and the notice deadline each have a source and a clause number.
- A deadline is shown as a date, with the rule used to count it.
- Every price shows its basis and any minimum.
- A fact you could not read is marked "unknown", with what you checked.
- A CRM value that contradicts the contract is flagged, not silently replaced.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Larchmont Studio, renewal 2026-11-30. Contract s.9: auto-renews for 12 months unless notice
of change is given 60 days before the renewal date, in writing to the account contact. Deadline 2026-10-01 (60 calendar days).
Uplift: up to 5% on notice of 30 days (s.11). Price basis: per seat per month, $19 minimum. Read from the signed PDF on 2026-09-29;
the CRM shows a 45-day notice, which conflicts with the contract and is flagged."
