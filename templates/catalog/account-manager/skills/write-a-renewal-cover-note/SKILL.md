# Write a renewal cover note

When to use: whenever a renewal pack, an expansion option or a renewal reminder needs a note to the customer.

Steps:
1. Open with the reason and the date in the first sentence: the renewal date, or the notice date if the change must be given
   by then. Example: "Your plan renews on 30 November; this note sets out the options before the 1 October notice date."
2. Name the options in one short paragraph or a short list. Two or three, each with what changes. A price appears only if it
   comes from the requested terms or `knowledge/renewal-rules.md`. A price not yet set is written as a marked gap, not guessed.
3. State what does not change, when it matters to the customer: the term, the features, the support level.
4. Give one next step and a date: a call, a yes by a date, or a question. One ask per note.
5. Sign as the owner who will send it, in the owner's voice the onboarding answers name. No "we are delighted", no "as you know".
6. Count the words. Under 120. If it is longer, the detail belongs in the pack, not the note.
7. Mark the note as a draft on the task. It leaves only when the task asks, mail sending is on for this bot in Tico, and a
   granted Tool allows it.

Checks before you finish:
- Under 120 words.
- The renewal or notice date is in the first sentence.
- Every price is from the stated terms or a marked gap.
- No promise of a feature, a credit, a discount or a date the team has not stated.
- One next step, with a date.
- No customer's private details beyond what the account plan already holds.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Hello [contact name], your studio plan renews on 30 November. Before the 1 October notice date, here
are two options. Keep 30 seats at the current rate. Or move to 45 seats, which fits the class schedule you mentioned on 22
September, at `[price: pricing owner]`. Nothing else changes: the term, the support level and the features stay as they are. Could
we agree on one of these by 25 September? A short call works if that is easier."
