# Count a notice deadline

When to use: whenever you state a notice deadline, a renewal window, or the last day to give notice, in a summary, a calendar
line or a task.

Steps:
1. Name the anchor the clause uses: "before the end of the term", "before the renewal date", "after receipt of notice", or
   "from the date of the notice". Quote the words.
2. Name the period and its unit: calendar days, business days or months. Note whether the clause says "at least", "not less
   than" or "no later than".
3. Name the delivery rule: when notice counts as given (sent, received or deemed received), to whom, and in what form.
4. Count from the anchor in the direction the clause gives: back for "before" anchors, forward for "after receipt" or "from the date of the notice". Write the arithmetic, for example "2027-03-31 minus 60 calendar days = 2027-01-30" or "2026-11-01 plus 30 calendar days = 2026-12-01".
5. If the last day is a weekend or holiday, apply the contract's own rule. If it has none, use the earlier date and say so.
6. Show two dates: the last day to give notice, and an action date (for planning) with the days left.
7. If the clause is silent on the anchor or the unit, flag it for counsel and show the deadline both ways.

Checks before you finish:
- The anchor and the unit are quoted from the clause.
- The arithmetic is written out.
- The delivery rule is stated.
- The weekend rule is stated, or its absence is said.
- Both the last day and the action date are shown, with days left.
- Any ambiguity is flagged, not resolved by guessing.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Clause 9.2: notice 'no later than 60 days before the renewal date', calendar days, in writing
to the supplier's notices address. Renewal date 2027-03-31. 2027-03-31 minus 60 days = 2027-01-30, which is a Saturday. The
contract has no weekend rule, so the last day used is the earlier one, Friday 2027-01-29, and this is flagged. Action date 2027-01-15, 14 days before the last day used."
