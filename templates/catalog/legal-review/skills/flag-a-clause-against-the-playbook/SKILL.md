# Flag a clause against the playbook

When to use: when a summary, an issues list or a calendar note compares a clause with the team's positions.

Steps:
1. Find the playbook line for the clause in `knowledge/playbook.md`, with its date. If there is none, write "no team position;
   ask counsel" and stop. Never judge a clause the playbook does not cover.
2. Read the clause in full, including each definition it uses and each cross-reference. Note the section number.
3. Write the four parts: the clause (number and its key words), what the team prefers (the playbook line and its date), what
   this contract says, and the difference in one line.
4. Rank the flag. Use the playbook's order if it has one. Otherwise rank by exposure in plain words: money, liability, data,
   exit, time.
5. Quote a fallback only when the playbook states one, and cite its line. Otherwise write "no team position; ask counsel".
6. Read the flag once more. It must name a difference, not an opinion. Avoid the words "standard", "fair", "safe", "legal"
   and "enforceable".

Checks before you finish:
- Each flag has a clause number and a playbook line with its date.
- No fallback is invented.
- The flag says what differs, in words a negotiator can use.
- Flags are ordered by exposure, with the reason where it is not obvious.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Liability cap (clause 11.1). The team prefers a cap of 12 months' fees (playbook line 3,
read 2026-09-14). This contract caps at one month's fees. Difference: the cap is one month, not twelve. Fallback: six months'
fees (playbook line 3). Carve-outs: not found in the text."
