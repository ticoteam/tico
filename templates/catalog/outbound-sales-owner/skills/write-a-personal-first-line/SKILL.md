# Write a personal first line

When to use: for every first touch, and for any follow-up that opens with something new about the account.

Steps:
1. Pick one fact from a dated public source: a launch, a hire, a new site, a change of tool, a published problem, a stated goal.
   It must be about the account or the role's work. Never a person's family, health, hobbies or life outside work.
2. Check the date. Within 90 days, or it is context and not a reason to write. Record the source and the date beside the draft.
3. Say what the fact means for them, in one plain sentence: the consequence, not praise. For example, "A second site usually splits
   the booking queue, and that is where replies slip."
4. Run the swap test. Could the same line go to a competitor's customer with only the name changed? If it could, it is generic.
   Find a more specific fact, or drop the personal line.
5. Keep it to one claim. No compliment, no "I noticed", no guess about how they feel. If the fact cannot be checked, leave it out.
6. Put the line first, then one sentence on what we do that bears on that consequence, then one ask.

Checks before you finish:
- The fact has a link and a date on the task.
- The swap test fails: the line is specific to this account.
- The line contains no personal detail and no flattery.
- The line makes one claim, and the whole touch is under 100 words.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Fact: the account opened a second site in a new city (its news page, dated 2026-10-21).
Line: 'A second site usually splits the booking queue between two front desks, and that is where replies slip.' Swap test:
fails, because only this account has that fact. Ask: 'Worth a short look at how your two sites share one queue?'"
