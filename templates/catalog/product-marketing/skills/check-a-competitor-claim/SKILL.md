# Check a competitor claim

When to use: before any comparison, battlecard line, launch copy or sales note says what a competitor does, charges
or lacks.

Steps:
1. List each claim on its own line: a feature, a price, a limit, a licence term, a customer, a number.
2. For each, open the competitor's own page (the pricing page, the docs, the changelog, the terms). A third-party
   article is a lead, not the source.
3. Write the source link and the date you read it: "as of 2026-10-18". Copy the competitor's wording for any
   term they define (their name for a plan, their licence name).
4. If the page shows no date, the claim is "unverified" and it stays out of public copy.
5. Test the claim for three things: is it current, is it comparable (same unit, same plan, same year), and would a
   buyer who checks it find the same thing?
6. Check the words. "Open source" and "source available" are different licences. A claim about switching or
   migration needs a tested path. "Free" and "unlimited" need the limit written beside them.
7. Mark each line: confirmed (with the link and date), changed (with the new wording), or removed.

Checks before you finish:
- Every confirmed claim has the competitor's own link and an as-of date.
- No price appears in a comparison unless the team keeps it current and dates it.
- Where the competitor is better on a line, the line says so.
- Removed claims are listed, so an old draft cannot bring them back.

Example (invented, for illustration): "'Rival X is open source' changed to 'Rival X is source available under its
licence (licence page, as of 2026-10-18)' after the licence page showed a restricted use clause. 'Migrate in one
file' removed: no tested path exists."
