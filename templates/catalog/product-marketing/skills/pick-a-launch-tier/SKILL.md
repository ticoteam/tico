# Pick a launch tier

When to use: when a launch is first proposed, and again when its scope or date changes.

Steps:
1. Name the change in one sentence: what the buyer can do after the launch that they could not do before.
2. Score three things, each as small, medium or large:
   - Reach: how many customers, prospects or users see it or depend on it.
   - Revenue: whether it changes pricing, a plan, a sales motion or a renewal.
   - Strategic weight: whether it opens a new category, a new buyer or a new channel.
3. Take the highest of the three as the tier. Two small scores and one large is still a large launch.
4. Map the tier to its plan from `knowledge/tiers.md`:
   - Tier 1: new product or market. A full plan over eight to twelve weeks, with press work and sales enablement.
   - Tier 2: a significant feature for one segment. Three to six weeks and a targeted channel mix.
   - Tier 3: a small update. A changelog entry and an in-app note.
5. Write the one-line reason: "Tier 2: reaches the mid-size segment (large), changes no price (small), no new buyer
   (small)."
6. Set the date and the lead times the tier needs. Tell the press owner or the PR bot when a Tier 1 launch is set,
   so press work starts in time.
7. Record the tier and reason in `knowledge/launches.md`.

Checks before you finish:
- The tier comes from impact, and the reason names the score for each dimension.
- A Tier 3 launch is not given a campaign plan, and a Tier 1 launch is not given a changelog entry.
- The date leaves enough lead time for the tier's plan, counted back from the date.
- Any change to the tier after it was set is recorded with its reason.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Waitlist for the team plan: Tier 1. Reaches new team buyers (large), changes the
pricing page (medium), opens a second buyer type (large). Plan over ten weeks; press work starts four weeks before
the date; sales hears the brief first."
