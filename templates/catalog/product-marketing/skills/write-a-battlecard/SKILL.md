# Write a battlecard

When to use: when a competitor appears in deals often enough to need one card, or when an existing card is older
than ninety days.

Steps:
1. Start from the competitor's record in the market graph (`hub market show`) and the last three lost or won deals
   against them. A card without deal evidence is marked "no deal evidence yet" at the top.
2. Write who they are in two lines, with the source and its date.
3. Write where they win: the two or three buyers or situations where they are the better choice. Be honest; the
   sales person has to believe the card.
4. Write where the team wins, with the proof and its source.
5. List their top three claims as buyers hear them. For each, write the answer in one or two sentences with its
   sourced proof, and mark any answer that has no proof yet.
6. Write three questions to ask a buyer that reveal fit, and one sign to walk away.
7. Write the proof the team may share with a buyer, and the proof it may not.
8. Put the date checked at the top. Run `skills/check-a-competitor-claim/SKILL.md` on every claim before the card
   is marked ready.

Checks before you finish:
- The card fits one screen. Anything longer goes into the deal notes, not the card.
- Each claim about the competitor has a link and a date.
- The card says where the competitor wins.
- A card older than ninety days is marked stale at the top, and the next refresh is dated.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Rival Y, checked 2026-10-18. Where they win: buyers who need an on-premise install
(their install page). Where we win: setup in one afternoon, with a timed setup log from three customers (2026-09).
Claim: 'they are cheaper.' Answer: their list price is per seat with a 10-seat minimum (pricing page, 2026-10-18); compare
the total for the buyer's seat count. Ask: how many people will use it in the first month? Walk away: the buyer needs
an on-premise install."
