# Launch and positioning method

The standing practice behind the positioning document, the launch brief and the battlecard. Each rule says where it
comes from. Vendor and template sources are used for structure only.

## Positioning, in order

1. Competitive alternatives: what a buyer would do if the product did not exist. This comes first, because a
   product is judged against what it replaces.
2. Unique attributes: what the product has that those alternatives do not.
3. Value: what each attribute means for the buyer, in their measure.
4. Best-fit customers: the buyers for whom that value matters most.
5. Market category: the frame that makes the first four obvious to a buyer.

Each line carries its evidence and the date it was checked. Position only against competitors that appear in real
deals. A position against a name nobody meets wastes the launch.

## Message hierarchy

- Start with one positioning sentence, then three or four pillars, each with one proof point and its source.
- Outcomes before adjectives, and proof before flourish. The press boilerplate leads with the primary value,
  not the features.
- A five-layer stack is a common structure: point of view, value proposition, benefits, proof points, features.
  A message that has no proof point below it is an opinion; say so.
- Review the hierarchy when the category changes or the ideal customer changes, and at least once a year.

## Launch tiers and sequence

- Size by expected business impact (reach, revenue, strategic weight), not by build effort.
- Sequence: positioning and the brief first, then the competitive check, then channel messages and the content
  calendar. Sales and support hear the brief before customers do.
- A launch brief is one page. A launch with no owner two weeks before its date is red.

## Claims about competitors

- Each rival claim links the rival's own page, with an as-of date. A claim with no date is removed.
- Do not put prices in a comparison page unless the team keeps them current and dates them.
- Say where a rival is better, plainly. A comparison that wins every row is not believed.
- Do not claim portability, switching in one step or a migration path unless the path has been tested.
- Words such as "open source" and "source available" are different claims. Check the licence before using either.

## Claims about the product

- A feature is described as shipped only when it is in the release. A planned feature is "coming", with no date
  unless the owner has committed one.
- A number, a customer quote or a result needs its source and its period. A quote is recorded words or marked as a
  draft for the speaker to approve.
