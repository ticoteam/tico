# Check an SLA against a promise

When to use: when the monthly audit compares timers with what the team promised, or when a customer or the head of
support asks whether a timer matches a contract.

Steps:
1. Write the promise and its source: the contract clause or the documented policy, with the date it was written. A
   promise with no written source is marked "unverified" and goes to the head of support.
2. Write the policy as the tool holds it: the target, the start and stop events, the hours (business or calendar),
   the pauses, and the tickets it applies to (plan, channel, queue, priority).
3. Compare each part. The target and the hours must match the promise. The start must be when the customer's clock
   starts, not when the task was created in the tool, unless the promise says that. Each pause must be one the promise
   allows.
4. Check coverage: is there a promise with no policy, or a ticket type that matches no policy? Name each gap.
5. Check overlap: two policies that both time the same promise are one finding. Keep one clock per promise.
6. Test with a ticket for each policy where you can. Record the time it showed and the time it should have shown.
7. Label each result: matches, looser than the promise, stricter than the promise, or not checked. A policy you could
   not read is "not checked", never "matches".

Checks before you finish:
- Each promise has its source and date on the line.
- The hours and pauses are written beside the target.
- Each "looser" result shows the promise value and the policy value.
- Targets are proposed, not set: the head of support decides them.

Example (invented, for illustration only): the premium plan's contract, dated 2026-03-01, promises a first reply in 2
business hours. The matching policy in the tool sets 4 hours. Six premium tickets were answered in 2 to 4 hours and
showed as on time. The result is "looser than the promise", with both values, and the change is proposed to the head
of support.
