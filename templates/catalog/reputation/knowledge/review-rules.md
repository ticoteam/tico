# Review rules and reply practice

The standing rules behind invitations, replies and flags. Platform rules change; each line is dated, and the platform's
own policy page wins over this file. Check the page before a batch.

## Rules that apply to every review

- Fake reviews are banned. In the United States, the FTC's rule on fake and paid reviews (16 CFR Part 465) took effect
  on 21 October 2024. It covers fake or false reviews, buying positive or negative reviews, and misuse of fake signs
  of social influence. Civil penalties apply per violation, and the amount is inflation-adjusted each year. Warning
  letters were sent to ten companies in early 2026 (secondary reporting; check the FTC's guidance and the eCFR text).
- Paying for a review is allowed when the payment does not depend on what the reviewer writes and the review is
  disclosed. Paying for a positive or a negative review is banned.
- Liability for a fake review can depend on whether the business had reason to know it was fabricated. Keep the
  record of who was invited and how.

## Platform rules to check before each batch

- Google Business Profile prohibits offering anything, money, a discount or a gift, for a review, and it prohibits
  rewards for changing or removing a negative review. Secondary reports from February 2026 describe updated wording,
  and some reports describe rules on staff review quotas and on scripted wording. Verify each against Google's
  Prohibited and Restricted Content page before relying on it.
- Google and most app stores allow an unpaid, identical request to every customer. Do not screen customers by how
  they seem to feel.
- Yelp allows no request for reviews at all, and this team does not ask on Yelp.
- Paid honest-review programs on software review sites (G2, the Gartner Digital Markets listings) are run by the
  platform, which pays the reviewer, verifies them and labels the review. Confirm each program's terms on its page.

## Reply practice

- A reply is read by people who were never the reviewer. Write for them.
- BrightLocal's 2026 consumer survey (cited through secondary pages): 80% are likely to use a business that answers
  all its reviews, 42% are unlikely to use one that never replies, and templated replies put off half of consumers.
- GatherUp's January 2025 survey of about 1,200 US consumers: nine in ten treat owner replies as part of good service,
  and 79% of people who left a negative review expect a reply from the business.
- Survey figures come from vendors who sell review tools. Use them as direction, not as a benchmark to report.

## Reading the listings

- A signed-out or limited view of a listing is "blocked" or "partial", never "no reviews". Record which.
- Counts differ between a listing, the team's own page and a search summary. Quote each with its source and date, and do not average them.
- A listing can show N reviews filed and publish fewer. Do not invent rows for the ones with no public text.
- Some listings hide reviews in a second section (for example, "not currently recommended"). Read every section before counting.
- Missing data is not zero. An export that failed is "unknown" in the ledger.

## Rating arithmetic

- An average moves more when it has fewer reviews. Ten reviews averaging 4.8 become 4.45 with one new 1-star review.
  The same 4.8 over 100 reviews becomes 4.76. Report the count next to every average.
- A rating is a count of reviews on one platform on one date. Two platforms are never averaged together.
