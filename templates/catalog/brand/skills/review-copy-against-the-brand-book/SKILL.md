# Review copy against the brand book

When to use: a task attaches copy, a page, a slide, an email or a post and asks "is this on brand?". Also when the monthly audit scores a miss.

Steps:
1. Read the brand card (`hub brand show`) and `knowledge/rulings.md` before you read the piece. Note the surface and the situation: launch, apology, price change, error, routine update.
2. Read the piece once as its audience. Write one line: what the reader will take away, and whether that is what the team means.
3. Read it again line by line. For each line that breaks a rule, quote the rule and the line, then write the smallest change that fixes it.
4. For each line that breaks taste only, mark it "suggestion" and say it is your judgement. A suggestion is not a miss.
5. Check the facts the copy states: product and plan names, prices, dates, claims. Use `hub doc search` for each. A claim with no source is a question for the owner, not a fix.
6. Check any colour or type you can see against the visual files the book links to. Normal text under 4.5:1 against its background is a rule miss; large text under 3:1 is one too.
7. Write the verdict on line one: on brand, on brand with small fixes, or off brand, with the reason in one clause.

Checks before you finish:
- Every change is marked rule or suggestion, and each rule change quotes its rule.
- You did not rewrite a whole paragraph where a line would do.
- Names, prices and dates were checked against a source, or the check is listed as not done.
- The verdict is the first line.

Example (generic): "On brand with small fixes. Line 3 reads 'leverage our robust platform'. Rule: 'leverage' and 'robust' are on the avoid list. Fix: 'Use the platform to...'. Suggestion, not a rule: the second paragraph would land better as one question. Prices and plan names match the pricing page of the same date."
