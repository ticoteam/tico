# Returns practice

Standing reference for how return, exchange and refund requests are decided, with the rules and measures behind them. The
shop's written policy sets the terms, but a statutory consumer right overrides it wherever the right applies, whatever the
policy says (see below). `policy-checks.md` turns the policy into checks. Where this file and the policy disagree, a statutory
right wins; otherwise the policy wins, and the disagreement is written on the ledger line. This is not legal advice. Check the
rules for each market with counsel.

## Order of questions
Ask in this order and write each answer with its source: inside the window (counted from delivery); item eligible (final sale and
personalised items); condition acceptable (photos count); already returned or refunded; who pays the return shipping; refund,
exchange or store credit, and does the policy allow it. The steps are in `skills/check-a-return-against-the-order/SKILL.md`.

## Consumer rights above the policy
- A statutory right sets the minimum. A policy may give more than the law does, never less: a request inside a statutory right
  is not refused on the policy alone.
- European Union, distance sales: under Directive 2011/83/EU a consumer can withdraw within 14 days of receiving the goods,
  without giving a reason. Only the exclusions in Article 16 (such as clearly personalised goods) remove it; a final-sale label
  does not (check at source). The trader must refund all payments within 14 days of being told of the withdrawal. For a withdrawal
  from the whole order, that includes the standard delivery cost. The trader may hold the refund until the goods come back or
  the consumer shows proof of sending them, whichever is earlier (Article 13, as summarised). Under Article 14 the consumer pays
  return postage unless the trader agreed to pay, or did not say beforehand that the consumer would. Check Article 13 and 14 on
  EUR-Lex before promising a date or a cost.
- European Union, withdrawal button: Directive (EU) 2023/2673 (Article 11a) requires a withdrawal button for distance
  contracts concluded through an online interface, where the right applies, from 19 June 2026. National rules vary.
- United States: there is generally no federal right to return an ordinary purchase, so the shop's written policy sets the terms.
  State rules on posting a refund policy, and on some products, vary.

## Refund amounts
- Refund the net amount paid for the item: its pre-discount line amount less its share of any order discount, shared once in
  proportion (`skills/work-out-a-refund-amount/SKILL.md`).
- Original standard delivery cost is refunded on a statutory whole-order withdrawal, and otherwise only where the policy says so.
- Return shipping is deducted only where the policy charges it and no statutory right applies. On a statutory withdrawal, check
  Article 14 before deducting any return cost.
- On a statutory withdrawal no restocking fee applies, flat or percentage; the only deduction the law allows is for handling
  beyond what was needed to check the goods (Article 14(2), check at source). Outside a statutory right, a restocking fee
  applies only where the written policy names one, as a percentage for the case.
- Never refund more for the goods than was paid for them. The delivery refund is capped separately, at the standard delivery
  charge paid.

## Return rates and denominators
- A return rate needs the units shipped of the same product in the same period as its denominator, not orders or sales dollars,
  unless the report says otherwise.
- Report a rate only with about 30 or more units shipped in the period. Otherwise report the count.
- Compare like with like: the same product, the same period, the same channel.

## Industry context (reported figures, not targets)
- NRF with Happy Returns, 2025 Retail Returns Landscape: about $849.9 billion of returns forecast for 2025, 15.8% of sales; online
  returns 19.3% of online sales; about 9% of returns fraudulent. The 2024 figure in the same series was about $890 billion, 16.9%.
- Appriss Retail, 2026 Total Retail Loss benchmark: $706 billion returned in 2025; 14.2% of it (about $100 billion) preventable
  fraud and abuse.
- The two series use different methods and their totals differ by about $144 billion. Do not compare one with the other. Neither
  figure is a target for this shop.
