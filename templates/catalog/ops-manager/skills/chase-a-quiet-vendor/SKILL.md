# Chase a quiet vendor

## When to use

Read when the team asked a vendor something and the agreed wait in `knowledge/vendors.md` has passed with no answer.
Not for a thread where the vendor replied, since the ball is with the team. Not for a vendor who asked the team for a
decision, a price or a signature: that goes to the owner, at the top of the page.

## Steps

1. Read the whole thread: the operations mailbox if it is connected, otherwise the task and `knowledge/vendors.md`.
   Note the date of the team's last message, and the date of the vendor's last reply if there was one.
2. Decide. Follow up only when the team asked and the wait has passed with no answer. If the vendor replied, make a
   task for the owner instead.
3. Check that the question has not been answered anywhere in the thread, the task or the docs. Do not repeat an
   answered question.
4. Write the draft in plain text, under 100 words, with one ask and one date. Say what was asked and when, what is
   needed, and by when.
5. Make the answer easy: name the exact document, a smaller question, or an alternative time. Never write "just checking in".
6. Leave out any price, commitment, cancellation or threat. If the draft needs one, mark the gap and send it to the owner.
7. At the top of the draft, put the recipient, the subject, and the source that shows the thread was quiet, with its
   date. Attach it to the task.
8. Update the vendor's row: last touch, the date the follow-up was drafted, and the next check date.
9. If a second follow-up has gone unanswered, say so on the page and name the person at the team who should call.
   Do not draft a third.

## Checks before you finish

- The draft asks one thing and names one date.
- It has no price, commitment, cancellation or threat, and no date you did not read in a source.
- The thread was read in full, and the quiet period is stated with its dates.
- If the thread could not be read, nothing was drafted, and the page says so.

## Example

Invented example. The team asked a cleaning company for its October schedule on Monday 2026-09-21. The team's wait is
three working days, and the thread shows no reply by 2026-09-28. Draft:

"Hi, on 21 September we asked for the October cleaning schedule. Could you send it by Friday 2 October so we can
confirm the studio hours? Thanks, the operations team."

Recipient: the company's scheduling contact. Source: thread read 2026-09-28; last message 2026-09-21; no reply.
