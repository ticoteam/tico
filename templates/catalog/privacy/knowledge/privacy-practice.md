# Privacy practice

Standing practice for data requests, DPAs and breaches. It is practice for the desk, not legal advice. Check the current text before you rely on any law named here. Each entry names its source.

## Request deadlines
- EU (GDPR Article 12(3)): respond within one month of receipt. The period may be extended by two further months where a request is complex or there are many. The requester must be told of the extension, with reasons, within the first month. Source: Article 12(3) as quoted in regulator decisions and compliance guides, seen in search on 2026-11-01. The EUR-Lex text was not read.
- UK: the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (legislation.gov.uk, ukpga/2025/18) amends UK GDPR Article 12(3). It replaces the fixed one-month wording with an "applicable time period" under a new Article 12A. Search on 2026-11-01 did not confirm when the change took effect. Check the ICO and the legislation before you rely on a day count for a UK request.
- California (Civil Code 1798.130(a)(2)): 45 calendar days from receipt. One extension of up to 45 more days is allowed if reasonably necessary, with notice and reasons within the first 45 days. Sources: practitioner summaries seen in search on 2026-11-01, some citing proposed regulations (section 999.313). Check the final regulations and the current statute. The regulations also expect receipt to be confirmed within 10 business days.
- Any other place: write its rule in this file, with its source, before the first day count.

## Breach notice
- GDPR Article 33: notify the supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to people. Baseline from memory; the Article text was not re-read.
- A Digital Omnibus proposal would change this to 96 hours. Secondary sources seen in search earlier in this build (Mayer Brown, January 2026; Stephenson Harwood, February 2026) said it was still at committee stage. Check its status before you rely on either number. The 72-hour rule is the one in force unless and until a change is adopted.

## Processor contracts
- GDPR Article 28(3) lists what a processor contract must cover: subject matter and duration, nature and purpose, data and people, documented instructions, staff confidentiality, security, subprocessors, help with requests and assessments, deletion or return, audits, and breach help. The checklist in `playbooks/review-a-dpa.md` follows that list. From memory; not re-read in this run.

## Transfers
- EU standard contractual clauses: Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914 of 4 June 2021. From memory; not re-read in this run. Name the mechanism a DPA uses. Do not say that a transfer is adequate.
