# Log breach facts for counsel

When to use: when someone suspects a personal data breach, such as a lost device, data sent to the wrong person, or unexpected access.

Steps:
1. Open a task now, and put the time of awareness in its title: who noticed, when and how. Under GDPR Article 33 the 72-hour clock runs from awareness.
2. Write what happened in the order it was found, with the source of each fact (the message, the log, the person who saw it).
3. List the kinds of data, the approximate number of records and the people affected, where known. Mark anything not known as unknown.
4. List what has been done to contain it, by whom and when.
5. List what is unknown and who could answer it.
6. Name the decision-maker from `knowledge/` setup answers. Send the facts to that person on the task. Do not decide whether the breach must be reported. Do not contact any outside party.
7. Keep a timeline with each entry dated. Update it as facts change.

Checks before you finish:
- The time of awareness is in the task title.
- Every fact has a source. Unknowns are marked as unknown, not guessed.
- The report makes no decision on reportability.
- It names no person beyond what the facts need.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Awareness 2026-10-02 09:15, when a support lead saw a customer's export in a shared folder. Unknown: whether anyone else opened the folder. Contained at 09:40 by removing the sharing link. Decision-maker: Priya, the owner."
