# Renewal practice

How vendor renewals usually go, what the sources say, and where they disagree. Vendor and consultancy figures are not
independent benchmarks. The contract's own text is the number to use. A benchmark never replaces it.

## Notice periods
- Notice periods are stated in days, often 30 to 90. Some are stated in months or years (Zuva, read 2026-11-01).
- Common Paper's benchmark of more than 1,000 cloud agreements, as reported by Bindlegal, puts a 30-day window in about
  84 percent of cases (read 2026-11-01). It is the only sample-based figure found. Check the year and method at source.
- Sources disagree on what is standard. CloudNuro calls 60 days typical for enterprise deals and 90 days the buyer's
  target. Terms.law calls 30 days fair and 90 or more excessive. Both are vendor or legal-marketing sources (read
  2026-11-01).
- Some enterprise platform contracts ask for notice 60 to 90 days before term end, and some ask for 120 (Redress
  Compliance, read 2026-11-01).
- Without written notice inside the window, many contracts roll over, often at the vendor's then-current price list
  (Redress Compliance, read 2026-11-01).
- Practice: count back from the contract's end date by its notice period, and state the rule used. Check whether notice
  must be received or only sent, and by what method. Sort by the deadline, not the end date.

## Price increases at renewal
- Estimates of annual SaaS price increases cluster around 8 to 12 percent. The range recurs across vendor-side and
  consultancy sources, which may cite each other (2025 reports, read 2026-11-01). Treat it as a range of estimates, not a
  verified benchmark.
- Vendorbenchmark's 2025 figure for uncapped deals is 8.4 percent a year, which compounds to 27.4 percent over three
  years. Methodology not stated.
- Vertice's tracker reports an average of 12.3 percent year on year as of September 2025 (read 2026-11-01).
- Negotiated caps average 4.2 percent a year, per Vendorbenchmark (2025). A cap written into a contract is the figure
  to use, not the average.
- CloudNuro reports, from its own observation (2025), 12 to 18 percent per renewal without active vendor management and 4
  to 7 percent with central governance. Not verified.
- Salesforce's August 2025 list-price rise is reported at an average of 6 percent, and its order forms are reported to
  allow renewal uplifts of 8 to 10 percent. Secondary reports; not verified at source.
- Practice: plan with the contract's uplift clause. Where there is none, show the external range with its source and date,
  marked "estimate, not in contract".
