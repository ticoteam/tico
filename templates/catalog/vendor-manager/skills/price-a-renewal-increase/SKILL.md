# Price a renewal increase

When to use: in every renewal brief, and when someone asks what a renewal will cost next term.

Steps:
1. Find the uplift clause in the contract: a fixed percentage, an index (for example a consumer price index), a list price at renewal, or no clause. Record the page and the exact wording.
2. Find any cap on the uplift and its basis (per year, or over the term). A cap written into the contract is the upper figure to use.
3. Calculate next term's price as the current annual cost times one plus the uplift. For an index, state the index and the rate used, with its date and source. With no clause, write "no uplift clause on file" and show the price as unchanged, flagged.
4. For a term longer than one year, compound each year's uplift and show the total over the term, not only the first year.
5. Adjust for seats or volume. For a per-seat price, state the seat count you plan to renew.
6. Show outside estimates separately, if they help. Estimates from vendor and consultancy sources are a range with their source and date, marked "estimate, not in contract". Never write one as the price.
7. Put the number in the brief with its clause and the calculation on one line, so anyone can recheck it.

Checks before you finish:
- Every price names its uplift clause and page.
- The calculation is written out and recomputes.
- Multi-year totals compound each year.
- Outside figures are labelled as estimates, with their source and date.
- A missing clause is "not on file", not an assumed rate.

Example (generic): "Annual cost 31,200. Uplift clause 3.2: up to 8 percent a year, capped (contract p.5). Next year at the cap: 31,200 x 1.08 = 33,696. Two-year term at the cap: 33,696 + (33,696 x 1.08 = 36,391.68) = 70,087.68, shown as 70,088. Outside estimates of 8 to 12 percent a year (2025 vendor-side reports, read 2026-11-01) are shown separately and are not the price."
