# Calculate a notice deadline

When to use: for every contract in the register each week, and before any renewal brief is written.

Steps:
1. Read the clauses in the contract: the end date (or renewal date), the notice period and its unit (days, months or years), and whether the contract renews automatically and for how long. Note the page and clause for each.
2. Where the order form and the contract disagree on a date or period, record both with their pages. Do not pick one silently. Ask the Contracts Manager (`legal-review`) which one governs.
3. Calculate the deadline by counting back from the end date by the notice period. State the rule you used, for example "calendar days, end date not counted". For a period in months, say which day of the month is used.
4. Check whether notice must be received by the deadline or only sent, and by what method (email, letter, portal). If the clause says received, the deadline is the day it must arrive.
5. If the deadline has passed, the contract has usually renewed. Say so plainly, with the new end date if the clause gives it. If the clause gives no new date, write "not on file". Do not calculate a deadline for a term that has already started.
6. Mark the contract by its deadline: **open now** when it falls inside the window (default 90 days), **urgent** inside 30 days, **passed** when it has gone. Sort the list by deadline.
7. Write the deadline on the register row with its clause and page. A deadline with no clause behind it is "not on file", never an estimate.

Checks before you finish:
- Each deadline names the clause and the page it came from.
- The counting rule is stated.
- Receipt or sending is recorded.
- A passed deadline says so and shows the new term, or "not on file".
- Nothing was calculated from a vendor email or an invoice alone.

Example (generic): "Vendor X: end 2026-12-18, notice 60 days (contract p.2, clause 4.1, read 2026-09-29). Notice must be received by email. Deadline 2026-10-19, 20 days from the read date: urgent. The order form gives 30 days. The contract's 60 days is used, and legal-review is asked which governs."
