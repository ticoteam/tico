# Review practice

What the research and practice say about performance feedback, rating differences and hard conversations, and how this bot
applies it. Written for managers and the HR owner. It shapes packs, calibration sheets and conversation notes. It never decides
an outcome for a person.

## Feedback helps on average, and sometimes hurts

Kluger and DeNisi (1996) analysed studies of feedback interventions. The mean effect on performance was positive (d about .41),
but about a third of the interventions lowered performance. Later reviews summarise their explanation as this: feedback that
pulls attention toward the self (a person's traits or worth) does worse than feedback that points at the task and the standard.

Applied here: packs and guidance cite the task, the standard and the result. A draft that describes a person's character or
style is flagged for rewording before it reaches the manager.

Source status: abstract and secondary summaries read; the full text was not.

## Ratings vary by rater, and a new scale alone does not fix it

Landy and Farr (1980) reviewed performance-rating research. As later work cites it, changing the rating format alone made
little difference to rating quality; the rater's understanding of the job and the standard mattered more.

Applied here: the bot asks for written definitions and dated examples before a rating is compared with others. It does not
propose a new scale.

Source status: citation and secondary summary read; the full text was not.

## Conversation structure: Situation, behaviour, impact

The Center for Creative Leadership teaches the Situation-Behavior-Impact (SBI) model: when and where it happened, what was done
or said in terms a witness could check, and the effect on the work, people or customers. A guidance note adds the expectation,
the support offered and a follow-up date. The structure keeps the talk on the work. It does not replace the employee relations
route for conduct, health or leave.

Source status: CCL published guidance, read through a secondary summary.

## Conventions this bot uses (house rules, not research)

- Each rating point cites at least one dated example, so calibration has something to test.
- A distribution is shown only for groups of five or more. Vendor help pages for people-analytics tools use five as the minimum
  group size for shared results, because smaller groups can identify individuals.
- Calibration sheets carry no pay data. Pay sits in its own cycle.
- Ratings are compared with the team's written definition, not with the bot's own view of good performance.

## Open questions

- No benchmark for review completion rates was found in this run. Do not state one. The team's own cycle history is the baseline.
- How long a conversation note should be is the team's choice. The default is one page.
