# Prioritisation practice

Standing reference for how product decisions, roadmaps and scores are framed. This team's own scale, units and roadmap live in `scoring.md` and `roadmap.md`; this file holds general practice and dated sources. Where a source and this team's practice disagree, the team's practice wins for this team, and the difference is noted.

## Score to rank, not to decide

- Reach × impact × confidence ÷ effort (RICE, from Intercom) ranks candidates on one scale. The score orders the candidates; the owner decides.
- A score is only as good as its weakest input. A guessed effort or a guessed reach lowers the confidence, and the score shows the guess.
- The impact scale used here (3 massive, 2 high, 1 medium, 0.5 low, 0.25 minimal) is the RICE scale.

## Outputs and outcomes

- A feature shipped is an output. The change in what people do, or in a number the business watches, is the outcome. Perri (2018) argues for organising product work around outcomes. Every proposal names the outcome it moves; one that moves none is parked.

## Continuous discovery

- Torres (2021) recommends a customer contact every week and an opportunity solution tree: each opportunity ties to an outcome, and solutions are tested before they are built. Secondary summary; check the book before quoting it.

## One-way and two-way doors

- Bezos (2015, shareholder letter): reversible decisions can be made quickly, by fewer people, on less information; irreversible ones deserve more evidence and more care. Some sources date the letter wrongly. It is 2015.

## Decisions on record

- Nygard (2011, "Documenting Architecture Decisions") proposes a short record: title, status, context, decision, consequences. The decision log here adds who decided, the evidence, and what would reopen the decision.
