# Write an app store reply

When to use: for each store review that needs a public reply, on either app store only.

Steps:
1. Read the whole review and note the one point that matters most: the crash, the charge, the wait for support, the feature
   that is missing. Note the store and the review's date.
2. Open the reply with one line that names that point, in the reviewer's own terms where the review gives them.
3. Say what the team has done or will do, in one sentence. If the team cannot say yet, say that the report has been passed
   on and that the team will respond through the route in step 4.
4. Give one route: the in-app help or a support address the team owns. Do not put an account, an amount, a payment or a
   dispute detail in the reply.
5. Close without a promise the team has not approved: no refund, credit, date or feature in public, and no "we will fix it"
   unless the product owner approved those words.
6. Check the length. Keep a routine reply short, and a serious one short enough that the route still reads at the end.
7. Check the store's current reply rules in `knowledge/store-rules.md`, including any rule about naming the reviewer and any
   rule about incentives. If the rules are not recorded or are older than the last check, say so and do not draft.
8. Save the draft to `knowledge/drafts/reply-<store>-<review-date>.md` with the review's id and the point it answers. A
   serious or legal review is not drafted here: it goes to the owner as a question.

Checks before you finish:
- The reply answers the point the review made, not a generic one.
- No private detail, no argument, and no blame on a named person.
- No promise of money, a feature or a date the team has not approved.
- Two replies in the same batch do not repeat the same wording. Each names its own point.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Thank you for telling us the card screen closes when you open it on 3.8.2. We have
passed the report to the team with your device model. If it happens again, the help in the app can reach us directly." The
reply makes no promise, gives one route, and is under 60 words.
