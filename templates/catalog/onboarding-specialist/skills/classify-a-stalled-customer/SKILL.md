# Classify a stalled customer

When to use: on the weekly board, for every customer with no progress past the stuck rule, or when a person asks why a customer is behind.

Steps:
1. Check the status fields and any change after the last note first. A customer marked lost, paused or cancelling is not a stall; it goes to the account owner.
2. Find the last progress with its date and source. If there is none, say so: "no progress recorded since signing".
3. Check for a booked step: a call or visit with its date. A booked step is not an abandoned one. Mark it booked until it is completed or missed.
4. Check for a waiting state: a step the customer owes, with a date. Mark it waiting on customer until that date, then overdue on customer, with the last touch.
5. If the blocker is our step, our fix or a person on our side, mark it stuck on us, with the owner and a date.
6. If nobody on either side is named for the next step, mark it no owner and take it to the top of the board.
7. Record the state, the date it started and the source in the plan's progress log. Do not record an absence of usage as "no usage" unless the usage source is confirmed to cover this account; record it as unknown.

Checks before you finish:
- Each stalled customer has one state, a date and a source.
- A booked step or a waiting state is not counted as stuck.
- An unconfirmed usage source is recorded as unknown, not zero.
- Status changes after the last note were checked.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Northside Dance: overdue on customer, not stuck on us. Waiting on the class spreadsheet, owed by 2026-09-17 (asked on the kickoff call 2026-09-10). Last touch 2026-09-24, unanswered. Owner: the account owner. Next step: message ready on the board, for 2026-09-29."
