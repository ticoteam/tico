# Onboarding practice

Standing reference for moving a signed customer to first value. It holds what is known about the practice and where it comes
from. The team's milestones, target durations and stuck rule live in `milestones.md`, written at setup. Where the two disagree,
the team's rules win and the disagreement is written in the customer's plan.

## Milestones follow outcomes
- Four to six milestones, each one a state the customer can show: their data is in, their people are invited, a first real
  transaction happened, a named person has used the product once without help.
- First value is the first outcome the customer said they bought, not a setup step. Sign-up is not first value.
- A milestone is done with evidence: a call, a ticket, a customer message or a usage reading. Never because the plan's date passed.

## The handoff: six items to check
Read every new deal against these before kickoff:
1. Who signed, and who will use the product day to day.
2. What they are replacing, and where their data lives today.
3. Their goal, in their words, from a call or an email.
4. Every promise sales made, with its source: date, integration, training, data move.
5. The operating fields: the day-to-day settings, the schedule or inventory owner, and who approves changes. Handoffs often fill
   the account and payment fields but rarely these.
6. A named human on each side for each milestone.

A missing item is a gap on the plan, never a guess.

## Go-live blockers
Check these before a go-live date is set, not on the day:
- Regulated prerequisites: a licence, permit or registration the customer's market or product requires. It is on file with its
  date and source; a customer's statement is not a copy.
- One live copy: when the same item exists in two places (a listing, a catalogue entry, a booking page), only one stays live.
  Two live copies cause duplicate bookings or orders.
- Access: a customer who says they cannot get in stays open until they confirm, in writing, that it works.
- Ownership: the account owner on each side is named. A change of managing party has an explicit step that removes the old
  party, with the customer's approval.

## Stall states
Classify each stuck customer as one of these, rather than "stuck":
- Waiting on customer, with a date: they owe a step by a stated date. Shown as waiting until then.
- Booked step: a call or visit is scheduled. A scheduled step is not an abandoned one.
- Overdue on customer: the date passed with no reply. Name the last touch.
- Stuck on us: our step or fix is the blocker. Name the owner and the date.
- No owner: nobody on either side is named for the next step. This goes to the top of the board.

## Silence is not inactivity, and unknown is not zero
- No login or usage reading is not "no usage" until the usage source is confirmed to cover the account. Record it as unknown.
- A stall check reads later status changes too. A customer may be marked lost or paused after the last progress note.

## Two stages, marked separately
Account setup (the customer's records and access exist) and operating readiness (the customer uses the product) are different
stages. Account created, login ready and the account owner's acceptance are three separate states; mark each one.

## Timing: no target from vendor benchmarks
Vendor benchmarks for time to first value disagree, and their samples are often unstated. They are not targets. Use the team's
own history instead: once there are ten customers of a size, use the median of those ten.
- Userpilot (2025), a vendor benchmark: average time to value for B2B SaaS about 1 day 12 hours; sample and method not stated.
- Rocketlane (year not stated on the page): fewer than 20% of projects finish on time.
- OnRamp (2026), a vendor guide: describes under 14 days as top-performing.
