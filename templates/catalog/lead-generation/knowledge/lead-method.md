# Lead method

The standing rules behind the weekly lead generation review. `qualified.md` holds the definition Sales agreed. `sources.md`
holds each source's figures. This file says why each rule is there, so each run does not re-derive it. Every figure carries
its source and the year it was read. Check it at its source before you quote it.

## Counting leads

- A lead is one company or person who gave a business contact through one source, in one week. Duplicates inside the week
  are one lead; the same person in a later week is a repeat, counted separately and labelled.
- Qualified means the definition in `qualified.md` was met and Sales accepted the lead. Submitted, received and qualified
  are three counts. Report the one the review asks for, and say which.
- Report the four-week trend beside each source. A source with fewer than about five qualified leads in four weeks is too
  small to read. Say so, and do not rank it.
- Source attribution is first touch as the tracking records it, following the convention `marketing-ops` keeps. A blank
  source is unknown. Do not reassign it.

## Hand-off quality

- Sales' feedback is the measure. Each lead is accepted, rejected, or sent back for more information, with the reason in
  Sales' words where possible.
- The reject pattern by source matters more than the reject rate. Ten rejects with one reason are a targeting problem;
  ten rejects with ten reasons are noise. Say which.
- A lead nobody has reviewed within the agreed time is "awaiting Sales". It is not accepted, and not rejected.

## Lead magnets and forms

- One useful thing per magnet: a checklist, a template, a short calculator. The promise on the page must match what it delivers.
- Ask only for the fields the follow-up uses. Each extra field lowers the rate of completed forms, and a field the team does
  not use is a field it must explain under data protection rules.
- Draft the thank-you page with the magnet. Follow-up emails belong to Email Marketing (`email-marketing`), not to this bot.
- Every claim on a magnet keeps its basis and its date, as the team's claims list states them. A number that cannot be sourced
  becomes a marked gap for the owner.

## Target lists from public sources

- Use public business sources: company sites, trade directories, event exhibitor lists, public filings where they exist.
  Record the source and the date for each row.
- Record the company and the role. Do not record a home address, a personal phone number, a personal email address, or anything
  about a private person that the source does not already show.
- Fit is a reason, not a score. Each row names the fact that makes it a fit, from a dated source.
- A row is marked "to verify" until a person or the SDR has checked the contact is the right person. A list is not a contact.

## Outreach limits

- A bulk message needs a person's approval for that list, the consent basis written down, and an opt-out in every message.
- In the United States, the CAN-SPAM Act (15 U.S.C. 7701 and following) requires a working opt-out, a valid physical postal address and
  honest subject lines for commercial email. Check the current text before you quote it.
- In the European Union and United Kingdom, data protection rules set the basis for contacting people, and the rules on
  business-to-business email differ by country. Do not state a rule from memory; name the country and the rule to a person.
- A person who has asked to stop is removed from every list at once, and the removal is recorded with its date.

## Decision table

| Situation | Action |
| --- | --- |
| A source has fewer than five qualified leads in four weeks | Too small to read. Say so. Do not rank it. |
| Sales rejects most leads from one source with one reason | Propose a targeting change to the owner. Do not change the definition yourself. |
| A list would include someone who asked to stop | Remove them. Record the removal. Tell the owner. |
| A source needs money | Propose it with the cost. A person approves the spend. |
