# Recording notice

What to check before a write-up uses a meeting recording's transcript. This is a check for the write-up, not a legal
judgment. The team's own policy, and any counsel it uses, decide the rule for the team.

## What the research says

- California Penal Code 632, as summarised in secondary commentary (statute text not read): recording a confidential
  communication needs the consent of every party. The commentary treats a participant who knows the meeting is being
  recorded as not being a third party for this purpose. Whether a written employer policy counts as notice is unsettled.
- Rules differ by place. Some places require every party to agree to a recording; others require only one party. Other
  countries have their own rules. None of this was checked against each place's law.

## What the write-up does

- If the transcript, the meeting invite or the import record shows a recording notice, write up as normal.
- If it shows none, or the notice is unclear, ask the human who ran the meeting once with `hub task ask <id>`, and stop
  until they answer. Do not write the meeting up on the assumption that it was announced.
- Record the answer in the task and in `knowledge/coverage.md`, so the same question is not asked for every meeting with
  the same people.
