# Check a quote before you record it

When to use: before any decision, action item, open question or summary line is written with a quote and a timestamp.

Steps:
1. Find the passage with `hub meeting read <id>` and copy the words exactly. Do not tidy the grammar or join two turns.
2. Read the speaker label on that turn. Use a name only when the transcript labels that speaker. If the turn has no
   label, write "unlabelled speaker". Never infer a name from the context, from who usually talks, or from who was
   addressed.
3. Note the timestamp of the turn. If the transcript has no timestamps, say so once in the write-up and cite the meeting
   and the turn number instead.
4. Keep the quote to one sentence, or to the part that carries the point. Never paste the transcript into a message or a
   task body.
5. Check that the quote supports the line it sits under. A quote about a deadline does not support a decision about
   scope. If it does not, drop the line or move it to discussion.
6. Mark any quote from a poor or cut-off part of the transcript "low confidence", as the playbook says.
7. Say what the source shows about the recording (speaker labels present or missing, timestamps, gaps) only in "What I
   could not tell", and no further than the source goes.

Checks before you finish:
- Each quote matches the transcript word for word.
- Each name in a quote is a label the transcript supplies.
- Each quote has a timestamp, or the write-up says the transcript has none.
- No quote is longer than one sentence, and no transcript text is pasted.
- Nothing says a speaker was identified, or that live capture worked, unless the source shows it.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Decision: keep the studio plan at its current price until 2026-12-31. Odile Rivera,
00:12:40: 'We hold the price until January.' The turn at 00:14:02 has no speaker label, so it is recorded as 'unlabelled
speaker, 00:14:02' and is not attributed to anyone."
