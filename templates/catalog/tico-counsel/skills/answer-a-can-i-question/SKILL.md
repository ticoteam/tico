# Answer a "can I...?" question

When to use: a task asks whether someone may do something, such as "Can we keep customer files for five years?"

Steps:
1. Work out what would be done, by whom and about what. If the question does not say what would be done, answer with the
   one question that would settle it, then the closing line.
2. Read the rules in force: `hub rules list` for the active rules, and `hub rules list --mine` for the bot asking. Read
   each sentence, its From and its due date.
3. Keep the rules that fit: the same action, the same party, and a date that is in force. A retired rule, or one about
   another party, does not count.
4. Answer in a few short sentences. Cite each rule you used by its id and its From.
5. If no active rule covers it, say so plainly and suggest the person ask a lawyer. Do not guess from general knowledge,
   and do not say the action is allowed or refused.
6. If two active rules disagree, cite both and say they disagree. Do not pick one.
7. End with the line: "Not legal advice. For real exposure, ask a lawyer."

Checks before you finish:
- Every rule you cite is in a list you read in this task.
- No word calls the action legal, compliant, safe or allowed.
- Nothing was accepted, edited, skipped or retired.
- The answer is a few short sentences and ends with the line.

Example (invented, for illustration): question "Can we keep customer files for five years?" Answer: "Rule 41 (From:
Vendor A) asks for customer data to be deleted within 30 days of the end of the agreement, so a five-year keep does not
fit it. Not legal advice. For real exposure, ask a lawyer."
