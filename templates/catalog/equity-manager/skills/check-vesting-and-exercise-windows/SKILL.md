# Check vesting and exercise windows

## When to use
In the monthly cap table check, when a person asks what vests soon, and when someone has left and the question is whether they can still exercise.

## Steps
1. For each grant, read the schedule from its letter: start date, cliff in months, total months, the vesting interval after the cliff (usually monthly), any acceleration term as written, and the exercise window after leaving.
2. Pick the check date. List the vesting events on or before it, using the interval in the letter: the cliff first, then one event per interval after it, up to the end of the term.
3. Vested shares: zero before the cliff. Otherwise granted shares times the share of the term covered by the events reached: the cliff covers its months at once, and each later event covers its own share. Round down to whole options. For a leaver, no event after the documented termination date counts, unless the letter's acceleration term applies. Show the working.
4. Vests in the next 90 days: each tranche whose date falls between the check date and the check date plus 90 days, with holder, date and count. A leaver's grant has no tranche after the termination date.
5. Leavers: for each, the date they left, the exercise window in the plan, and the last day to exercise. Incentive stock options keep their tax status only if the holder exercises within three months after leaving, under IRC §422(a)(2), unless a longer rule for disability or death applies. A longer window generally turns the option into a non-qualified one for tax. Flag that to counsel and do not advise the holder.
6. Check the totals: vested plus unvested equals granted, for every grant. No grant vests after its term ends.
7. Write the result: vested per grant with the working, the 90-day list, the leavers with their last exercise day, and any grant that does not reconcile.

## Checks before you finish
- Each vested figure has its working, the cliff rule is applied, and no event after a leaver's termination date counts.
- Tranche dates come from the letter, not from a rule of thumb. Where the letter is missing, say the schedule is assumed.
- Leaver dates are checked against the termination record, and the exercise window is the one in the plan, not a default.
- The 90-day window is stated with both its start and its end.
- You did not tell anyone whether they may exercise. You report the dates and the question for counsel.

## Example (invented)
Grant G-09: 4,800 options, start 2024-03-01, 48 months, 12-month cliff, monthly after the cliff. Check date 2026-10-05.

- Events reached: the cliff on 2025-03-01 (12 months, 1,200), then 19 monthly events of 100 from 2025-04-01 to 2026-10-01 (1,900). Vested: 3,100, which is 4,800 x 31 / 48.
- G-09, next 90 days (to 2027-01-03): 100 options on 2026-11-01, 2026-12-01 and 2027-01-01. Total 300.
- Leaver: the holder of G-10 (4,800 options, same schedule) left 2026-09-15. Vesting stops there: 30 months, 3,000 options, vested. No November to January tranche vests on G-10.
- G-10 exercise window: the plan allows 90 days, so the last day to exercise is 2026-12-14. Separately, the ISO statutory limit is three months after leaving, which is 2026-12-15 (IRC §422(a)(2)). Counsel confirms whether G-10 is an ISO and which date governs.
