# Compare plans on total cost

When to use: before open enrollment, when a plan changes at renewal, or when a task asks how two plans differ. The result
is shared, so it has to hold up on its own.

Steps:
1. Collect each plan's figures from its document: premium per pay period by coverage tier, deductible, out-of-pocket maximum,
   visit and prescription cost, the network, and any HSA or FSA it pairs with. Write the plan year and page beside each
   figure. A figure you cannot find is a gap, not an estimate.
2. If the plan has a Summary of Benefits and Coverage, read its coverage examples first and reuse the same case in the table.
3. Set three use levels and state them: routine care only (a few office visits, a few prescriptions); one planned event (a
   procedure the plan's example shows, or a stated cost); and a major event that reaches the out-of-pocket maximum.
4. For each plan and level: the premium for the year for the employee's tier, plus the cost sharing for that use, capped at
   the out-of-pocket maximum. Show the arithmetic in one line under the table.
5. Write what the table does not show: provider and drug network, prior authorisation, services the plan excludes. One line
   each.
6. Add the how, not the which: the enrollment window (from `skills/count-a-life-event-window/SKILL.md`), the default if
   nobody chooses (the plan rule), and who answers personal questions. Do not label any plan "best for" anyone.

Checks before you finish:
- Every figure has its plan, page and plan year.
- Every assumption is written in the table's note.
- Totals are rounded and labelled as estimates for the stated use.
- The table has no recommendation, and no line says which plan suits a person.
- Any figure marked as a gap is named in the note.

Example (invented plans A and B, numbers for illustration only): assumptions are 6 office visits at a copay and 2
prescriptions a year, one planned $4,000 procedure allowed by the plan, and 20 percent coinsurance after the deductible for
both plans. Plan A: premium $1,200, deductible $1,500, visit copay $25, prescription $10. Plan B: premium $800, deductible
$3,000, visit copay $40, prescription $10. Routine care only: A $1,370, B $1,060. One planned procedure: A $3,200, B $4,000.
Not shown: whether the named clinic is in each network, which the broker can confirm. Replace the invented figures with each
plan's own before anyone uses the table.
