# Benefits practice

Standing reference for life-event windows, plan comparisons and plan questions. A plan document governs: where it says
more than this file, the plan wins. Each rule carries its source and the date it was read. The US rules here apply to
US plans only. Other countries need their own reference.

## Windows

- **A life event opens a window; the plan document sets its length.** Federal special enrollment rules for employer group
  health plans give a person at least 30 days after a qualifying event to request enrollment. The events include loss of
  other coverage, marriage, birth, and adoption or placement for adoption. A plan may allow more time, never less. Loss of
  coverage for unpaid premiums or for-cause termination is not a qualifying event. (DOL compliance guidance, 29 CFR
  2590.701-6; summary read 2026-11-01. The regulation text itself was not read.)
- **Count from the start point the plan names, not the day it was reported.** That is usually the event date; where the plan
  counts from the notice, it is the notice date. Record the counting rule with its page: calendar or working days, and which
  start point.
- **COBRA.** The first premium after electing is due at least 45 days after the election (DOL, COBRA employer FAQ, read
  2026-11-01). The other COBRA clocks (the employer's notice to the administrator, the election notice and the election
  period) depend on who runs the plan and on its terms. Not verified in this run. Check them against the DOL model notices
  before a deadline depends on them.
- **Changes in the middle of a plan year.** Most plans allow a change to elections only after a qualifying event and within
  the window. The plan document lists which changes each event allows. A change that does not match its event goes to a
  human, not into the change log as done.

## Comparisons

- **Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC).** US group and individual plans give a standard summary: a uniform layout of no
  more than four double-sided pages (eight sides), type no smaller than 12-point, and worked coverage examples such as a pregnancy. The rules are in 45
  CFR 147.200 and 29 CFR 2590.715-2715. The HHS SBC fact sheet (June 2021) describes the format; read 2026-11-01. The
  examples show each plan's cost for the same case, so start a comparison there.
- **Total cost of care (team method, not a published source).** Premium is one part of what a person pays. For each plan,
  add the employee's premium for the year, then the cost at three levels of use: routine care only, one planned event, and a
  major event. Cost sharing stops at the plan's out-of-pocket maximum. State each assumption in the table. A plan with a lower
  premium and a higher deductible can cost more in a year with an event.
- **Plain words.** Define premium, deductible, coinsurance, copay, out-of-pocket maximum and network once each, in the plan's
  own terms where it defines them.

## The line with a person's case

- A general question ("what does the plan say about physical therapy?") can be answered from the page. A question about
  whether a treatment or claim is covered for a named person goes to the broker or HR owner.
- Health detail stays out of every file. Dependants appear as a count.
