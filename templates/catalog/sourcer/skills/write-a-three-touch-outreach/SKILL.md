# Write a three-touch outreach

When to use: for each person who has passed the check, before any first message or follow-up.

Steps:
1. Find one specific piece of the person's public work that matches a required criterion: a project, a talk, a write-up, a
   repository. If there is not one specific thing, do not write to the person. A generic note does worse than none.
2. Touch one, on day 0, under 90 words:
   - the specific work, in one line, and why it caught your eye;
   - the role, in one line: what the person would do first;
   - the pay range and the location or remote rule, where the team allows them;
   - one easy question, such as "open to a 20-minute call next week?".
   Sign as the sender named in `state.md`. No flattery, no "exciting opportunity", no pressure.
3. Touch two, on day 5, under 60 words: something new. A line from the hiring manager on the problem, a detail about the team,
   or a link the team published. Never "just bumping this".
4. Touch three, on day 12, under 40 words: close the loop politely and leave the door open ("If the timing is wrong, no reply
   needed"). After this, stop and log it.
5. Stop at any point on a "no". Log it the same day and write no more.
6. Read all three as the recipient. Check each claim is accurate and nothing is promised: no pay beyond the posted range, no
   role, no interview.
7. Put the drafts on the task with the planned dates. A message is sent only when a person has turned mail sending on in Tico
   and the task asks for it and a granted Tool allows it. Record the actual send date in `knowledge/contact-log.md` after it goes.

Checks before you finish:
- Touch one is under 90 words and names one specific piece of work.
- Touch two adds something new and is under 60 words. Touch three is under 40 words.
- No promise of pay, role, interview or outcome.
- The contact log holds the planned dates, and the sequence stops after touch three or any "no".

Example (generic, invented, first touch): "Hi Priya, your write-up on retrying failed backfills in batch jobs is the clearest I
have read on it. We are hiring a data engineer to run our nightly pipelines, starting with the same problem. The range is
$130,000 to $150,000, remote in US time zones. Open to a 20-minute call next week?" (under 90 words, one ask)
