# Sourcing practice

Standing reference for finding and approaching people. Figures carry their source and the year they were read. Most
reply-rate figures come from recruiting vendors, use different denominators, and disagree. Treat them as directional, quote
them only with their source, and measure this team's own replies in `knowledge/searches/` before you rely on any of them.

## Reply rates (vendor figures, read 2026-11-01)

- Pin's analysis of more than four million recruiting messages (2026) puts the average cold-email reply rate near 5% and the
  average LinkedIn message reply rate near 17%.
- Noon's data on 844,234 sequences (August 2025 to July 2026) reports a 16.6% overall reply rate, and 18.8% when the sequence
  starts on LinkedIn.
- Belkins (20 million-plus outreach attempts, date not confirmed in the read): personalised connection requests drew a 9.36%
  reply rate against 5.44% without a personal note.
- Woodpecker (2025 cold-email analysis): a message with at least one personal element beyond the name drew about 17% more
  replies than a templated one.
- The gain from personalisation is consistent across vendors, but each vendor's size of gain differs. Measure this team's
  gain on this team's own sends.

## Message length (sources conflict)

- LinkedIn's 2025 guidance, as quoted by a recruiting platform, favours InMails of 50 to 70 words.
- Boomerang's 2024 email study, as quoted by the same kind of source, found 75 to 125 words drew the most replies.
- The template's first message is under 90 words, which sits between the two ranges. That is a team choice, not a finding.
  Test it on this team's own replies.

## Notice to people found in public sources

- The GDPR, Article 14, requires a controller who obtains a person's data from another source to tell that person about the
  processing. The default outer limit is one month. The limit is earlier if the data is used to contact the person or is
  passed on before then. Exceptions exist in Article 14(5). Read the regulation's text and the national regulator's guidance
  before you rely on an exception.
- At least one recruiting vendor's tool deletes sourced profiles that were not contacted within a month. That is a vendor
  setting, not the law.
- Naming the source in the notice is common practice in recruiting guides (for example, the profile page, the talk, or the
  repository). Use the source line that `skills/check-a-person-before-outreach/SKILL.md` records.
- Whether public profiles can be used for sourcing at all is a lawful-basis question, not a settled rule. Get legal advice
  for the team's own use. Do not state that public visibility makes the use lawful.
- The sources read here were vendor blogs and help pages. No regulator page was read for this file. Check the EDPB, the ICO or
  the national authority that applies to the team's people before relying on any timing.

## Search

- Start with the places where the work is shown (talks, repositories, write-ups), not with the places most people use.
- A search that returns profiles without the criteria is a finding, not a failure. Record it, then change the place.
- Keep each string and its yield. The search file is the only memory the next week's run has.
