# Onboarding practice

What the research and practice say about onboarding new employees, and how this bot applies it. Written for the HR owner and
managers. It shapes checklists, the 30, 60 and 90 day check-ins and the questions the bot asks managers. It does not set goals
for anyone.

## Four parts of a good onboarding

Bauer (2010), in the SHRM Foundation's guidelines "Onboarding New Employees: Maximizing Success", describes four parts of
onboarding: compliance (the paperwork, policies and legal requirements), clarification (what the role is and what good work looks
like), culture (how things are done here, the values and the unwritten rules) and connection (relationships with colleagues and
the manager). Compliance is the part most teams already do. Clarification, culture and connection are the parts most often left
to chance.

Applied here: every checklist has items in all four parts. A checklist with only compliance items is shown as incomplete.

## Clarity and connection carry most of the early weeks

Bauer's guidance ties early onboarding to role clarity, to knowing who to ask, and to building relationships early. The guidance
treats these as what new hires need first, and paperwork as a prerequisite rather than the goal.

Applied here: the first-week items name a person to ask (a buddy or the manager) and a first real piece of work. The 30, 60 and 90
day check-ins ask what the new hire can do alone and what they still need to ask about.

## What the bot does not know

- No benchmark for the right number of check-ins, or the right length of a first week, was found in this run. The 30, 60 and 90 day
  points are the team's default, and the team may change them in `knowledge/onboarding-base.md`.
- Whether a team's onboarding improves retention is not claimed here.

## House rules (not research)

- Checklist items are one line: what, owner role, due date. Owner and date are required before an item is shown as open.
- The manager owns the 30, 60 and 90 day goals. The bot proposes questions, never goals.
- Records about a new hire carry role, start date, manager and a reference or a first name. No salary, medical or family detail,
  identity number or home address.
