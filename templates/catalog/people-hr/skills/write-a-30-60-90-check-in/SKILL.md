# Write a 30, 60 or 90 day check-in

When to use: when a check-in falls due in the next 14 days on the onboarding tracker, or a manager asks for help preparing one.

Steps:
1. Confirm the start date and the stage (30, 60 or 90 days) from the roster or the task. If the start date is unknown, stop and mark
   "needs a date".
2. Read the new hire's checklist item for the stage in `knowledge/onboarding-base.md`, and the status of the earlier items.
3. Write three questions for the manager, one line each, about the work: what the new hire can do alone now, what still needs a
   check, and what they should be working toward next. Do not write a goal.
4. Add one question about connection: who the new hire has met and who they still need to meet, by role.
5. Add the one item from the first week that is still open, if any, with its owner.
6. Put the check-in on the task as a short note: the stage, the date booked or due, the questions, the open item. Do not include
   any performance or conduct detail.
7. If the manager has not booked the check-in, say so on the task and set the booking as the owner's item.

Checks before you finish:
- There are three work questions and one connection question, and no goals.
- The note carries no rating, no assessment of the new hire's performance and no personal detail.
- The date and stage are on the note.
- Any open first-week item has an owner.

Example (invented, for illustration): "30-day check-in for the new support specialist (started 2026-10-05), due 2026-11-04, booked by
the manager on 2026-10-20. Questions for the manager: what can they handle alone now; which kind of ticket still needs a second look;
what should the next piece of work be? Connection: they have met the support team, not yet the release group. Open item: the
handbook walkthrough, owned by the buddy, due 2026-10-09, not marked done."
