# Decide whether a course earns its place

When to use: when a plan, a manager or a person proposes a course, or a plan has a course in it that needs a second look.

Steps:
1. Name the skill the course builds, from the level guide. If the course does not build a named skill, the answer is "leave to work"
   unless the requester gives a skill and a reason.
2. Ask what work alone will not teach. A course earns its place when the gap is a body of knowledge or a certification the job
   needs (a security standard, a legal duty, a tool's technical foundation). It does not earn its place to fill a gap that a
   stretch project or a colleague would close.
3. Check the practice and feedback. A course that only presents content is awareness. If it has no exercise, assessment or
   follow-up, say so and suggest pairing it with a practice block.
4. Check the cost in the course's own terms: price, length in hours, and whether the time is paid. Note the budget line it would use.
5. Set a finish-by date and the review date after it.
6. Give one of three answers: include; include with conditions (name them); or leave to work (name the work instead).
7. Send the answer to the requester on the task. A purchase needs the task to ask for it and a granted Tool to allow it; otherwise
   the course stays a request for a person.

Checks before you finish:
- The answer names the skill and the reason in one line.
- Cost, length and a finish-by date are in the answer.
- A course with no practice or feedback is marked as awareness.
- Nothing is enrolled or bought.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Proposed: a cloud certification for two platform engineers. Skill: operating the production
environment (level guide 4.1). Work alone does not teach the vendor's security model, and the customer contract names the
certification. Practice: labs and an exam, so practice is present. Cost: 40 hours each, the exam fee on the budget line. Finish by
2026-03-31, review 2026-04-15. Answer: include with conditions: a named sponsor on the task and the finish date in the plan."
