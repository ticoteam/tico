# Design a practice block

When to use: for each skill in a role plan, or for a course a team wants to add to a plan, before the plan goes on a task.

Steps:
1. Name the skill and the level expectation it serves, from the level guide (`hub doc ask`). If there is no guide, use the skill
   the requester named and say so.
2. Name one piece of real work in the next quarter that stretches the skill, by type (run a customer review, lead a small
   release, review a design). Do not assign it to a person.
3. Add practice with feedback: who gives the feedback, how often, and on what. A course with no feedback step is noted as
   awareness only.
4. Set review dates. Practice repeats in short sessions spread over weeks, not one long block. Give the first and second review
   date.
5. Name the manager's part: the next piece of work to try the skill on, and the check at the next review.
6. Add one observable sign the manager can check, in terms of the work (for example "the review of the release cites two
   decisions the engineer made alone"). No trait words.
7. Mark the block "built from work" or "needs a course" by its largest part.

Checks before you finish:
- Each block has work, feedback, two review dates, the manager's part and a sign that can be observed.
- The sign describes the work, not the person.
- No course is in the block unless the course-check skill says it earns its place.

Example (invented, for illustration): "Skill: running a customer review (level guide 3.2). Work: run two customer reviews for
accounts you do not own, by 2026-12-15. Feedback: the account owner gives written notes within two days of each. Reviews: 2026-11-15
and 2026-12-15. Manager's part: pick the second account; check the second review at the 1:1. Sign: the review's action list names
an owner and a date for each item."
