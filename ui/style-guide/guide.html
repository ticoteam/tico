Layout and rules

Rules first, each with its reason. Then the frame, the page header, the subheader, and the size every element takes, on desktop and on a phone.

Helper text Rule

  • Under the field, one muted line.It stays in view while typing; a placeholder does not.
  • Say the format or the effect. Never repeat the label.A hint that repeats the label is noise.
  • More than a line goes in the ? beside the label.The form keeps its height; detail is one hover or tap away.
  • An error takes the hint's place, in red, with the fix.One place to look.
  • No hint when the label is enough.Less is more.

One .hint, --t-xs, muted, 4px under the field. Every hint uses it: onboarding, settings, the bot editor and the setup notes.

Getting started and tips Rule

  • Setup card: stays until done or skipped.For a step the page is empty without. A needed step must survive a stray click, and stop after a clear no. Saved per person on the server; ⋯ brings it back.
  • Tip: shown once, closed with ✕.For what is good to know. Optional help that comes back is nagging. Saved on the server, so it stays closed on every device.
  • Tour: once, six steps at most, replay from the account menu.A walk is for the first day only.
  • Checklist: one, in the sidebar, until complete.Finish setup is the company's list; a page never adds its own.
  • One at a time, and the setup card wins.Two prompts on a page and neither is read.

Where are your docs?

Each place your team keeps what it knows is added to Docs, read, and mapped so anyone can find things.

The Docs card is the pattern for a setup card: one question, one filled button, a second way in, and Skip for now. Skipped is saved per person (librarian.docs_setup).

Top bars: one line Rule

  • A top bar is one line: title, tabs, tools, the filled button, then ⋯.A bar that wraps pushes the page down; on a phone that is the title and the first control.
  • The ⋯ menu is last, at the far right, on every page, in the same style on desktop and phone.One spot, so nobody hunts for it.
  • One filled button at most, at the right, just before ⋯. A page whose adding is rare has a quiet + there instead.The eye lands on the page's main action.
  • Common actions stay in the bar when there is room.What is used daily, like a layout switch or New task, is one click, not two; ⋯ takes it only when the row is too short. Everything else lives in ⋯ from the start.
  • When the row is tight, a tool's word becomes its icon, then tools move into ⋯.Nothing wraps and nothing is lost; the title and the filled button stay.
  • What is used less than daily goes in ⋯. Nothing sits in both.Two places for one action is two things to learn.

Platform rules Rule

From Apple's Human Interface Guidelines. Where the guide chooses differently, the line says so.

  • Touch targets are 44px on a phone: buttons, selects, switches.A finger needs it. Every phone control is this size.
  • Destructive is red, through the danger style. Confirm only when it cannot be undone and is not expected. Anything reversible gets Undo.A confirm on every delete teaches people to click through.
  • A dialog always has Cancel and one primary. Labels are verbs, never Yes or No. Cancel left, action right.Apple's WWDC22 example puts the destructive button on the left. The guide keeps the house order, so the action is in one place in every dialog.
  • A narrow task is a dialog or sheet. Long or multi-step on a phone is full screen. A drill-in pushes.The size says how much is being asked.
  • The phone tab bar has five items or fewer.More shrinks every target. The bar is five slots: Team, Threads, the Assistant, Tasks and More.
  • A context menu is for one item: most used first, destructive last and red. A choice list is a pop-up that shows its value. Actions are a pull-down.The control's shape says whether it sets a value or does something.
  • A segmented control has two or more segments, each a button. On or off is a switch.One segment is not a choice.
  • Few toasts. An error stays until it is dismissed or retried.A message that leaves by itself cannot be acted on.
  • Text contrast is 4.5:1. Controls and icons that carry meaning are 3:1.Fields and selects take the stronger edge.
  • Nothing clips or overlaps at 200% text size.Heights come from content, not fixed pixels, wherever text sits.

Dates and times Rule

  • Month first, 12-hour clock: Tue, Nov 11, 10:15 AM.How most people here write and read a date.
  • Today and Tomorrow replace the date: Today, 10:15 AM.The word is faster to read than the date.
  • The year shows only when it is not this year. A date field and a range header always show it.The year is noise until it differs.
  • Under a day old, say how long ago: 35m, 2h. After that, the date.Recent things are judged by age, older ones by day.
  • A day line in a chat spells the day out: Thursday, November 12.It is a heading, so it has the room.
  • No leading zeros and no 24-hour times. A running clock is minutes and seconds: 2:14.One way to write a time.

Menus: one level Rule

  • A menu never opens a menu beside it.Flyouts need a steady pointer, fail on touch, and hide where you are.
  • More choices: step in, inside the same popover, with a way back.Tasks' filter does this.
  • Views of one thing are tabs: one row, five at most.Tabs show every choice at once. No tabs inside tabs.
  • Vertical navigation has one level: the sidebar.A page never gets a side menu of its own. A list of records beside its detail is a list, not a menu.

No nested menus: no submenu, flyout or menu item that opens a menu.

Where chat lives Rule

  • Every page has a chat in a column on its right; the page keeps the left.The work is read first, the talk about it second.
  • Chat that is the work is the main column, never a rail.A bot's page, a thread and a task are conversations.
  • The chat is with the page's owner bot. Every page has an owner.The same column in the same place, whoever answers.
  • The chat column starts open.It is there without asking. Closing it is the person's choice and is remembered. The button that closes it is a quiet icon with no outline.
  • No chat button in the top bar.The column is the way in. When there is no room beside the page it folds to a tab on the right edge and opens over the page.
  • No right column on a phone or in a small or medium dialog.A phone shows one thing at a time, and opens the chat full screen; a small dialog has no room.
  • The Assistant opens in the sidebar. It takes the sidebar above the foot and never covers the page or its chat.The page's chat stays with its owner on the right; the Assistant is always on the left. Two bots, two fixed places.
  • Most work on a page is done by talking to its owner.The page shows the state; the chat changes it. A button is kept for what is done daily.

When it animates

  • Only the live dot moves. It is the Assistant row (or the phone's middle tab) while the Assistant's chat is closed, and the chat's own dot while it is open.
  • A dot off screen, or in a tab that is hidden, pauses.
  • With reduced motion the dot holds still and the closed row says the state in a word: Listening…, Working… or Replying…. An open panel says it in the thread instead.
  • Moving between places: when the Assistant's chat opens, the dot flies from the sidebar row (or the phone tab) to the chat's name, along an arc, at a third of the normal time. On close it flies back. With reduced motion, nothing flies.

Why it is built this way

  • Cheap. Only transform and opacity animate. The shape never animates, and each part is contained, so no motion reflows the page.
  • Contained. A flight runs on a copy in one fixed layer, so a move adds nothing to the page's layout.
  • Quiet. Idle motion comes from one timer, not a frame loop, and stops while the tab is hidden.
  • Accessible. Reduced motion stops everything and the state is still said in a word.
