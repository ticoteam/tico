# Test cuts: plan (2026-11-06)

Goal (Chris): "reduce low value tests, keep more end to end high quality over quantity of tests, reducing my overall
test time by at least 25% by cutting things, and then making sure they are balanced to reduce the overall build time"

This is a plan only. No tests are deleted here. Each batch below becomes its own PR.

**Summary**

- Today the gate costs 30,675 one-worker seconds: 27,595 Python plus 3,080 browser.
- Six cut batches remove 5,804 s (18.9%). They remove tests, trim parametrizations, and fold narrow tests into end-to-end
  ones. Security, privacy, data-safety, release and real-regression assertions are kept: every fold keeps them.
- Cutting alone falls ~1,865 s short of 25%. What is left is mostly distinct paths whose cost is the shared fixture.
  The fixture itself is the lever. `create_app` (S1) costs ~0.9 s on every one of ~9,500 tests, so speedup S1 alone is
  worth 6,000-8,500 s with no coverage loss. Cuts plus S1 bring test time down 40% or more.
- Gate wall goes from 502 s (8.4 min) to ~415 s (6.9 min), at which point the product/images build is the pole. The
  rebalance refreshes the timings and moves python from 28 to 22 parts, while browser stays at 4.

## 2. Cut list

Six batches. Each one is a reviewable PR, cut by area so that one reviewer knows the code. Every item was opened and
read before it was proposed. Five reviewers covered the area slices, and I did the demo builds myself. Seconds are the
gate's one-worker seconds for the cases removed. For a fold, that is (N-1) times the repeated setup. Every fold
keeps every assertion: "consolidate" items are written as `{A, B, ...} -> 1 e2e test "<name>"`, and the kept test
is named in the "Still covered by" column. Risk is the reviewer's call. **Uncertain removals are the rows marked
med, med-high or low-med**, and section 5 lists the riskiest.

### The 25% is not reached by cutting alone

Removal reaches **5,804 s, or 18.9%** of 30,675 s, which leaves **~1,865 s** short of the 7,669 s target. All five
reviewers aimed for 30% of their slice and landed at 16-28%. They hit the same floor. Each test that is left is
mostly a distinct path, and most of its ~1.2-3.3 s is fixture cost, not test logic. A further scan of every
parametrized group over 6 s that nobody proposed found ~420 s of candidates. Most of those are private-task and
security tables that the reviewers kept on purpose (`test_task_from_private`, `test_conformance` credential and
audience cases). Two ways to close the gap:

- **Batch 7 (not recommended):** fold distinct paths that share a fixture into longer story tests, 3-5 per story, in
  the ~60 files with the most `api` tests. That needs ~1,600 tests folded. The saving is pure fixture time, and every
  fold costs failure isolation.
- **Recommended:** take the same seconds from S1 (`create_app` built once per process, section 3). It removes the
  ~0.9 s that every one of those tests pays, keeps every boundary, and is one PR. Cuts (18.9%) plus S1 (20-28%) cut
  test time by 40% or more. Cuts plus S2 alone reach ~26%.

Cross-batch links. Batch 3's `test_thread_rail` migration cut relies on
`test_schema_every_table.py::test_every_table_of_an_upgraded_database_has_the_columns_of_a_new_one`, which batch 4
keeps (it folds only the other two tests in that file). The old-runner deletions in batch 4 must ship with the
legacy-runner code they guard. Batch 5's `test_e2e_mcp` sample and `test_e2e_cli` sample should not both take the
deepest cut. Take the cli sample and keep mcp whole if only one is wanted.
